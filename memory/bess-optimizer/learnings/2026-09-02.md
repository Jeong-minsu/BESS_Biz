## 2. Delta Analysis — What Worked / What Didn't

### What worked:

**ECRS HE22–23 deployment captured well**: RTSPP $81.89/MWh at HE22 triggered ECRS deployment at 50 MW; $481.50 + $405.00 = $886.50 realized. This was the highest-value AS deployment of the day. The evening wind surge (HE21 onward) created the scarcity condition.

**DA sale HE20**: $1,894.50 from 50 MWh at $37.89. At HE20, RTSPP = $53.95 (RT > DA by $16.06), meaning DA-RT spread was negative — we captured $37.89 while RT was $53.95. Missed the extra $16.06 × 50 MWh = $803 by being locked into DA. However, given no Smartbidder data, the DA sale was a defensible risk-reduction move.

### What didn't work:

**RT charging HE09–11 too expensive**: Battery loaded 99.4 MWh at RTSPP $25–29/MWh (HE09–11 cost: -$2,717.60). Yet HE01–06 RTSPP was $27–35/MWh — not materially different. Overnight spread was not meaningfully cheaper than midday. HOWEVER: charging midday (HE09–11) is structurally less efficient on Sep-type days versus overnight because:
1. Midday charging displaces potential AS revenue in peak hours
2. Heat wave overnight prices are marginally cheaper ($26–30 vs $27–34)
3. Principal lesson: shift charging earlier in overnight window

**RT Energy net negative (-$600)**: RT charging cost $2,717.60 while RT discharge earned $2,117.42 across HE17–21 (approximately). Net RT = -$600.18. If charging had been done in DA (purchase) instead of RT, charge cost at DASPP ~$22–28 vs RTSPP $25–29 would have saved ~$300–600. Pursue DA energy purchases going forward.

**Spread miss at HE20 (DA sale)**: Locked $37.89 DA while RT was $53.95. Negative DA-RT spread signal was present but ignored (no Smartbidder P(DA>RT) to validate). Action: even without Smartbidder, the market-analyst spread directional call on Sep 1 flagged HE19–20 as "slightly negative to negative" (RT > DA at cliff). Should have deferred DA sale at HE20 and held for RT discharge OR reduced DA sale quantity.

**HE15–16 underperform**: DASPP HE15 = $44.55, HE16 = $44.97. RT at HE15 = $34.29 (DA > RT, positive spread). Battery was not dispatched in energy at HE15–16 — only NonSpin 40–56 MW. Missed: ~56 MW × ($44.55 − charge cost $27) = ~$980 in additional energy arbitrage. On Sep 1, the shoulder prices were moderate ($44–48) — energy dispatch was possible but not pursued.

**Structural conclusion**: Sep 1 was an AS-heavy day executed with reasonable success, but the 50 MWh DA sale at HE20 (wrong sign) and RT charge (vs DA charge) represent systematic losses. Total estimated performance gap vs optimal: ~$1,500–2,500.
