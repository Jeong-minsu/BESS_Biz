## Key Decision Deltas

### Delta 1 — DA Charge Bid Failure (7th+ Consecutive Cycle): -$15,668 DA Energy Impact

The recommendation specified -100 MW DA charge at HE10 and -88 MW at HE11, both at bid cap ≤$35/MWh. Actual DA clearing prices were $23.12 (HE10) and $23.67 (HE11) — well below the bid cap. The bid should have cleared on price alone. It did not. This is a dispatch system / operational constraint issue, not a pricing error.

What actually charged: HE04 (-30 MWh DA at $24.30) and HE05 (-49.4 MWh RT unscheduled). These suggest the BESS system has an alternate charge schedule loaded. Combined charge from these two hours: ~67.5 MWh effective SoC, vs 188 MWh target.

Downstream consequence: SoC insufficient for full HE19 discharge (50 MW delivered vs 100 MW recommended). HE20 discharge impossible (0 SoC remaining after HE19 imbalance). Estimated cascade loss: -$15,668 (DA energy delta alone) + partial RT imbalance.

### Delta 2 — HE19-20 Price Cliff Catastrophic Forecast Error: -$20,618 combined

The recommendation was built on a heat wave Day 13 solar cliff model projecting HE19 at $95-160/MWh and HE20 at $120-250/MWh. Actuals:
- HE19: $42.22/MWh (midpoint miss: -$82.78/MWh, or -66%)
- HE20: $29.31/MWh (midpoint miss: -$155.69/MWh, or -84%)

The expected solar collapse to net load of 64,215 MW at HE20 did not produce the price spike. Late heat-wave days (Day 10+) show suppressed peak prices — demand response programs are fully enrolled, ERCOT may have issued emergency reserve alerts earlier in the week, and thermal unit re-dispatch is more effective by day 13. The cliff model calibrated on days 1-5 of a heat wave significantly overstates prices in late-wave conditions.

Revenue impact: HE19 DA ($125 expected vs $42 actual): -$4,150 (on 50 MW delivered). HE20 DA (0 MW delivered vs 50 MW at $185 target): -$9,250. Combined: -$13,400 attributable to price forecast error alone (separate from the SoC/charge failure delta).

### Delta 3 — AS+Energy Overlap at HE19 (Third Consecutive Cycle): -$2,227 RT Imbalance

Actual HE19: 50 MW DA discharge committed + 85 MW NonSpin offered simultaneously. This exceeds physical capacity (100 MW). The NonSpin was called, BESS could not simultaneously serve both schedules, and 50.86 MWh was bought back in RT at $43.79/MWh = -$2,227.

This is the same root cause as Aug 31 (-$25,691 RT loss) and has now recurred in at least three cycles. The recommendation explicitly prohibited AS in energy hours. This is a BESS operations / bid submission system issue, not an optimization decision. Requires Tenaska dispatch system audit.

### Delta 4 — HE21 RT Option and ECRS Windfall (Positive Deviation): +$3,508 incremental

RT spiked to $106.63/MWh at HE21 (driven by wind ramp delay or scarcity). BESS deployed 27.33 MWh RT discharge at $2,914.57 and cleared 50 MW ECRS at HE21-24 ($593.50 total). This was the sole outperformance vs recommendation ($3,003 RT + $450 ECRS = $3,453 recommended vs $3,509 actual = +$56 overperformance on RT/ECRS combined). The RT option trigger (>$80) worked exactly as designed.

### Delta 5 — NonSpin Severe Underperformance: -$5,523

Recommended: 80 MW NS HE01-09, 100 MW NS HE12-18 = 1,620 MW-h total capacity.
Actual: 15 MW at HE01-02, 8.6 MW at HE08, 85 MW at HE13-18, 40 MW at HE20 = roughly 590 MW-h (effective, excluding the HE19 overlap).
Revenue: $857 actual vs $6,380 recommended = -$5,523.

Without Smartbidder clearing price data, NS bids are placed blind. The near-zero clearing in HE01-09 (0-15 MW vs 80 MW target) suggests NS bids were too high for the overnight window where wind was abundant and system stress was low. The 85 MW clearing in HE13-18 confirms the afternoon window was priced correctly. Structural fix requires Smartbidder restoration.
