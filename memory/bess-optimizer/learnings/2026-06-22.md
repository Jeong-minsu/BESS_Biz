## 4. Key Structural Learnings from 2026-06-21 Benchmark

### Learning A: Benchmark Holds SoC for AS at HE20-21 When NS Is Very High

When DA NS prices at HE20-21 exceed $5/MWh, Smartbidder allocates to NS rather than energy dispatch. This produces:
- HE20 DA NS ~$608 (100 MW × ~$6.08/MWh) + no energy
- HE21 DA NS ~$505 (100 MW × ~$5.05/MWh) + no energy
- But RT Energy negative at HE19-20 (charging cost) — system was adding SoC in anticipation

Implication for 6/23: DA NS at HE21 = $14.20/MWh (from market briefing). This is 2.8x higher than 6/21 HE21 NS. If benchmark logic holds, the optimal allocation may be MORE NS at HE21, not energy-first.

**6/23 counter-argument**: HE21 DA energy = $36.44/MWh. Per MW: energy $36.44 >> NS $14.20. Even at 80 MW energy cap (SoC-constrained), 80×$36.44 = $2,915 >>> 100×$14.20 = $1,420. Full energy is the dominant choice when energy price >> NS price. The benchmark may hold SoC for NS only when energy prices are not compelling (as on 6/21 where HE20-21 DA energy prices were presumably lower).

### Learning B: RT Energy Charging Cost Embedded in Benchmark

The benchmark RT negative energy at HE10-11 ($-185, $-106) confirms RT charging at the duck curve trough. On 6/22-6/23, DA charge at HE09-11 may be the better structure (locks in $15-16/MWh cost) vs RT floating charge (uncertain price in the 5-min market). My recommendation uses DA charge — this is correct.

### Learning C: ECRS Cleared at HE15, HE17 on 2026-06-21

Benchmark ECRS DA was +$5.45 at HE15 and +$23.08 at HE17. This contradicts the 3-cycle non-clearing pattern observed in 6/15-6/20 actuals. Possible explanation: 6/21 was a Sunday (shoulder demand, different AS dynamics). Weekday 6/23 ECRS clearing may be different.
- Implication: ECRS may clear on some days at unexpected hours (HE15-17 rather than HE20-24). Limited operational value since we are not offering ECRS at those hours given SoC = 200 MWh and energy not dispatching. Consider adding ECRS at HE15-18 if DA ECRS price > $2/MWh becomes available in forecast.
