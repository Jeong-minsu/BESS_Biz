## 2. Oct 17 Strategy Design Decisions — Forward Justification

### 2.1 DA vs RT Venue Call: DA Lock-in at HE19-20

The most important strategic departure from Oct 16: Oct 17 uses DA lock-in for discharge (HE19-20) while Oct 16 used RT retention. The signal inversion is clear and structural:

| Day | DA-RT spread (HE19) | P_DA_gt_RT | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 16 | -$14.7 (RT >> DA) | ~30% | RT discharge |
| Oct 17 | +$21.51 (DA >> RT) | 69.9% | DA lock-in |

The Oct 17 DA premium (+$21.51) is driven by solar collapse coinciding exactly with wind trough — the DA market has pre-priced the scarcity event. Oct 16's extreme duck (NL -345 MW) suppressed DA mid-day and created a steeper RT-favored afternoon. Oct 17's more moderate duck (NL ~2,565 MW) with similar evening ramp produces the classic "DA locks in the spike" pattern.

### 2.2 REGDN Trade-off — Explicit Quantification First Time

Previous strategies have not explicitly quantified the REGDN trade-off. Today's strategy introduces this calculation:

- Gain from REGDN (62 MW for HE17-18 with 62 MWh headroom): 62 × ($4.65 + $6.79) = $709
- Extra charge cost to enable REGDN (delay charge from HE1-2 at $3.53 avg to HE17-18 at $41.93 avg): 67 MWh × ($41.93 - $3.53) = $2,573
- Net: Early charging saves $1,864 net vs REGDN capture strategy.

This calculation should be standard in all future strategies when REGDN is a significant AS signal (> $3/MWh).

### 2.3 HE21 Idle Decision — Formalized Improvement

The Oct 16 strategy charged at HE21 (-100 MW at $50.60) because SoC was 0 after discharge and the strategy was structured around fast SoC rebuild. The Oct 17 strategy explicitly idles HE21 and starts recharging at HE22 ($41.06) — saving $960 in charge cost with identical terminal SoC.

**Formalized rule**: After post-discharge SoC depletion, if the first available recharge hour (next HE after discharge) has DA price > $45/MWh, idle that hour and start recharging one hour later. Break-even threshold: hourly idle cost = NSPIN revenue lost (approximately 0 if SoC ≈ 0, since discharge-side AS unavailable). So any hour with DA > $45/MWh immediately post-discharge should be idled.

### 2.4 AS Playbook Absence — Fallback Protocol

This is the first bess-optimizer filing without any AS playbook available. The fallback used:
1. Use Tyba DA AS prices directly to determine the best single product per hour
2. For most hours: NSPIN DA (highest single product in most hours)
3. For HE12-17 (mid-day solar trough): REGUP DA marginally better than NSPIN DA ($0.732-0.756 vs $0.406-0.669)
4. Post-peak (HE23-24): NSPIN DA (highest at $3.23-4.76/MWh)
5. REGDN excluded entirely due to SoC constraints

This fallback should be documented formally in the playbook generation script as a fallback mode: "If AS playbook unavailable, use Tyba DA AS prices directly with NSPIN as default and REGUP where REGUP > NSPIN."

Formal rule: "Post-discharge recharge: idle the first hour after full depletion if DA price > $45/MWh AND SoC = 0. This hour cannot provide discharge-side AS, so opportunity cost is zero. Begin recharge at the next hour."

### 5.3 Action (P1, NEW) — REGDN Trade-off Standard Calculation

First explicit REGDN quantification made today. Should be a standard calculation in every strategy when REGDN DA price > $3/MWh:
1. Calculate REGDN headroom available at peak REGDN hours (typically HE17-18): headroom = 200 - SoC_at_peak
2. REGDN revenue = headroom_MW × REGDN_price
3. Extra charge cost = delayed_charge_MWh × (peak_charge_price - overnight_charge_price)
4. Compare: if REGDN revenue > extra charge cost → consider delayed charging strategy; else → charge early.
