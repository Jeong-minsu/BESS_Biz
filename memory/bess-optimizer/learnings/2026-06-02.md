### 3c. HE20 solar divergence fork — which source was right?

At the time of the June 2 recommendation, Yes Energy showed HE20 solar at 4,764 MW (NL ~54,894 MW) and Enverus showed solar at ~541 MW (NL ~59,117 MW). The June 3 recommendation (generated the same morning) shows Yes Energy HE20 solar for June 3 = 4,555 MW and Enverus HE20 solar for June 3 = 6,115 MW — the YE-Enverus divergence pattern persisted into the next day. Smartbidder, when it became available on June 3, priced HE20 at $54.69 and HE21 at $48.39 — confirming that the tighter (Enverus-leaning) scenario partially materialized: HE20 exceeded HE21 in price, which is consistent with an earlier-tightening solar exit.

**Inference**: The Enverus solar-exit signal for HE20 was likely more accurate than Yes Energy on June 2 (as it was on June 3). The 70 MW HE20 discharge hedge (vs 100 MW if Enverus was fully confirmed) was conservative but not wrong. However, concentrating all remaining 100 MW at HE21 (rather than shifting some to HE20) may have under-captured HE20 value if that hour cleared above $47/MWh. This is a systematic underweight of HE20 that the June 3 plan corrected (increasing HE20 from 70 to 100 MW when Smartbidder confirmed $54.69 at HE20).

**Lesson**: When Enverus shows near-zero solar at HE20 and YE shows ~4,500 MW, the Enverus signal for price tightening at HE20 has been the more accurate indicator in this series (June 2, June 3 both consistent). Weight Enverus solar-exit timing over YE when structuring the HE20 MW allocation.
