## 4. Key Lessons

### 4a. ECRS October Trigger (New Validation)

The explicit-trigger framework for October ECRS (not mandate) worked correctly for both Oct 6 and Oct 8:

- Oct 6 (DEGRADED): Wind-variability trigger (GR_WEST intraday, WEST_TO_NORTH P10-20%) → ECRS included at 20-50 MW. Reasonable.
- Oct 8 (LIVE data): SEVERE duck (NL 25,380 MW) + 20 GW outage → ECRS at 20 MW HE01-10 and 20 MW HE12-18. Clear-cut.

**Revised trigger criteria for October (update to Oct 5 learning)**:
- SEVERE duck (NL trough < 28,000 MW): Always include ECRS 15-25 MW in duck window.
- MODERATE duck (NL trough 28,000-36,000 MW): Include ECRS 10-20 MW if wind variability signal present (GR_WEST overnight SCI > 0.20 or WEST_TO_NORTH P > 10%).
- MILD duck (NL trough > 36,000 MW): Exclude ECRS unless explicit ERCOT scarcity signal.

### 4b. October Duck Curve Depth — Revised Assessment

The Oct 5 seasonal learning table categorized October as structurally MODERATE (NL trough 28,000-36,000 MW). Oct 8 confirms this is an average, not a bound:

- Oct 8 actual NL trough: 25,380 MW (SEVERE). Solar record (30,472 MW) + near-zero wind + 20 GW outage.
- The combination of three adverse factors (October solar near-record, wind drought, autumn maintenance outage peak) can produce September-level or worse duck severity.
- **Revised October duck model**: Use a bimodal distribution. Base case MODERATE (NL 28-36 GW, probability ~65%). Tail case SEVERE (NL 22-28 GW, probability ~25%) when solar forecast > 28 GW AND wind < 3 GW midday. Tail case MILD (NL > 36 GW, probability ~10%) when cloudy/overcast.
- For charge strategy: When Yes Energy is LIVE and NL trough < 28,000 MW confirmed, always shift charge to duck trough (HE10-12 typically) instead of overnight. The duck charge advantage is $12-15/MWh ($17 overnight vs $3-5 midday) — worth at least $1,200-1,500 on 100 MW hour.

### 4c. SoC Assumption Risk — Compounding Degradation

Day 19+ without Tenaska creates compounding SoC uncertainty. Each day, the terminal SoC target from the prior recommendation is a good estimate, but:
- If the prior day's recommendation was not filed (Oct 7 gap), there is no terminal SoC anchor at all.
- The Rule 7 default (100 MWh = 50%) is conservative and safe: it keeps at least 100 MWh capacity available for both charging and AS provision. But it may significantly misstate actual conditions.
- **Risk scenario**: If actual SoC = 0 MWh (prior day full discharge, no recharge), the Oct 8 HE11-12 charge to 200 MWh still works (0 → 92.2 → 200 MWh via HE11 + top-up), but Rule 7 contingency instructions must be clearly written. They were for Oct 8 — this practice should become standard.
- **Risk scenario (opposite)**: If actual SoC = 200 MWh (prior day AS-only, no discharge), the Oct 8 HE11 charge bid would overfill without the contingency adjustment. The ≤$15 ceiling on the DA bid still applies but SCED would need to see reduced capacity offer.
- **Action**: Until Tenaska is restored, every recommendation must include explicit Rule 7 contingency instructions covering both SoC > 150 and SoC < 50 cases.

This is the second gap event in 7 days (Oct 1-5 = 5 days; Oct 7 = 1 day). Root cause is unclear but the pattern is consistent: when Smartbidder is EXPIRED and Tenaska is UNAVAILABLE, the orchestration cycle either does not initiate or does not complete.

An Oct 7 equivalent should have been filed without any live data.
