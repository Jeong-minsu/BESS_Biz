## 4. Root Cause Analysis

**Cause A (Execution disconnect — high probability):**
The recommended HE09-11 charge bids (ceilings $25-28/MWh) and HE19-20 discharge offers (floors $80-90/MWh) do not appear to have been submitted or cleared. The overnight positions (HE02-06) and HE21 position are inconsistent with the Stack C schedule and appear to be Smartbidder autonomous actions. The "UNRESOLVED RT dispatch risk" flagged in the recommendation (involuntary dispatch at non-recommended hours) likely covers this pattern. Without Tenaska Submissions endpoint data, the exact origin cannot be confirmed.

**Cause B (DA-RT spread reversal in charge hours — confirmed by data):**
All five DA charge positions had DASPP > RTSPP (positive spread). The recommendation correctly designated overnight hours as idle (no DA energy bid) precisely because the DA-RT hybrid signal favored RT > DA in the evening and overnight DA prices are not reliably below RT. The executed positions paid the DA premium in each charging hour.

**Cause C (HE21 charge at $64.06/MWh — critical failure):**
HE21 is a post-peak demand hour; the recommendation explicitly allocated it to "idle — SoC depleted" (expected SoC = 0 MWh after full HE19-20 discharge). Either the HE19-20 discharge did not clear (leaving residual SoC) and Smartbidder autonomously bid a charge position at peak-hour DA prices, or this was an erroneous Smartbidder bid submission. Either scenario is damaging: HE21 DASPP $64.06 > RTSPP $53.74 produced a −$309.75 DART virtual loss on 30 MW.

**Cause D (HSL constraint — structural):**
Telemetered HSL averaged only 48.6 MW (range 42.8–56.6 MW) against a rated 100 MW. Even if the HE09-11 charge or HE19-20 discharge had cleared in DA, physical delivery would have been capped below the submitted MW. The recommendation assumed 100 MW availability; actual availability was ~49% of that. This gap was flagged as "confirm HSL daily" in standing rules but the degraded Tenaska data feed made real-time verification impossible.
