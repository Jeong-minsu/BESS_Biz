### Root Cause Decomposition

**PROBLEM 1: Severe RT imbalance HE19-21 (−$8,789 RT)**

The battery committed 50 MWh DA at HE19 but physically injected only 27.87 MWh in RT. Shortfall = 22.13 MWh bought back at $103/MWh = ~−$2,284 RT imbalance. At HE20 (committed 30 MWh DA, injected 24.79 MWh RT, RT at $163/MWh) and HE21 (committed 30 MWh DA, injected 11.93 MWh RT, RT at $225/MWh), the RT imbalance was even larger per MWh.

Root cause: Battery entered peak discharge window with INSUFFICIENT SoC. Daily total charging = 89.04 MWh via small RT increments overnight (HE01-07: 0.9-48.7 MWh each, at expensive RT prices $39-67/MWh). Battery was not fully charged before the most valuable hours.

**PROBLEM 2: DA commitment too small**

Even setting aside imbalance, 118 MWh total DA sales (avg ~$61/MWh) against a 200 MWh battery = only 59% of capacity utilized. The optimal move was to match DA bid precisely to physically deliverable MWh to eliminate imbalance.

**PROBLEM 3: Overnight charging at high RT cost**

Overnight RT prices HE01-07: $41-67/MWh (DA was only $31-40). Spread = DA − RT was negative (−$7 to −$28), meaning RT > DA. Charging in RT overnight means paying the RT premium. This created a negative foundation for the day's P&L before the main cycle even ran.

**PROBLEM 4: Under-utilization of BESS (0.46 cycles)**

Sep 16 used only 0.46 equivalent cycles. A properly optimized day should target 1.0 cycle (200 MWh charge → 170 MWh discharge) to maximize revenue per day.

## 5. Improvement Actions for Sep 18 (and Forward)

**Action 1 (CRITICAL): Dedicated DA charge block at midday solar trough**

**Action 2 (CRITICAL): Match DA discharge bid to physically deliverable MWh**

Never under-commit DA at peak discharge hours. The Sep 16 shortfall (physical < DA commitment) was caused by insufficient SoC entering the peak window. With a full midday charge (Action 1), the battery will have ~170 MWh deliverable. Set DA bid = 100 MW HE19 + 70 MW HE20 = 170 MWh, exactly matching deliverable capacity. This eliminates RT imbalance risk at peak hours.

Sep 16 used NonSpin HE01-20 at $23-280/h. Sep 16 ECRS at HE22-24 was the highest per-MWh AS product ($760/$600/$389). Going forward: commit ECRS (50 MW) during the full-SoC idle window HE12-18 instead of NonSpin (or in addition). Expected incremental AS revenue: $3,000-$5,000 vs Sep 16 NonSpin approach.

**Action 4: Avoid overnight RT charging**

Sep 16 overnight RT ($39-67/MWh) was 40-70% more expensive than midday DA charge. Going forward, start each day with SoC ~0 and charge exclusively via DA midday. Exception: if next-morning market shows strong evidence of negative overnight DA prices (storm event, extreme wind flood).
