**Learning 1 — October structure is more extreme than estimated from Sep 30 data + seasonality adjustments**: Both solar AND thermal outage increased beyond the seasonal expectation. Solar at 30 GW in October is near-summer levels; 20 GW outage is nearly double the prior estimate. The combined effect (deeper duck + higher evening ramp) creates conditions more like late summer than early autumn.

**Learning 2 — Optimal charge window shifts dramatically by duck tier**: 
- MODERATE duck (estimated Oct 6): Charge overnight at $17/MWh (HE01-02) was optimal (shallow midday minimum)
- SEVERE duck (confirmed Oct 8): Charge HE11 midday at $3/MWh is optimal. Switching from overnight to midday charge saves ~$1,400 per 100 MWh cycle (${17 - $3} × 100). 
- **Rule**: When live Yes Energy confirms SEVERE duck (NL trough < 26,000 MW), shift primary charge to NL trough hour. Do NOT default to overnight charge under SEVERE conditions.

**Learning 3 — Starting SoC management vs charging window timing**:
With 100 MWh starting SoC and a SEVERE duck, the optimal charging allocation is:
- Skip overnight charge (hold AS all night)
- Wait for absolute NL trough (HE11 today: $3/MWh)
- One full charge hour (-100 MW) fills ~92 MWh of headroom
- Small top-up needed (9 MW × 1h at HE12) to reach full 200 MWh
- Total charge cost: ~$342 (vs $1,870 overnight charge on Oct 6)
- Revenue impact: +$1,528 in lower charge costs alone

**Learning 4 — Morning discharge cycle (two-cycle strategy) economics**: Analyzed whether to add HE06 early discharge (-92 MW) to create headroom for 2 full charge hours at midday. Net result: +$297 energy gain (post-Rule-2) but -$690 AS opportunity loss from HE07-10 at 0 SoC. Net: -$393. **DO NOT add morning discharge cycle on SEVERE duck days with 50% starting SoC** — the AS revenue from HE07-10 with SoC = 100 MWh exceeds the energy gain from the early cycle.
