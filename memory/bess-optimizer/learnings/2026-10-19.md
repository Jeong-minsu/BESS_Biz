## 6. Three Actionable Insights (Oct 19 Generation Cycle — Filed 2026-10-20)

### Insight 1: Split the HE18 RT/DA Position When P(RT>DA) < 57%

On Oct 20, the HE18 position was 100% RT at P(RT>DA) = 54% — near coin flip. The variance on this single 100 MW hour dominated total day revenue uncertainty ($879 swing between RT and DA outcomes). A more risk-efficient approach: when P(RT>DA) is 52-57% (coin-flip zone), split 50 MW RT + 50 MW DA. Expected value impact: approximately -$50 to -$100 vs full RT position at P50, but standard deviation of the HE18 revenue is halved. Apply this rule on any future day where the highest-value discharge hour has P(venue>other) between 52% and 57%.

**Implementation**: Add to standing rules — "Coin-flip dispatch rule: when P(primary venue) 52-57% AND single hour represents >50% of daily energy revenue, execute 50/50 split rather than 100% primary venue."

### Insight 2: Establish a Minimum 100 MWh SoC Carry-In Protocol

The Oct 18 → Oct 19 → Oct 20 three-day zero-SoC chain cost an estimated $5,000-$8,000 in forfeited overnight NSPIN revenue across two cycles, while Oct 21's 30 MWh carry-in generated $1,587 overnight AS. The Oct 19 generation cycle explicitly noted the compounding cost but did not formalize a carry-in minimum. Now that the cost is quantified across three consecutive cycles: establish a hard protocol that terminal SoC should be at minimum 85 MWh (not 0) whenever overnight HE23-24 DA prices are below $35/MWh. On Oct 20, HE23 DA was $35.63 and HE24 $31.90 — borderline but below threshold on HE24. A 50 MW × 2h recharge at $33.77 average cost (100 MWh drawn, 85 MWh stored) would cost $3,377 for 85 MWh SoC carry-in, enabling ~$1,500-$3,000 overnight NSPIN on Oct 21. This was break-even to positive; the failure to include it was a protocol gap.

**Implementation**: Standing rule addition — "Minimum SoC carry-in: always recharge to 85 MWh SoC via HE23-24 if (a) HE23 or HE24 DA price < $35/MWh AND (b) next-day overnight NSPIN forecast > $2.50/MW average. Override only if next-day specific analysis shows energy arbitrage spread > $40/MWh is available."

### Insight 3: Formalize the Oct 20 Regime Trigger as a Standing Rule

The Oct 20 generation cycle correctly identified the RT>DA regime (weekday + high outage + GR_WEST wind trough) and overrode the playbook to use RT dispatch at HE18. This was done by judgment, not a standing rule. The evidence now shows this regime is:
- Reproducible: driven by GR_WEST HE15-16 < 5,500 MW + load > 58,000 MW + outage > 25 GW
- Transient: completely reversed on Oct 21 (DA>>RT with P(DA>RT) = 94.9%)
- Profitable to correctly identify: HE18 RT>DA premium was $8.79/MWh × 100 MW = $879 capture vs locking in the lower DA price

**Implementation**: Add to standing rules — "Evening venue trigger: when GR_WEST STWPF at HE15-16 < 5,500 MW AND ERCOT load > 58,000 MW at HE17-18 AND outage average > 25 GW: set HE17-18 dispatch venue = RT (not DA). When GR_WEST HE15-16 > 6,500 MW OR outage < 20 GW: set venue = DA. In between (5,500-6,500 MW, 20-25 GW outage): apply coin-flip split rule from Insight 1."
