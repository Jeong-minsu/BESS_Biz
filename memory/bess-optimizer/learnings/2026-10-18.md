## 1. Oct 17 Strategy Self-Review (retroactive)

### 1.1 Oct 17 Key Decisions

The Oct 17 strategy (history: `memory/bess-optimizer/history/2026-10-17.md`) made three major calls:

**Call 1 — Charge overnight at cheap $3-4/MWh DA (HE01-02) then NSPIN all day**
- Rationale: Oct 17 had extremely cheap overnight DA ($3.24-3.82) — likely driven by high overnight wind (GR_WEST overnight 13,757-15,250 MW per Yes Energy). This was the cheapest charge window in weeks.
- Assessment: Correct in structure. The overnight charge captured energy at ~$3.50/MWh before SoC fill (200 MWh), then the NSPIN hold through the day ($2,913 AS revenue) was appropriate given full SoC.
- Gap (no validation available): Actual DA clearing at HE01-02 unknown (Tenaska down). The Oct 17 forecasted prices were AG2/Tyba-based; Saturday overnight behavior can be erratic.

**Call 2 — DA discharge HE19-20 ($82-74) with P_DA_gt_RT 69.9-68.0%**
- Rationale: Strong DA > RT signal on Saturday, confirmed pre-solar-collapse NL structure.
- Assessment: Likely correct. The DA-RT spread was +$21.51 at HE19 (DA expensive). P(DA>RT) near 70% is a high-confidence DA lock-in signal.
- Missed upside flag: If actual HE21-22 wind underperformed forecast (GR_SOUTH was near-zero HE15-17 per today's market briefing), the RT might have spiked briefly. But given the early post-discharge recharge starting HE22, we exited cleanly before late wind complications.

**Call 3 — Post-peak recharge HE22-23 ($41/$36 DA), terminal SoC 138.7 MWh**
- Rationale: Pre-positioning for D+2 (Oct 18) Sunday duck curve cycle.
- Assessment: Two-day combined value: recharge cost −$5,912 (Oct 17) produces Oct 18 benefits:
  - NSPIN HE01-11 at 100 MW = +$3,763 (from 138.7 MWh SoC collateral)
  - Reduced duck charge cost: 66.5 MW instead of 200 MW = saves $2,406
  - Net two-day value of recharge decision: −$5,912 + $3,763 + $2,406 = +$257 (marginal positive)
- The recharge decision was approximately break-even on a two-day basis. Marginally correct, not a clear win. The break-even point analysis suggests that if tomorrow's overnight NSPIN prices were below ~$2.50/MW average, the recharge would have been a losing trade. Oct 18 NSPIN HE01-11 averages $3.42/MW — above the break-even threshold.

## 2. Oct 18 Strategy Forward-Looking Notes

### 2.1 Key Decision: Do NOT Discharge Overnight Despite $35-45 DA Prices

This is the most important structural call for Oct 18. Oct 17 had $3-4 overnight DA, making overnight charging obviously optimal. Oct 18 has $35-45 overnight DA — much higher. The question is whether to discharge at HE01 ($45.30 DA) before duck curve charging.

Decision: NO. NSPIN income from 138.7 MWh SoC ($3,763 HE01-11) > net overnight arbitrage income ($1,943 = $3,851 discharge revenue − $1,908 recharge cost at HE11). Margin: $1,820 in favor of holding for NSPIN.

**Rule to record**: When starting SoC > 100 MWh AND overnight NSPIN prices average > $3.00/MW AND overnight DA energy prices < $50/MWh, NSPIN-hold is preferred over energy arbitrage for the overnight period. This day (NSPIN avg HE01-10 = $3.48/MW) comfortably clears the threshold.

### 2.2 AS Playbook Override at HE08-09 (ECRS/RRS → NSPIN)

The playbook's HE08 ECRS_DA ($0.86/MW) and HE09 RRS_DA ($0.63/MW) are both dominated by NSPIN_DA ($2.75, $2.62/MW). The sparse cohorts (15, 22 training hours) for these morning solar-ramp hours are not robust enough to justify lower-priced AS products when NSPIN is clearly superior by 3-4×.

**Rule to record**: At any hour where Tyba NSPIN_DA price > 2× playbook-recommended AS price AND cohort training hours < 30, override to NSPIN_DA. The playbook's solar-ramp transition recommendations (ECRS at HE08, RRS at HE09) may have been trained during a period when NSPIN clearing was lower. Current 25 GW outage environment likely elevates NSPIN relative to the Jan-May 2026 training period.

### 2.3 HE19-20 Playbook ECRS_RT Divergence — Formal Rule

The playbook's ECRS_RT at HE19-20 (Evening|Q4|Q3 cohort, 16 training hours) is a known limitation: the fine model has insufficient high-NL evening hours from the Jan-May 2026 training window (Q4/Q5 NL in October only). This cohort falls through to a lower-information regime.

**Formalized rule (from Oct 14 learnings §6.3, restated)**: When HE NL > 35,000 MW AND DA energy price estimate > $55/MWh AND SoC > 100 MWh available for dispatch — allocate 100% of SoC to energy dispatch. No AS product override needed. This applies unambiguously to HE19-20 on Oct 18.

### 2.4 HE21-24 Structural Gap (ongoing)

The Oct 18 strategy, like Oct 17, leaves HE21-24 stranded (SoC=0, no AS). The NSPIN at HE21-22 ($8.93-9.09/MW) represents approximately $1,000-1,800/day in missed revenue. No operational fix within current asset constraints (100MW/200MWh). The only path to capture HE21-24 NSPIN is:
- 3-hour battery (300 MWh): would allow discharge spread over HE19-21, then NSPIN HE22-24
- Partial dispatch at HE20: sacrifices too much energy revenue (analyzed: −$1,882 net)

For Raven BESS (100MW/200MWh, '26 late): same constraint applies. No improvement until asset upgrade.

**Flag for operator/capacity planning**: If NSPIN prices at HE21-22 continue to average $8+/MW in October, a battery duration upgrade to 3-4 hours would add ~$600-1,800/day in AS revenue. At 250 operational days/year, that is $150k-450k/year in additional margin — may justify capex evaluation.
