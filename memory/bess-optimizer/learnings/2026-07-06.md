## 4. Root Cause Analysis

### 4a. HE21 Price Miss ($58.64 forecast vs $41.74 actual) — Primary Miss

The recommendation was anchored to the market-analyst's HE21 forecast of $58.64/MWh, flagged as an "extreme outlier $24 above adjacent hours" driven by solar extinction + post-holiday demand recovery. Actual clearing was $41.74 — still a premium, but 28.8% below forecast.

**Root cause**: Market-analyst price views for scarcity events systematically overshoot in the upper tail. The solar cliff event is structurally predictable but the magnitude of the clearing premium is highly uncertain. A post-holiday Monday typically sees less demand recovery than a typical Monday (offices/commercial load ramps more slowly after a 4-day holiday weekend).

**Corrective rule**: For post-holiday day-ahead price spikes forecast above $50/MWh, apply a 20% downward correction to the primary revenue estimate. Treat values above $50 as upside scenario, not base case. The 0.80x DA Energy haircut in Standing Rule 2 was insufficient — the issue was forecast price not a nodal haircut.

### 4b. NS Revenue Miss ($4,950 forecast vs $2,262 actual) — Large Structural Miss

Three compounding errors:
1. **MW assumption wrong**: Forecast assumed 80 MW; actual was 70 MW (-12.5%)
2. **Hour count wrong**: Forecast assumed 24h flat; actual was 19h (-20.8%) — Smartbidder switches to RRS in HE20-24 when physical dispatch begins
3. **Evening clearing price wrong**: Forecast expected $9-13/MWh in HE20-23 (from the market-analyst scarcity view); actual NS was $0 in those hours (RRS replaced NS). Daytime/overnight NS clearing was only $1.70/MWh average (at GKS node, actual).

The NS structure model was wrong in all three dimensions. The "80 MW × 24h flat" assumption treated Smartbidder's AS behavior as unconditional. In practice, Smartbidder optimizes NS vs RRS dynamically, switching to RRS during discharge-active windows.

### 4c. Execution Structure: Overnight Sell + Afternoon Buy (vs Recommended Charge HE09-10, Sell HE21-22)

This is the most significant structural finding. The actual Smartbidder DA strategy was:
- **DA sell in HE01-07 (overnight)**: capture positive DA-RT spread of +$5-11/MWh
  - Overnight DA avg $34.71/MWh vs RT avg ~$29.67/MWh = +$5.04/MWh × 350 MWh ≈ +$1,764 net
- **DA buy in HE15-22 (afternoon)**: create long position ahead of RT physical dispatch
  - DA buy avg $32.80/MWh; physical RT discharge in HE21-22 at RT prices $38.94/$43.99
  - HE22: RT ($43.99) > DA ($37.83) = positive RT>DA spread, made buy position + RT gen highly profitable
- **Physical RT discharge in HE21-22**: actual generation ~47 MW in each hour

This is the third consecutive cycle (7/4, 7/5, 7/6) where Smartbidder has used the overnight-sell/afternoon-buy structure. The recommended solar-cliff DA arbitrage (buy in midday, sell peak DA) has NOT been Smartbidder's execution pattern on any of these days.

**Root cause**: The bess-optimizer's energy strategy targets DA hub-level prices directly, but Smartbidder operates a more complex structure involving: (1) DA-RT spread arbitrage on overnight hours, (2) strategic DA position creation as hedge for RT dispatch, (3) physical RT dispatch in the highest-value RT window. This is fundamentally different from simple DA energy buy/sell arbitrage.

### 4d. RT > DA in HE22-24 (Structural Spike Not in Recommendation)

Spread convention (DA − RT):
- HE21: $41.74 - $38.94 = +$2.80 (DA marginally expensive — DA sell would have been fine)
- HE22: $37.83 - $43.99 = **-$6.16 (RT expensive — holding for RT capture was superior)**

The recommendation locked in DA for HE22 at $35.73 effective. Actual RT in HE22 was $43.99 — a $6.16 advantage foregone. This pattern of late-evening RT spikes (HE22-24 > DA) is consistent with the 7/1, 7/2 HE17 RT spike observations and indicates a structural summer evening RT scarcity in the CT 9-11 PM window (HE22-24), not just HE17.

### 4e. ECRS Non-Clearing (Forecast $300 vs $0 Actual)
