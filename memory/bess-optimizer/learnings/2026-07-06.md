## 3. Analysis: Why Did Actual Outperform Recommendation?

### 3a. NS Revenue Gap ($5,704 actual vs ~$1,200 recommended)

The recommendation targeted 100 MW NS for HE11-22 (12 hours). Actual data shows 80 MW flat 24 hours — twice the hour-count at a slightly lower MW. The NS clearing on 7/5 was stronger-than-forecast (evening HE21-24 averaged ~$3.00-4.00/MWh clearing), resulting in $5,704 total vs an estimated $1,200-1,643 recommendation figure.

**Root cause of forecast gap**: The 7/5 recommendation appears to have underestimated NS forecast prices or underallocated NS hours. This is a calibration issue — on weekend/holiday-adjacent days with high evening scarcity (solar cliff still active), NS clearing prices can be 2-3x weekday trough levels.

**Corrective action**: For holiday-adjacent Mondays and summer weekend days, apply a 1.25x NS forecast multiplier to account for tighter reserves and demand recovery.

### 3b. RT Energy Net Higher Than DA Energy Net ($7,665 vs $4,386)

Smartbidder charged overnight (HE01-06) at an effective average price of approximately $9-10/MWh (implied from ~$9,531 total charge cost over 6 hours at 100 MW), then discharged RT in the afternoon (HE13-22) at an effective average of ~$17,220 / 170 MWh ≈ $101/MWh gross dispatch price.

The recommended strategy (charge HE09-10 at ~$22/MWh GKS effective, discharge DA HE21-22 at 0.80x of $56.15 and $41.53) would have yielded:
- Gross discharge: $5,615 + $2,367 × 70/57 = ~$7,500 gross (at recommended 70 MW HE22)
- Less 0.80x haircut: ~$6,000
- Less charging cost: $2 × 100 × $19.40 + ... = $3,880 + $1,600 = $5,480 (approx)
- Net: ~$520

Wait — re-examining. The 7/5 recommendation expected $4,386 DA Energy net. Actual RT net was $7,665. Smartbidder outperformed the recommended strategy by $3,279 on energy alone, despite operating in RT mode (generally lower certainty).

**Why Smartbidder's overnight-to-afternoon spread was better on 7/5**:
- Overnight HE01-06 RT SPP on 7/5 (Saturday) was approximately $8-15/MWh — very cheap, cheaper than the HE09-10 solar trough DA price of $22/MWh
- Afternoon RT prices HE13-22 recovered to $40-60/MWh range
- The overnight/afternoon spread was structurally wider than the midday-trough/evening-peak spread I recommended

**Key insight**: On Saturdays and holiday-adjacent days, overnight RT prices often collapse further than solar trough DA prices (lower load + full wind). The overnight-to-afternoon RT arbitrage can exceed the solar-trough-to-peak DA arbitrage. This pattern has now appeared in multiple consecutive cycles.
