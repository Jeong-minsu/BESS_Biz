## 1. Key Decisions Made in the 2026-10-15 Strategy

### 1.1 Discharge Window: HE18-19 Only — No Extension to HE20

The single most consequential structural decision was limiting discharge strictly to HE18 (+100 MW) and HE19 (+84 MW) and declining to continue into HE20. The driver was the NL reversal at HE20: net load peaks at HE19 (52,856 MW) and immediately drops −1,418 MW at HE20 (51,438 MW) due to wind surge (+2,378 MW). With SoC reaching ~0.2 MWh after HE19, this was physically forced, but the reasoning is worth recording: even if SoC had allowed HE20 dispatch, the wind-driven NL decline and the aggressive WEST_TO_NORTH binding signal HE20 (SCI 0.359, 10-16%) made HE20 a poor candidate. The discharge window on Oct 15 is narrower and sharper than Oct 14 (HE19-20 window). This is a structural feature of the extreme diurnal GR_WEST swing (16,209 MW intraday range — all-time Stage 0 record).

**Implicit assumption**: The evening spike concentrates in HE18-19, driven by the solar collapse (−16,987 MW HE17→HE19) with wind only partially compensating. If wind surge onset is delayed by even 1 hour relative to forecast, HE20 could remain elevated — but given the DA lock-in strategy, RT timing uncertainty is a post-clearance risk not an ex-ante dispatch decision.

### 1.2 HE11 Partial Charge (−67 MW + NSPIN 33 MW)

Starting SoC of 138 MWh means the battery needs only 62 MWh of net charge to reach full (200 MWh). At 85% round-trip efficiency, that requires ~73 MWh input, achievable in ~44 minutes at 100 MW. Rather than charge a full hour, the strategy charged −67 MW (approximately 44-minute equivalent at full rate, adjusted to hourly block: 67 MWh input × 0.922 eff = ~61.8 MWh stored → SoC 199.8 MWh) and kept 33 MW in NSPIN_DA to maintain some AS revenue. The energy spread at HE11 — discharge estimated $60/MWh, charge estimated $22/MWh — is $38/MWh, substantially exceeding NSPIN_DA at ~$5/MWh. The partial charge approach recovers most of the energy arbitrage while limiting AS sacrifice.

**Note**: The SoC math here is important. The −67 MW partial charge combined with 85% round-trip efficiency results in SoC going from 138.0 to ~199.8 MWh, just below the 200 MWh physical limit. This leaves ~0.2 MWh headroom. In retrospect, −67 MW is tight if SoC efficiency has degraded even slightly from the 85% assumption. If actual round-trip efficiency is 80%, then 67 MWh input × 0.894 charge efficiency ≈ 59.9 MWh stored → SoC 197.9 MWh. Still very close to full. The risk is asymmetric and small.

### 1.3 Recharge HE20-22 with Staggered AS Mix

Rather than a clean full recharge in a single hour post-discharge, the strategy spread recharge across three hours (HE20: −70 MW, HE21: −50 MW, HE22: −30 MW) with AS co-optimization alongside each. The logic:
- HE20 (recharge + NSPIN_RT 30 MW): wind surge begins, price declining. DA bid at ~$27.5/MWh effective hub. Using RT for NSPIN preserves optionality since RT market is open at execution.
- HE21 (recharge + ECRS_DA 50 MW): playbook fine_rule (87 hrs) recommends ECRS_DA. NL 47,008 MW still elevated. ECRS makes sense at this NL level. Dual-use: charging battery while providing ECRS capacity response signal.
- HE22 (recharge + NSPIN_RT 70 MW): congestion signal shows GKS MCC −$2.5 to −$4.0/MWh at HE22 (WEST_TO_NORTH VERY HIGH, SCI 0.532). GKS effective charge price ~$14/MWh — lowest of any recharge hour. NSPIN_RT honored from playbook (fine_rule, 75 hrs).

Terminal SoC: 138.3 MWh, matching the Oct 14 start assumption. This is intentional — D+3 carry-in SCI 0.614 strongly signals cheap overnight Oct 16 charging, so there is no value in entering Oct 16 at high SoC.

### 1.4 AS-Hold HE01-06 (NSPIN_DA) and HE12-17 (NSPIN_DA)

These hours were assigned pure AS-hold positions with no energy dispatch. The reasoning is simply that there is no structural driver to charge in the OFFPEAK overnight (SoC already at 138 MWh going in, and overnight prices are $25-40/MWh — charging here would raise the effective charge cost, worsening the energy spread against HE18-19 discharge). The playbook fine_rule alignment for HE01-06 NSPIN_DA is strong (166 training hours). Similarly, HE12-17 NSPIN_DA represents the highest-confidence portion of the playbook, with consistent fine_rule signals across all midday/afternoon hours.

### 1.5 SoC Initialization Assumption (138 MWh)

This was carried from the Oct 14 strategy's terminal SoC projection. The Oct 14 strategy modeled HE22-24 recharges of −50 MW each from SoC=0 after HE20 discharge, arriving at ~138 MWh. This is unverified (Tenaska DOWN). If actual SoC entering Oct 15 is significantly different:
- If lower (e.g., 80 MWh): additional charging needed at HE10 or expanded HE11 block; NSPIN sacrifice larger.
- If higher (e.g., 200 MWh): skip HE11 charge entirely; full NSPIN_DA at HE11; discharge can start at full capacity from HE18.
- The strategy notes this and requires operator verification before bid submission.

### Key Override Decisions

**HE18-20 (fine_sparse_fallback_to_medium — low reliability)**: All three override decisions are well-founded. The `fine_sparse_fallback_to_medium` source label means the fine-grained model (Jan-May 2026) did not have enough Evening|Q5 training data (NL above ~47,000 MW) and fell back to the broader medium-grained cohort (140 training hours). This is precisely the problem: the highest-value hours for energy arbitrage are the hours where the fine playbook has the least resolution. The optimizer should always treat these hours as open candidates for energy dispatch rather than AS commitments.

**HE11 partial override**: The $38/MWh energy spread vs $5/MWh NSPIN is the correct calculus. 45 training hours for Midday|Q5|Q2 is adequate for NSPIN_DA in a pure AS-hold scenario, but when SoC needs topping up and energy spread is wide, the override is mandatory. **HE09-10 RRS_RT (20 training hours — sparse)**: Keeping full 100 MW in RRS_RT despite only 20 training hours was a judgment call. The fine_rule designation (not fallback) means the sparse cohort did produce a stable rule; the Morning|Q5|Q2 cohort transition from ECRS_DA (HE07-08) to RRS_RT (HE09-10) as solar enters the picture (6,191-15,042 MW HE09-10) has a plausible structural explanation: ECRS demand shifts to RT as the solar ramp creates frequency response needs, and RT venue captures the RT clearing premium during that transition. Judgment: honoring this prior is defensible but the sparseness warrants a mental flag — if actual clearing data shows ECRS_DA HE09-10 outperforming RRS_RT on similar days, revert.

## 3. SCI Binding Risk (GR_WEST HE14: 2,090 MW) — Influence on Dispatch Decisions

### What the Risk Was

The market briefing and congestion report flagged GR_WEST HE14 = 2,090 MW as 90 MW above the SCI binding threshold (~2,000 MW). With STWPF forecast error of ±10-15%, actual GR_WEST at HE14 could range 1,800-2,400 MW, meaning real-time binding was non-trivial. The congestion report rated this MILD duck curve (8-14% per hour HE10-12).

### How It Influenced the Oct 15 Strategy

**Direct effect on GKS dispatch: minimal.** The SCI binding constraint is a WEST_TO_NORTH transmission constraint, primarily affecting the flow of GR_WEST generation northward. GKS BESS (in the Houston/central zone) sits on the receiving side of this flow. When WEST_TO_NORTH binds, West Hub prices drop (west generation is curtailed) and North/central prices rise or remain flat. The GKS node MCC during midday is estimated at −$0.3 to −$0.8/MWh — a negligible effect.

**What changed because of the SCI flag:**
1. The congestion report's DUCK_CURVE_MIDDAY tier was upgraded from the Oct 14 sub-MILD level (35,522 MW trough) to MILD (33,443 MW trough). This is a mild deepening, not a structural change in strategy. The HE11 charge decision was already justified by the NL trough; the additional MCC benefit of −$0.3 to −$0.8/MWh only marginally improved the charge economics.
2. The SCI flag at HE12-17 (GR_WEST 2,090-3,703 MW) means there is essentially no West wind support during solar peak hours. This independently supports the NSPIN_DA hold strategy for HE12-17 (prices not aggressively suppressed by West wind, AS hold is competitive against energy at moderate NL levels of 35,000-44,000 MW).

**Critically**, the SCI binding concern for HE14 did NOT affect the evening discharge decision. The evening dispatch window (HE18-19) corresponds to SCI 0.197-0.281 — well below binding territory. The dominant HE18-19 story is solar collapse + moderate wind, where SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT P < 2% and GKS MCC ≈ $0.

**What the SCI flag did affect (for other agents):** The congestion report issued a strong signal for HE20-24 (WEST_TO_NORTH VERY HIGH, PANHANDLE HIGH) as the all-time record GR_WEST surge develops. This created the dispatch-ending signal at HE20: once SCI crosses 0.359 (HE20) and surges to 0.614 (HE24), the late-evening and overnight become low/negative GKS net LMP hours. The strategy correctly treated HE22-24 as pure recharge territory, exploiting the congestion-driven cheap charging window.

**Implicit test for RT validation**: If the HE14 SCI actually bound (GR_WEST dipped below 2,000 MW RT), there should be a negative MCC at GKS midday in the settlement. If Tenaska data ever restores, HE13-15 GKS MCC should show approximately −$1 to −$3/MWh (vs the −$0.3 to −$0.8/MWh Stage 0 estimate) if binding occurred. This would be a calibration data point for the congestion model.

## 6. Improvements to Model or Process

### 6.1 Action (P0, ONGOING) — Smartbidder Renewal

Day ~81 and counting. As noted in Oct 13 (Action 1), this is the highest-impact single restoration. AS price uncertainty of ±$5-10/MWh translates to ±$3,000-8,000/day revenue forecast error. No new development since Oct 13 flag.

### 6.2 Action (P0, ONGOING) — Tenaska PTP Restoration

~180 flowdays without settlement data. SoC compounding error from unverified daily cycle transitions. No new development. Joint escalation with Smartbidder renewal to Ascend rep remains the correct path.

### 6.3 Action (P1, NEW) — Drop HE18-19 ECRS Hedge in 21+ GW Outage Context

Both Oct 14 and Oct 15 strategies included a partial ECRS hedge at HE19 (16 MW each). The break-even price for ECRS to justify this hedge over full energy discharge is approximately $64/MWh in the Oct 2026 energy price regime. No realistic ECRS price in October shoulder season reaches this level. The hedge adds operational complexity without expected value.

**Rule to add**: When HE19 NL > 50,000 MW AND energy estimate > $55/MWh AND ECRS estimate < $40/MWh (all conditions) → allocate 100% of available SoC capacity to energy discharge. Drop ECRS hedge. Expected incremental revenue: +$200-400 per qualifying day.

### 6.4 Action (P1, ONGOING FROM OCT 13) — ERCOT Public Data Fallback Integration

Oct 13 learnings (§7.4E) proposed using ERCOT public SPP postings, AS clearing price archive, and operating reserve data to restore ~60% of calibration capability without Tenaska or Smartbidder. This has not been implemented. Given the ongoing 80+ day Smartbidder absence, the urgency is increasing. Each additional day without this fallback means another day of uncalibrated AS price estimates.

**Concrete improvement**: Add a daily check to `shared/scripts/fetch_market_data.py` or a separate `fetch_ercot_public_data.py` script to pull: (a) ERCOT SCED LMP at HB_HOUSTON and settlement points (1-day lag, public); (b) ERCOT DA/RT AS clearing prices (public); (c) ERCOT ORDC adder by hour (public). These sources require no API key and would restore price magnitude calibration immediately.

### 6.5 Action (MEDIUM, NEW) — Wind Surge Onset Timing Confidence

Oct 15's extreme diurnal GR_WEST profile (16,209 MW intraday swing) creates a critical strategic uncertainty: when exactly does the evening wind surge begin? The Yes Energy BidClose provides STWPF forecast shape, but RT wind can deviate by ±1-3 hours on surge onset relative to forecast. In the Oct 15 strategy:
- If surge starts at HE17 instead of HE18: discharge window may compress to HE18 only (HE17 still has 19,730 MW solar partially suppressing, but HE18 wind already at 13,000+ MW dampens spike).
- If surge starts at HE20 instead of HE19: HE19 spike lasts longer, Stack B's HE20 partial discharge is worth capturing.

**Proposed improvement**: Add a wind surge onset signal to the strategy documentation using the GR_WEST forecast derivative (rate of change per hour) to estimate timing risk. When GR_WEST is forecast to increase >3,000 MW in a single hour during the HE18-22 window, flag that hour as "surge onset candidate" and explicitly model the Stack B alternative with a P(surge delayed) weighting.

### 6.6 Action (LOW, ONGOING) — Playbook Fine-Grained Training Coverage Gap for Evening|Q5

The three HE18-20 fine_sparse_fallback_to_medium hours represent a structural gap in the playbook: Jan-May 2026 training period had insufficient Evening|Q5 (NL > ~47,000 MW) hours because Q5-level NL in October occurs only in fall shoulder season. When Oct/Nov 2026 data accumulates, the playbook should be retrained to add these high-NL evening hours at fine granularity. Until then, HE18-20 should always be treated as open energy/AS optimization candidates regardless of fallback recommendation.

### 6.7 Calibration Note — D+2 Carry-in Methodology First Failure

The congestion-analyst documented the first D+2 carry-in failure this cycle (Oct 14 predicted MEDIUM overnight for Oct 15, actual was LOW/NEGLIGIBLE). The overnight wind decay rate was 2.6x faster than the Sep analog used for calibration. This does not directly affect bess-optimizer decisions (the overnight NSPIN_DA position was not dependent on a D+2 carry-in signal), but it is a reminder that the congestion model is single-analog calibrated and can fail on wind regime transitions. For Stage 1 development, multi-analog calibration with decay-rate distributions is needed.
