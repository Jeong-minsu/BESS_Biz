### 7.3 Cap-out 20-21 GW → ORDC 영향 반영 수준

**Outage context:** 21,192 MW maintenance outages on 2026-10-14. ERCOT summer thermal capacity nominally ~85 GW. Net available capacity (rough): ~85,000 − 21,192 = ~63,808 MW vs NL peak 54,632 MW → implied operating reserve margin ~9,176 MW (16.8%).

**ORDC mechanism:**
- ERCOT ORDC adds a $ adder per MWh to SCED dispatch prices when operating reserves fall below high-sustained limit thresholds
- ORDC adder tiers: VOLL ($5,000/MWh) × P(reserves < physical responsive capacity)
- For a 16.8% margin day: ORDC adder during peak hours typically $2-8/MWh for energy, but AS prices (ECRS, RRS) can see 20-50% uplift from ORDC-triggered scarcity signal
- With 21 GW out, any unplanned 1-2 GW trip during HE19-20 could rapidly compress reserve to <3,000 MW and trigger the $25/MWh ORDC band

**What was reflected in Oct 14 recommendation:**
- Qualitative flag: "21+ GW maintenance outages — ORDC/AS spike flag HE16-21" ✓
- AS bull case: ECRS/RRS $20-30/MWh if ORDC spikes ✓
- Base case ECRS HE07-10: $12/MWh — this does NOT incorporate ORDC uplift ✗
- Base case ECRS HE19 hedge: $12/MWh — same issue ✗

**Calibration gap identified:** The base-case AS prices were not adjusted upward to account for the elevated outage environment. For a 20+ GW outage day in shoulder season, a more defensible base-case adjustment would be:
- ECRS DA HE07-10: $12/MWh → $16-18/MWh (+33-50% for ORDC signal). This would add ~$200-$240 to the AS revenue base.
- ECRS/RRS at HE19-21: similarly $12 → $16-20/MWh. 

**Root cause:** Without Smartbidder, there is no systematic way to model ORDC-AS price correlation. The correction would require either: (a) access to ERCOT historical ORDC adder data by outage level; or (b) Smartbidder AS price forecasts. Both are unavailable.

**Forward rule (add to plans):** When congestion-analyst reports maintenance outages >20 GW AND it is a WDPEAK day, apply +$4/MWh base uplift to ECRS DA assumptions (HE07-10, HE19-21) and +$2/MWh to NSPIN DA assumptions (HE13-17). This is a conservative outage-day premium heuristic until Smartbidder restores. Estimated incremental revenue with this adjustment on Oct 14: +$400-600.

### 7.4 실적 데이터 부재 상황에서 Self-Calibration 한계 구조 분석

**Five dimensions of calibration failure:**

**A. Price magnitude — no ground truth:**
All price estimates ($30/MWh charge, $80-85/MWh discharge, $12/MWh ECRS) are derived from NL shape + rule-of-thumb regression (×0.80 haircut on crude estimates). There is no verified price-to-NL regression coefficients from recent clearing data. Historical Yes Energy BidClose prices provide shape, not magnitude. Uncertainty: ±$20-30/MWh on energy prices (±$10 on AS prices).

**B. SoC continuity — compounding error:**
Oct 11 terminal SoC 92.2 MWh → Oct 14 assumed 92 MWh (2-day gap carry). If GKS operated in default or emergency mode on Oct 12-13, the actual SoC could be 0-200 MWh. Each day without settlement data adds 0-200 MWh SoC uncertainty. Current compounded uncertainty from ~179 days without Tenaska: theoretically unlimited (though bounded by daily cycle reset constraints). In practice, the Oct 11 SoC estimate is the most reliable anchor since the system has been offline.

**C. AS price calibration — seasonal drift undetected:**
The ECRS $12/MWh and NSPIN $5/MWh estimates are from memory. October shoulder season may have structurally different AS clearing prices than the summer peak. Without Smartbidder data (expired Day ~82 = ~late July 2026), there is 82+ days of AS market drift not captured. If the fall maintenance season has driven ECRS to $15-18/MWh structurally (consistent with higher outage levels), the AS revenue is systematically underestimated. Estimated bias: −$1,000 to −$3,000/day.

**D. Benchmark gap — no error signal:**
Without Smartbidder's Mount Blue Sky benchmark, there is no signal to distinguish systematic errors from random variation. The optimizer cannot know if it is: (1) correctly identifying optimal hours but mispricing; (2) selecting wrong hours; or (3) misweighting AS vs. energy. The benchmark gap means the optimizer's calibration model is running open-loop with no feedback signal for 82+ days.

**E. Structural decision validation — directional only:**
Self-reviews since early May 2026 have been qualitative ("directionally reasonable", "structurally correct"). Zero quantitative delta analysis has been possible. A 179-day absence of settlement data means the optimizer has effectively been running on priors alone for 6 months. Any systematic error introduced in early May would have compounded unchecked. This represents the highest-severity calibration risk in the system.

**Self-calibration protocol proposal (to add to plans):**

When both data sources are down, the following manual calibration checks should be applied weekly:
1. Cross-check Yes Energy BidClose prices vs. ERCOT public price postings (SPP at HB_HOUSTON, DA LMP at settlement points) — these are publicly available with a 1-day lag without Tenaska.
2. Use ERCOT's public AS clearing price archive (ERCOT website, daily posting) to verify ECRS/NSPIN clearing prices — no Smartbidder needed.
3. ERCOT public operating reserve data to validate ORDC adder levels on high-outage days.

These three public data sources could restore ~60% of calibration capability without Tenaska or Smartbidder. They should be integrated into the daily cycle as a fallback input pathway. This is a P1 improvement action.
