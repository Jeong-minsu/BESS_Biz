## 2. Root Cause Analysis — -$4,997 Delta

### Finding A: Actual DA charge schedule was HE11-HE18 (not HE09-HE11)

**Evidence**: Tenaska shows DA energy purchases in HE11-HE18 (50 MW each at $17.51-$25.80/MWh). Our recommendation called for DA charging at HE09-HE11 (100+100+35 MW at effective $15-18/MWh nodal).

Actual charge cost: -$8,972 (8 hours × 50 MW × avg $22.43/MWh)
Recommended charge cost: ~-$3,172 (3 hours at lower prices with MCC benefit)
**Charge cost overrun: ~$5,800**

The actual physical schedule charged LATER in the day (HE11-18) at progressively more expensive hours, missing the duck curve trough at HE09-10. In particular:
- HE09 actual: no DA purchase (while net load = 25,966 MW, optimal charging moment)
- HE10 actual: no DA purchase (net load trough = 21,706 MW with -$5.5/MWh MCC — cheapest hour of the week)
- HE15-18 actual: charged at $23-$26/MWh — 2x the optimal charge cost

The actual schedule appears to have been submitted by Smartbidder's automated bidding engine (which was DEGRADED for market data but may have retained its default bid templates), not following bess-optimizer's morning charge recommendation.

### Finding B: Discharge was RT-only, not DA as recommended

**Evidence**: DA energy sales at HE20-21 were only 0.1 MW each ($3.49 + $3.58 = $7.07 total DA sell) — effectively no DA discharge committed. Actual discharge occurred in RT: HE20 +$1,125.56, HE21 +$1,011.75, HE22 +$1,234.66.

RT prices at HE20-22 were ~$37-40/MWh (reconstructed from RT SCSPP data). Our recommendation modeled DA discharge at $29-31/MWh hub ($31-33/MWh nodal). The actual RT outcome was BETTER than our DA estimate ($37-40 > $31-33), so the failure to commit DA discharge was actually partially beneficial — RT outperformed our DA forecast on this day.

**However**: This masks the structural issue. The recommendation explicitly said "DA dominant" with P(DA>RT) reasoning. The actual outcome showed RT > DA at HE20-22, meaning our DA-heavy call was wrong directionally for Jul 25. The correct strategy on Jul 25 was RT-heavy discharge, which is what actually happened (possibly by default/automation, not by deliberate signal reading).

### Finding C: Non-spin fell short of recommendation by $1,319

Actual Non-spin: $2,549. Recommended (60 MW HE01-08 + 60 MW HE12-19 + etc.): ~$3,868 total AS.

The gap has two sub-causes:
1. **Standby hours HE12-19**: Actual Non-spin revenue was $501 across these hours (42.4-32.0 per hour for 80 MW). Recommendation expected ~$1,980 for this window. The actual clearing prices were significantly lower than estimated ($0.41-$1.38/MW·h vs assumed $4.50/MW·h average).
2. **Evening Non-spin HE21-24**: Actual Non-spin captured $778 (ECRS $283 added). This performed well; our recommendation underestimated HE22-23 Non-spin ($348+$314).

### Finding D: RT energy HE09-10 was a loss (-$670 + -$690)

Actual RT energy at HE09: -$729.66; HE10: -$689.75. This is a consumption cost — the BESS was drawing power from the grid in RT at a positive RT price. With no DA purchase in HE09-10, and Non-spin committed at 80 MW, the imbalance position resulted in RT consumption being billed at RTSPP. This is the residual of the NS-energy interaction: the battery may have been physically charging during these hours (operator opportunistic charging) while simultaneously carrying Non-spin obligation.

**Actual**: RT discharge prices $37-40/MWh; DA discharge was essentially zero (0.1 MW). RT was HIGHER than DA at HE20-22.

**Root cause of wrong call**: 
- Jul 25 was Saturday WEPEAK with lower base demand (69,740 MW vs weekday). Lower base demand → DA market did not price the SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT constraint as heavily into DA clearing as on WDPEAK days.
- RT prices at HE20-22 were elevated by residual evening demand without DA fully capturing it.
- Lesson: On WEPEAK Saturdays with LOW-MEDIUM constraint probability (22-30%), RT is more likely to exceed DA at discharge hours because the constraint premium is NOT fully priced into DA. Reserve more RT optionality on weekend days.
