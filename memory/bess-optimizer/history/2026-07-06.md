## 7. AS Product Allocation Rationale

### Why 80 MW NS across all 24 hours
Smartbidder's confirmed execution pattern (6 consecutive days including Jul 4) is NS 80 MW × 24h flat regardless of recommendation. This is treated as a structural constant. Expected clearing improves significantly in HE20-23 (forecast $9-13/MWh) aligned with the scarcity event — the timing of Smartbidder's default flat pattern coincidentally aligns with the optimal NS window on this day.

### Why no ECRS/RRS in primary revenue line
- ECRS cleared on Jul 4 ($351) — one instance after 4+ consecutive $0 cycles. Jul 6 ECRS forecast ($5.95 HE21, $5.71 HE22) is compelling, but NS at $12.09/$10.94 in the same hours means Smartbidder will prioritize NS (NS >> ECRS in these hours). ECRS headroom = 100 - 80 NS = 20 MW at most. Most likely ECRS offering occurs in HE1-8 where NS is lower ($2-4/MWh) and ECRS may compete at the margin. Included at $300 (low confidence).
- RRS: No clearing signal. $0 maintained.

### Why no RegDown despite HE13-17 spike ($3.19)
- RegDown requires available absorption capacity (SoC < 190 MWh). We hold SoC at 190 MWh from HE10 through HE20 to preserve full HE21 discharge capacity. If we left 20-30 MWh headroom for RegDown in HE13-17, we'd add $3.19 × 20 MW × 5h = $319 max, but risk not being at full SoC for HE21, costing potentially $3.19 × SoC_reduction × $58.64 / SoC = far more in lost discharge revenue. RegDown excluded.

### Why no RegUp during discharge (HE21)
- RegUp requires available upward capacity. We're already bidding 100 MW discharge in HE21 (physical max). No headroom for RegUp on top of full discharge.

### ECRS + NS constraint check
- HE21: NS 80 MW + ECRS 0 MW = 80 MW ≤ 100 MW limit ✓
- All hours: 80 MW NS stays within AS constraint throughout schedule ✓
