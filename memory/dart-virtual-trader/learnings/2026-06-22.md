## 5. Key Learnings

### Learning 1 — Morning DEC at HE10-11: cloud cover + high cap-out = extreme RT spike

The HE10-11 DEC positions were issued with modest expectations (E[P&L] +$24 combined). Actual proxy outcome: ~+$2,176. The driver was a morning RT spike to ~$60/MWh when:
- Solar severely underdelivered (AG2 forecast 74-88% cloud cover at HE07-09 materialized)
- Cap-out of 12,196-12,246 MW constrained available resources
- DA was set at solar-suppression lows ($16.62 / $17.50) — the market fully priced in solar peaks that did not occur

**Actionable**: When AG2 cloud cover forecast is ≥70% for HE07-10 AND cap-out exceeds 11,500 MW, the DEC spread at HE10-12 should be estimated not at the compressed 0.6x base case, but at a cloud-bust scenario that explicitly models: "solar at 50% of YE forecast × RT price adjustment." The current model compresses all estimates to 0.6x and does not have a separate meteorological-bust scenario. This is a quantifiable model gap.

**Consider**: Adding an AG2 cloud cover parameter to the E[spread] calculation for morning DEC hours. Cloud cover ≥70% at HE07-09 → apply 1.5x multiplier to raw E[spread] DEC estimate before 0.6x compression (net effective: 0.6 × 1.5 = 0.9x).

### Learning 2 — HE14 RT spike: afternoon thermal stress not captured

Tenaska data implies RT at HE14 ≈ $175/MWh against DA ≈ $18.63/MWh (DA−RT ≈ −$156/MWh). The model predicted INC for HE14-18 (P=0.57-0.58) and skipped due to below-floor 0.6x EV. The actual spread direction at HE14 was strongly negative (DEC wins), not positive (INC). This is a model direction error for HE14-15 specifically.

The DEC signal at HE10-11 was recognized and positioned. The model's afternoon INC signal for HE14-18 appears to have been wrong in direction for at least HE14. Possible causes:
- The GR_WEST trough at HE14 (forecast 6,911 MW) likely realized lower, creating RT thermal stress that drove RT >> DA
- The 0.6x compressed spread for INC at HE14 was well below floor — which inadvertently avoided an incorrect INC position, but the model failed to recognize the true DEC signal persisting into the afternoon

**Actionable**: The morning DEC regime (P(DA<RT) 0.60-0.63) should not be assumed to terminate at HE14. On days with AG2 cloud cover lingering AND high cap-out, the morning DEC regime can extend to HE14-15 as solar generation ramps slower than forecast. Add an "extended DEC regime" check: if cap-out remains > 11,500 MW at HE12-13 and cloud cover at HE10 was confirmed ≥70%, extend the DEC regime evaluation through HE15 before transitioning to afternoon INC.

### Learning 3 — Below-floor override cap: 3 hours, not 2, on cloud-bust days

On 2026-06-22, the highest realized spread hours were HE10 (−$43.87) and HE11 (−$43.14). HE09 was skipped because the 2-override cap was exhausted. HE12 had higher cap-out (12,276 MW) than HE10-11 and was also skipped. The 2-cap rule was correctly applied given the pre-trade information set, but if a cloud-cover parameter were incorporated (Learning 1), the EV ranking within the dual-trigger window would have scored HE09-12 much higher, and the 2-cap might be used at HE09+HE10 rather than HE10+HE11.

The cap at 2 is still appropriate as a discipline anchor. The issue is input quality to the EV ranking. Better AG2 cloud-bust adjustment (Learning 1) would rank these hours more accurately within the cap limit.

### Learning 4 — HE19 basis position: net worst hour of the day (−$558.50)

GKS's worst hourly result was HE19 at −$558.50. This reflects the physical BESS having a large DA purchasing position (50 MWh) at a time when the DA price was very high and RT recovery was insufficient. The DART virtual basis position at HE19 (Long HB_HOUSTON DA / Short HB_NORTH DA) is embedded and unverifiable separately, but the large negative DA energy at HE19 is concerning.

Possible interpretation: DA at HB_HOUSTON at HE19 was very expensive (consistent with HOUSTON_IMPORT binding). If so, the basis trade (Long HB_HOUSTON DA) had a high-cost entry. The RT recovery at HE19 (+$519.75 on the physical side) suggests RT at GKS's physical node was significantly below DA — which for the basis trade means: we bought HB_HOUSTON DA at a premium, and RT settled lower than DA at that node. For the pure virtual basis (not physical), the P&L depends on HB_HOUSTON DA vs HB_NORTH DA, not on the DA−RT spread at any one node.
