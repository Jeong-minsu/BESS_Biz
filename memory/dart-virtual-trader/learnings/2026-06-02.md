## 4. Key Decisions Assessed

### 4.1 Overnight Long DA Suspension — Confirmed

The 2026-06-01 learning instructed: "Drop overnight Long DA (HE01-02) until settlement data is available." This was correctly applied in the June 2 cycle — no HE01-02 positions issued. The wind at HE01 (16,040 MW) strengthens this suspension: high overnight wind compresses DA, making RT > DA (Long DA profits) less likely not more likely, unless wind suppresses DA all the way below RT (which would be a negative spread outcome). The structural case for overnight Long DA is weaker than originally modeled.

Status: SUSPENSION MAINTAINED. No re-entry until Tenaska settlement validates the overnight Long DA signal for at least 3 confirmed days.

### 4.2 Confidence Discount 0.85 (Smartbidder Absent) — Appropriate

The 0.85 factor reduced total notional from the prior-day 275 MW to 175 MW. All 5 active positions are assessed as directionally likely correct based on structural proxy. This suggests the 0.85 factor may be over-conservative for direction but appropriate for size (the actual spread magnitudes are uncertain without Smartbidder). The decision to resize HE19 and HE22 to MED (25 MW) instead of HIGH (50 MW) was the primary practical effect of the discount.

Finding: The 0.85 discount protocol should be maintained as-is. Direction was likely intact; the size reduction was the appropriate risk management response to data uncertainty. However, this also means the position was leaving potential P&L on the table at HE19 and HE22 — the cost of the data gap.

Proposed refinement (pending confirmation): If future Tenaska data shows that 0.85-discount cycles produced the same win rate as 0.95-discount cycles, the size reduction formula should be revisited. Consider keeping HIGH tier for hours where structural analog (prior confirmed days) is strong AND spread forecast is above $6/MWh, even without Smartbidder. This would have raised HE19 from MED to HIGH (spread +$4.00 — actually below the $6 threshold, so the rule would not have applied here). But HE20 (+$8.00) and HE21 (+$8.00) would both qualify, and both were already HIGH — so no change there. Only HE19 and HE22 were downsized.

### 4.3 HE20 Solar Source Divergence — Open Item

The Enverus (541 MW) vs YE (4,764 MW) solar gap at HE20 is the largest unresolved forecast calibration issue in this review. Two consecutive days (June 1 and June 2) showed significant Enverus-YE divergence at HE20. The June 3 history file notes the same divergence was smaller (Enverus 6,115 MW vs YE 4,555 MW — +1,560 MW) and the position was held at HIGH regardless.

Until settlement data resolves which source was more accurate at HE20, this item cannot be closed. It is tracked as the top open calibration item for the model.

### 4.4 HE17 Thin-Spread MED Entry — Borderline

The HE17 position (DA ~$33, spread +$2.00, P=68%, E[gross] ~$41) cleared all thresholds but barely. In comparison, June 3 shows HE17 with spread +$6.26 (Smartbidder confirmed) — three times the spread. Without Smartbidder, the June 2 HE17 entry was supply-demand inferred with low spread confidence.

The $41 E[gross] clears the $30 minimum, but the actual expected outcome is sensitive to the assumed spread. If actual spread was +$1 instead of +$2 (a plausible range given supply-demand inference without Smartbidder), E[gross] would be only $20 — below threshold. This suggests the HE17 entry in a no-Smartbidder cycle should have a higher spread minimum (e.g., +$3 or above) to account for forecast error in the spread itself.

Proposed rule addition: In Smartbidder-absent cycles (confidence 0.85), apply a minimum spread floor of +$3.00 for HE15-18 pre-ramp entries (vs the standard $5 MED floor), replacing the standard floor calculation with a stricter one to account for spread inference uncertainty. Under this rule, HE17 at +$2.00 would be skipped in a 0.85-confidence cycle.
