## 3. Root Cause Analysis

### Issue 1 — All-day DA > RT regime: model missed the dominant spread structure

The entire 2026-06-20 trading day showed DA > RT in 18 out of 24 hours, with a 24-hour average spread of +$2.74/MWh. **INC (Short DA / Long RT) was the correct direction for most of the day**, including the active position hours HE19-21.

The model positioned DEC (Long DA / Short RT = INC direction WRONG) for HE19-21, expecting RT to exceed DA during the evening ramp. Instead:
- RT at HE19-21 was $26.15, $30.03, $31.32/MWh
- DA at HE19-21 was $29.12, $34.46, $37.39/MWh
- DA remained above RT with expanding spreads through the evening

The DEC thesis required a RT spike to push RT above DA. This did not occur. The evening ramp (net load +9,498 MW HE19→HE21) did materialize in load terms, but the RT price response was moderate, staying well below DA.

**Why was the all-day DA > RT regime not identified pre-trade?** The model focused on the evening ramp as a potential RT spike catalyst and used HOUSTON_IMPORT binding (20-30%) to support a DA congestion premium thesis. But the 20-30% binding probability also implies 70-80% non-binding — the modal outcome was no binding, no spike, and DA simply clearing higher than RT due to the inherent DAM conservative pricing structure on a Saturday with thin liquidity.

### Issue 2 — Critical logical inconsistency in the recommendation

Reading `reports/daily/dart-trader/2026-06-20.md` (the "Why" section under DEC thesis), the stated rationale includes:

> "HOUSTON_IMPORT_345 P(binding) 20-30% MEDIUM → DA가 혼잡 프리미엄 선반영 → DA > RT 구조 예상"

This reasoning says: **DA > RT is expected** (because DA congestion premium).

But a DEC position (Long DA / Short RT) wins when **RT > DA** (spread negative). The stated reasoning explicitly predicts DA > RT, which means **INC (Short DA / Long RT)** is the correct position if the reasoning is believed.

The recommendation issued DEC while the reasoning predicted the outcome that makes INC win. This is an internal logical inversion. The position direction and the stated market conviction were opposite.

**This was the single most important error in the 2026-06-20 cycle.** It likely originated from conflating two separate components of the thesis:
- Component A (correctly stated): "DA congestion premium → DA > RT → INC wins"
- Component B (incorrectly applied): Using the HOUSTON_IMPORT congestion framing to justify DEC, when DEC actually requires RT > DA

The 2026-06-06 Saturday reference (INC all-loss, DEC was correct) may have caused anchoring bias — the model defaulted to DEC without re-checking whether the current cycle's specific reasoning actually supported that direction.

### Issue 3 — RT peak at HE14-15 CT: 4-5 hours earlier than forecast, entirely missed

Actual RT peak was at HE14-15 CT (13:00-15:00 CT):
- HE14: RT = $32.00/MWh, DA = $23.73/MWh → spread = -$8.27 (INC wins by $206.75 on 25 MW)
- HE15: RT = $34.02/MWh, DA = $25.43/MWh → spread = -$8.59 (INC wins by $214.75 on 25 MW)
- **Missed INC opportunity at HE14-15: approximately +$421.50 gross** (unplaced)

The model treated HE10-15 as a blanket solar plateau / low-spread skip zone ("태양광 25.7 GW 피크, NL 34,338 MW 최저, DA/RT 모두 저가 → spread 미미"). This assumption was wrong:
- While NL was at its daily minimum in HE12-13 (34,338 MW), the solar-induced DA price suppression created a window where DA was very low ($22-25/MWh) while RT responded to actual real-time demand/supply mismatch at a higher level ($26-34/MWh).
- The thermal unit commitment in DAM during periods of high solar generation leads to low DA prices. But RT dispatch during the solar-ramp-off period (14:00-16:00 CT when solar starts declining from peak) requires fast-start units that may clear at higher RT prices.
- On a Saturday, the effect is amplified: thin DAM leads to under-procurement of thermal capacity in DA, creating RT scarcity as solar declines.

**Learning**: Solar peak hours on Saturday should NOT be assumed to be low-spread. Check for INC opportunity when: (a) solar forecast >20 GW, (b) Saturday thin DAM, (c) thermal outages >10 GW. The combination suppresses DA, and RT may diverge upward during the solar transition period.

### Issue 4 — Model calibration gap after 7+ DEGRADED cycles

The model entered 2026-06-20 with zero confirmed settlement data since 2026-06-15 (7 cycles). The P(win) estimates (57-62%) and E[spread] estimates (-$5 to -$7.5/MWh) were generated from a model that had not been calibrated against any recent outcomes. The Regime A vs Regime B classification (RT spike vs DA premium) was untested for the MEDIUM HOUSTON_IMPORT tier on a Saturday.

With calibration frozen:
- P(win) estimates may be significantly off from realized rates
- Regime classification thresholds may be stale relative to current market conditions
- Saturday pattern hypothesis (Issue 2 of prior review) was built on a single data point (2026-06-06)

**Post-facto Saturday data points now confirmed:**
- 2026-06-06 (Sat): INC positioned → LOSS (spread was positive, DA > RT → DEC was correct direction)
- 2026-06-20 (Sat): DEC positioned → LOSS (spread was positive, DA > RT → INC was correct direction... wait)

Both Saturdays show DA > RT (positive spread). On 2026-06-06, the model used INC and lost (INC loses when DA > RT). On 2026-06-20, the model used DEC and lost (DEC also loses when... wait, DEC wins when RT > DA, DEC loses when DA > RT).

Re-checking: DEC (Long DA / Short RT) WINS when DA is bought low and RT is sold high — but actually for virtual positions: DEC = buy DA, sell RT. Revenue = DA_buy_price - RT_sell_price... 

Restating with ERCOT mechanics:
- DEC bid: buy in DAM at DA price, settle short against RTSPP → profit if DA < RT (RT is more expensive, you sold RT at higher price)
- INC offer: sell in DAM at DA price, settle long against RTSPP → profit if DA > RT (you sold DA at higher price, bought RT cheaper)

Wait — applying the project spread convention: spread = DA − RT. Positive = DA expensive.
- **INC (Short DA / Long RT) wins when spread > 0 (DA > RT)**
- **DEC (Long DA / Short RT) wins when spread < 0 (RT > DA)**

On 2026-06-20: spread = +$2.74 average (DA > RT). **INC was the winning direction.**
On 2026-06-06: the prior review says "INC all-loss" and "DEC was the correct direction." If DEC was correct on 2026-06-06, then spread was < 0 on that day (RT > DA). But on 2026-06-20, spread was +$2.74 (DA > RT), making INC correct.

The two Saturday data points actually show **opposite spread regimes**:
- 2026-06-06: RT > DA → DEC wins
- 2026-06-20: DA > RT → INC wins

There is no consistent Saturday directional pattern. Saturday-specific directional bias cannot be derived from 2 opposing data points. Saturday directional hypothesis is unsupported.
