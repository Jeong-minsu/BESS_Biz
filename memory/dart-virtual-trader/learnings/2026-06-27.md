## 5. Key Learnings from This Cycle

### Learning 1 — Cap-out profile week-over-week sensitivity

The cap-out trajectory in the June series has been:
- Jun 22 (Mon): 12,196-12,246 MW (dual-trigger activated → DEC override successfully applied)
- Jun 24 (Wed): 9,343 MW (below threshold — DEC skip correct; Smartbidder HE15 captured instead)
- Jun 26 (Fri): 10,051-10,069 MW (above 10,000 MW but below 11,500 MW — split signals)
- Jun 27 (Sat): 9,987-10,159 MW (below threshold consistently; DEC override NOT activated)
- Jun 28 (Sun): 8,884 MW max (weakest June series; dual-trigger furthest from activation)

The cap-out profile has been trending lower through the week. Sunday (Jun 28) has the lowest cap-out level in the entire June tracking series. This pattern makes structural sense: outages tend to be scheduled on weekends when demand is lower. The implication is that midday DEC opportunities (which require cap-out ≥ 11,500 MW or ≥ 10,000 MW as primary trigger) are systematically HARDER to capture on weekends — exactly when the weekend DEC ban also applies to evening hours.

**Actionable**: On weekends with cap-out expected below 9,000 MW, the probability of midday DEC dual-trigger activation is near-zero. The model should not allocate analytical effort to midday DEC evaluation on these days — the signal-to-noise ratio is too low.

### Learning 2 — HE23 basis choice (HOUSTON vs WEST_TO_NORTH) decision framework

On Jun 27, HE23 was entered as Short HB_WEST / Long HB_NORTH (WEST_TO_NORTH basis). On Jun 28, HE23 is entered as Long HB_HOUSTON / Short HB_NORTH (HOUSTON_IMPORT basis). The switch is driven by marginal probability differences (28% HOUSTON_IMPORT vs 14-23% WEST_TO_NORTH lower bound at HE23).

This decision creates cycle-to-cycle inconsistency that makes backtesting the HE23 basis harder. A cleaner rule would be:
- If P(HOUSTON_IMPORT binding) at HE23 > P(WEST_TO_NORTH binding) at HE23: enter HOUSTON basis
- If P(WEST_TO_NORTH binding) > P(HOUSTON_IMPORT binding): enter WEST_TO_NORTH basis
- If co-binding risk is high (both above 20%): consider splitting 12.5 MW each, or SKIP to avoid HB_NORTH co-exposure

The current decision is correct (HOUSTON_IMPORT at 28% > WEST_TO_NORTH at 14-23%) but the rule should be formalized to prevent ad-hoc switching.
