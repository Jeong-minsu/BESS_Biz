## 4. Key Findings from This Review

### Finding 1: HE21 "Exception Override" Was a Misclassification — Rule Clarification

The history file labeled HE21 as a "HIGH exception override (P=62.2%, technically MED by P alone)." This was incorrect. Per AGENT.md Section 2 matrix:
- High conviction: P ≥ 60%, W:L ≥ 1.5:1 → full size (50 MW)
- Medium: 55-60%, W:L 1.2-1.5:1 → half size (25 MW)

P=62.2% is above the 60% HIGH threshold. The position was correctly sized HIGH without any exception. The confusion arose from inconsistently reading "≥60%" as "≥65%." Going forward:
- HIGH tier: P ≥ 60% AND spread ≥ $4 (using existing hard floor)
- MED tier: P 55-60% AND spread ≥ $4
- SKIP: P < 55% OR spread < $4

This clarification means more hours qualify for HIGH tier than previously modeled. The prior cycles' MED-sized positions at P=62% (e.g., HE22 multiple cycles, HE18 some cycles) should have been HIGH-tier 50 MW, not MED 25 MW. **This is a material finding** — it suggests the model has been undersizing positions at P=60-65% range.

Impact on prior cycles (retroactive): If HE18 and HE22 at P=62% had been 50 MW instead of 25 MW, the total notional would have been higher and the proxy P&L estimates would be larger. Given the likely-win assessments for those hours, this represents opportunity cost from undersizing.

Proposed rule correction: **HIGH tier = P ≥ 60%** (not "≥65%"). MED tier = P 55-60%. This is already in AGENT.md — the agent was misreading its own source of truth.

### Finding 2: HE18 "Caution" Learning from 06-01 May Be Overly Restrictive

The 2026-06-01 learning established a general caution on HE18 sizing (keep at MED even if HIGH criteria met). This was a reasonable response to the thin-spread HE18 entry on June 1 (spread $2.40). But today's HE18 at +$8.80 and P=62% would be clearly HIGH under the corrected rule above. The "HE18 caution" was appropriate when the spread was thin ($2.40) — it is not appropriate when the spread is $8.80.

Proposed refinement: The HE18 caution should be retired as a blanket rule and replaced with: "HE18 MED if spread < $5; HE18 HIGH if spread ≥ $5 and P ≥ 60%." This preserves the caution for thin-spread entries while allowing full sizing when conviction is high.

### Finding 3: Trailing Proxy Hit Rate at HIGH Tier is Very Strong

Reviewing all HIGH-tier (50 MW) positions across 12 cycles: every HIGH position assessed in prior learnings has been assigned LIKELY WIN or HIGH CONVICTION WIN status at proxy. No HIGH-tier position has been assessed as LIKELY LOSS in proxy reviews. This is consistent with the ≥60% P threshold — at P=60%, the model's probability is inherently favorable.

The proxy win rate (82-88%) remains stable and above the 60% target minimum. The win:loss ratio (proxy ~1.8-2.5:1) exceeds the 1.5:1 HIGH threshold.

Confidence assessment: HIGH that the SHORT DA strategy in the HE19-21 evening window is structurally valid. Confidence remains MEDIUM on exact spread magnitudes (no settlement data).

The lower total notional on 06-05 (75 MW vs 225 MW) correctly reflects the weaker signal — fewer passing hours and lower spread magnitudes. The model is behaving appropriately.

### Finding 5: HE21 Narrowly Missed the 06-05 $4 Floor (spread $3.6 vs $4.0)

The 2026-06-05 cycle skipped HE21 at spread $3.6 (below $4 hard floor). But HE21 had Non-Spin $9.2/MWh — the day's highest AS signal. This raises the question: should the $4 spread floor have a Non-Spin modifier?

The model may be systematically underestimating the HE21 spread when Non-Spin is at cycle-high levels, because the AS-to-energy price coupling is not explicitly modeled.

Proposed test: Track whether realized spreads at HE21 on days with Non-Spin > $8 systematically exceed the Smartbidder forecast spread. If confirmed over 5+ cycles, reduce the spread floor to $3.50 for hours where Non-Spin > $8/MWh. This would have qualified the 06-05 HE21 position. Requires settlement data to confirm.
