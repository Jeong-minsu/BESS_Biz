### 5b. Overnight DEC Signal Paradox — Wind Surge

**Observation**: Oct 22 overnight DEC adj P 55-57% (ACTIVE, 4 positions). Oct 23 overnight DEC adj P 52-54% (all SKIP). Wind increase from 8 GW to 15.6 GW weakened, not strengthened, the DEC signal.

**Mechanism**: The DEC overnight signal requires RT > DA — i.e., RT to be soft while DA maintains a premium. This premium comes from DA incorporating thermal scheduling costs. When wind is very high (15.6 GW), DA itself is bid very low (reflecting expected wind supply), compressing the DA-RT spread from both sides. With DA at $28-30 (vs $30-34 on Oct 22), there is less "thermal premium" in DA for RT to underperform.

**Learning**: High overnight wind reduces DEC edge because it compresses DA toward RT rather than allowing RT to fall below DA. The optimal DEC signal occurs when wind is MODERATE to HIGH (enough to soften RT) but DA still maintains structural premium (thermal scheduling requires non-zero DA price floor).

**Implication for model**: Consider wind as a non-linear factor in DEC edge — moderate wind (8-12 GW) may create better DEC edge than very high wind (15+ GW).
