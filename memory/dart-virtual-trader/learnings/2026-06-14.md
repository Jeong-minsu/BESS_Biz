## 4. Pattern Analysis — ECRS Clearing as INC Risk Indicator

The 2026-06-14 P&L data shows `as_revenue_ecrs: $612.48` with ECRS clearing only HE21-24. ECRS is ERCOT's emergency reserve product; it clears when ERCOT anticipates reserve shortfalls. When ECRS clears in the evening hours:

- It indicates real-time supply is tight relative to demand in those hours
- RT energy prices are pulled up by ORDC adders stacked on top of energy prices
- DA prices, set the prior morning, often do not fully anticipate the severity of evening scarcity
- Result: RT > DA in scarcity hours → spread = DA − RT negative → INC (which needs DA > RT) LOSES

**Pattern**: ECRS clearing in HE21-24 is a contra-indicator for INC positions in those hours. The model correctly had ECRS data available (ECRS HE22: $2.27/MWh in the history file structural context), but the HE21 ECRS figure was not treated as a red flag for INC.

**New rule candidate**: When ECRS DA price for HEX > $0 (i.e., ECRS clears in the DA market at any price), flag INC positions at HEX as HIGH risk. If ECRS DA > $1.50/MWh, consider mandatory SKIP for INC regardless of Smartbidder P(win).

## 5. Learnings

### Learning 1 — ECRS DA clearing is a RT-spike precursor for INC positions (HIGH priority)
When ECRS clears in the DA market for a given hour, it signals ERCOT is purchasing emergency reserves ahead of a period it considers at risk. This correlates with RT scarcity premiums that push RT above DA. INC positions in ECRS-clearing hours face structural headwind. Add ECRS DA clearing price as an explicit modifier:
- ECRS DA > $0: flag HE as ELEVATED INC risk, reduce to 12.5 MW or skip
- ECRS DA > $1.50: mandatory skip for INC (same logic as Non-Spin > $10 for W23 dual-direction rule)

### Learning 2 — Sunday thin-DAM does not suppress RT; it can suppress DA
The prior thesis on Sunday was "thin-DAM sets DA conservatively → RT spikes → INC wins." The 2026-06-14 result shows the opposite pattern: thin-DAM set DA at $37.04 for HE21 while RT ran to $60.64. Sunday DA was LOW (not high), and RT exceeded it. This is consistent with the INC logic (DA > RT needed for INC to win), but here Sunday DA was too LOW to exceed what RT actually did. The thin-DAM environment on Sunday is double-edged: DA can be either over-priced (prior thesis) or under-priced relative to actual RT scarcity.

**Revised Sunday INC thesis**: On Sundays, check whether the Sunday DA is setting ABOVE or BELOW neighboring weekday levels. If Sunday DA HE21 < $45 (weekday baseline), and ECRS is clearing, the RT-spike risk is HIGH because DA was set too low to absorb the scarcity premium. In this case, DEC (Long DA/Short RT) is the wrong direction too — both INC and DEC are low-conviction when DA is underpriced and RT is volatile.

**Implication**: When Sunday DA at peak hours is unusually LOW (< 80% of prior weekday peak DA), and any scarcity product (ECRS or Non-Spin > $8) is clearing, the directional signal from Smartbidder P(win) is unreliable. Consider SKIP for all directions.

### Learning 3 — Adjacent hour scarcity escalation: treat HE21 and HE22 as a block, not independently
The W23 dual-direction scarcity rule skipped HE22 due to Non-Spin $13.63 + NL ramp > 8,000 MW. These same conditions were building at HE21 (Non-Spin $6.89 — below threshold, but ECRS clearing already). The model should treat evening scarcity as a block phenomenon: when HE22 triggers a scarcity skip, the hours immediately preceding (HE20-21) should be re-evaluated with elevated caution for INC positions. Proposed: when HE(X+1) triggers W23 dual-direction skip, reduce INC size at HE(X) by 50% and apply a spread floor uplift of $2 (i.e., require $6 minimum instead of $4).

### Learning 4 — HE22 SKIP confirmed correct: RT $52.09 > DA (INC would have lost)
Despite HE22 having P(win) = 0.650 (highest of the day), the mandatory skip was vindicated: RT ($52.09) exceeded DA ($35.81), so INC would have been a loss. The W23 rule is functioning correctly and its parameters (Non-Spin > $10 AND NL ramp > 8,000 MW) are well-calibrated for this market regime. Do not relax the HE22 skip rule.
