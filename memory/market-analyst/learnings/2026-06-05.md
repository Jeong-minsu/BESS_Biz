## 3. What the Briefing Missed or Got Wrong

### (a) RT price peak was HE17 CT, not HE21 CT — 4-hour forecast error on peak timing

The briefing placed the price-critical window at HE20-22 (DA peak $47-49/MWh). GKS's largest RT revenue hour was HE17 CT (UTC 21:00-22:00, $2,180.51) — the early evening ramp onset, not the late-evening scarcity window. This is consistent with RT occasionally spiking earlier than DA prices imply when the net-load ramp catches SCED short before AS products fully activate. The HE21 DA concentration is a useful structural signal, but RT events often materialize 2-4 hours ahead of the DA peak hour, especially on days with steep ramps.

**Rule update**: Add a flag when HE18-19 net-load ramp rate exceeds 2,000 MW/hour — these hours may see early RT spikes that precede the DA price peak. GKS's actual dispatch confirms the optimizer responded to RT in the field correctly, but the market briefing should warn downstream agents about this HE17-19 early-spike pattern.

The briefing cited RRS at $2.83-2.91/MWh in HE21-23, suggesting moderate value. DA_RRS_Amt = 0 for all 24 hours. RRS is apparently not clearing reliably in the June DAM environment. This extends the ECRS non-clearing pattern to include RRS. **Rule update**: Treat both ECRS and RRS DA forecasts above $1/MWh as "medium clearance probability" (not high). Only Non-Spin should carry "high clearance probability" in the current June regime.
