## 4. Analytical Methodology — Improvement Notes

### 4.1 GR_WEST MINIMUM Trigger for SCI Risk (NEW — from 10/15 data)

The 10/15 GR_WEST HE14 minimum of 2,090 MW demonstrates that SCI binding risk arises from **low wind**, not just from high wind through constrained corridors. The prior GR_WEST trigger (10/09 learnings) only addressed the surge case. Proposed addition:

**New trigger**: "When any HE in GR_WEST STWPF drops below 3,000 MW → flag SCI binding risk for that HE window in the briefing." At 3,000 MW, the margin above the ~2,000 MW binding threshold provides 2-3 hours of warning for forecast revision before the HE arrives.

**Implementation**: Add a single-line check to the briefing production checklist: `if GR_WEST_min < 3000: flag SCI_BINDING_RISK in Key Drivers section`.

**Status**: NEWLY IDENTIFIED. Target for next briefing cycle.

### 4.2 Discharge Window Duration Narrows with Strong Wind Recovery

The 10/15 evening wind profile (HE18: 12,552 MW → HE20: 17,496 MW → HE24: 24,701 MW) means that the RT spike window is likely **HE18-19 only** before prices crash. On 10/14, the spike window was HE18-20 (3 hours) because wind recovered more slowly. 

**Implication**: When GR_WEST HE20-24 shows a strong surge (>3,000 MW/hour ramp), the discharge window should be shortened to HE18-19. Conversely, when wind is flat through HE20-24, extend the discharge window to HE20-21.

**Status**: NEW OBSERVATION. Partially related to 10/09 learnings' spike window shift to HE20-22 recommendation — the correct window is now understood to be dynamic, dependent on wind recovery shape.

### 4.3 HE20-22 Spike Window Recommendation (from 10/09 learnings) — Status Update

The 10/09 learnings recommended shifting the spike window to HE20-22. The 10/15 data shows HE20 net load DECLINING (51,438 vs HE19 52,856 MW) due to rapid wind recovery. This is the OPPOSITE of the HE20-22 pattern assumed in the 10/09 recommendation, which was based on extreme duck curve conditions.

**Revised understanding**: The HE20-22 spike window applies when wind remains weak through the evening (low-wind-recovery regime). When wind surges after sunset (as in 10/15), the spike window reverts to HE18-19. This is a **regime-conditional** spike window, not a fixed shift.

**Status**: 10/09 recommendation to shift to HE20-22 is conditionally valid (low wind recovery days only). For 10/15-type strong wind recovery days, HE18-19 is the correct window.

### 4.4 Load Drop −4,128 MW and Front Passage Signature

The combination of (a) load drop > 4 GW and (b) GR_WEST HE24 > 15,000 MW on the same day suggests a cold front passage. This is a recognizable signature:
- Front passage: temperature drop → load decline; leading edge of front: strong west Texas wind → GR_WEST surge in late HE
- Pattern can be used as a **front passage flag**: `if (load_delta < -3000 MW) AND (GR_WEST_HE22_to_24 > 15000 MW): flag_cold_front_passage = True`

When front_flag = True, the overnight (HE21-24) net load collapse is likely steeper and the following day (10/16) will likely show continued high wind and potentially lower load.

**Status**: NEW HEURISTIC. Add to briefing Key Drivers identification logic.
