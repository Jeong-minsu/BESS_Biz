## Qualitative Review

### What I got right (directional)

1. **Wind anticyclone deepening trend was correctly flagged**: On Sep 1, I noted "three consecutive days of declining ERCOT wind" and warned Sep 3 could face compounding scarcity risk. This proved prescient — Sep 3 BidClose shows GR_WEST floor at 443 MW (a further 664 MW deterioration from Sep 2's 1,108 MW). The flag to watch the anticyclone deepening was the highest-value call.

2. **HE19–20 cliff risk was the dominant risk flag**: My HIGH-risk rating on the HE19–20 window was correct in principle for the heat wave period. The Aug 31 learnings confirmed extreme RT pricing materialized on Aug 31 (RTSPP avg $867.99); the pattern suggests HE19–20 is the right risk window to flag every heat wave day. However, the Sep 3 data now shows the HE20 cliff may be less severe on Sep 3 (net load 62,203 vs 64,215 MW; better wind buffer) — so the exact hour of peak stress is migrating day-to-day and I should not anchor too heavily on HE20 as the single worst hour.

3. **Elevated thermal outage (~9,000+ MW) was correctly identified as a persistent concern**: Cap out remains ~9,071 MW avg on Sep 3, essentially unchanged from the Sep 2 pattern. The concern is structural and ongoing, not a one-day anomaly.

4. **GR_COASTAL sea breeze as a key positive differentiator was correctly identified**: On Sep 2, I flagged GR_COASTAL as the "primary positive differentiator" and correctly identified HE19 = 3,735 MW. On Sep 3, GR_COASTAL peaks 1 hour earlier (HE18 = 3,670 MW) and provides afternoon shoulder support — a related but slightly different pattern. The structural relevance of GR_COASTAL during the anticyclone suppression period is a validated insight to carry forward.

### What may need refinement

1. **GR_WEST floor prediction was directionally correct but insufficient in magnitude**: I noted the "wind trend: three consecutive days of declining" risk but did not explicitly project a possible sub-500 MW GR_WEST floor. Given the anticyclone was already at record-low (1,108 MW on Sep 2), the Sep 3 BidClose of 443 MW shows the anticyclone effect can worsen further than I anchored on. Going forward: when GR_WEST floor is already in the 1,000–1,500 MW range for 2+ consecutive days, I should explicitly consider a sub-500 MW floor scenario in the Risks table.

2. **The intra-day price shape was likely too HE20-focused**: In my Sep 2 briefing, the "top 2 hours" estimate was HE19–20 (cliff window). On Sep 3, the model structure strongly suggests HE15–16 (shoulder, GR_WEST floor) will be the most expensive non-cliff hours. When GR_WEST approaches its absolute floor during the afternoon shoulder (not just midday lull), the shoulder hours can rival or exceed cliff hours in DA pricing. I should add a shoulder-vs-cliff comparison check to the daily price view process.

3. **Wind trend extrapolation — I understated the speed of deterioration**: My Sep 1 risk flag said "if the anticyclone continues to intensify, Sep 3 wind could fall further." The actual Sep 3 BidClose confirms this at an even more extreme level than a conservative extrapolation would suggest. For persistent anticyclone patterns (5+ days), I should apply a steeper deterioration multiplier to GR_WEST afternoon floor projections in the risk table.

4. **Smartbidder absence now spans multiple consecutive cycles — the qualitative price estimate error band is widening**: Without AS spread or P(DA>RT) data for multiple cycles, my price views lack systematic RT anchoring. As the absence extends, the uncertainty band on my price estimates grows (especially for HE14–16 shoulder pricing where RT spreads matter most). I should flag explicitly in each briefing how many cycles without Smartbidder and note that the price view confidence degrades proportionally.
