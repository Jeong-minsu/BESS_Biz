## 3. Enverus vs Yes Energy Divergence — Pattern Learning

This cycle provides the third consecutive confirmed data point on the solar divergence pattern:

| Cycle | Yes Energy COPHSL Peak | Enverus STPF Peak | Gap | Gap % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-06-08 | ~25,800 MW | ~28,000 MW (estimated from congestion notes) | +2,200 MW | +8.5% |
| 2026-06-10 | 26,229 MW (HE13) | 28,103 MW (HE14) | +1,874 MW | +7.1% |
| 2026-06-11 (D+1 fresh) | 25,684 MW (HE14) | 28,744 MW (HE14) | +3,060 MW | +11.9% |

Pattern: Enverus STPF is systematically higher than Yes Energy COPHSL on the solar peak, with the gap ranging +1,874 to +3,060 MW over three consecutive June cycle days. The gap is not random — Enverus STPF appears to represent a "clear-sky STPF" that includes capacity not typically committed in the COPHSL operating dispatch. Yes Energy COPHSL tracks actual committed HSL, which is constrained by ramp rates, local transmission limits, and curtailment decisions.

**Implication for briefing accuracy:** When the briefing writes the midday net-load trough, using Yes Energy COPHSL produces a conservative (higher) NL trough. If Enverus is the better forecast of actual solar output (not committed HSL), the actual midday NL trough on 2026-06-10 may have been 1,874-2,508 MW lower than the Yes Energy base case — approximately 13,843-14,477 MW at HE10 vs the briefed 16,351 MW. A lower NL trough deepens the midday overgeneration event and may push midday RT prices lower than Smartbidder's $12-15/MWh floor. The briefing noted this risk ("Smartbidder's $15.36 floor may be optimistic if dispatch models are not capturing full solar saturation") — this risk framing was appropriate but the Enverus-adjusted NL trough (~14-14.5 GW) should have been stated as the explicit alternative scenario alongside the Yes Energy base (~16.4 GW).

**Carry-forward rule:** When writing the midday net-load trough section, always state both the Yes Energy base case NL trough AND the Enverus-adjusted NL trough (YE NL minus the Enverus-YE solar gap). Label these as "Base (Yes Energy)" and "Solar Upside (Enverus-adjusted)" scenarios. This directly informs the charge-window price floor assumption and the midday DART alpha assessment.
