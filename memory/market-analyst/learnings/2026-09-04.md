## Key Misses

### Miss 1 — Solar estimate too conservative (SIGNIFICANT)

**Predicted**: 27,000-27,500 MW peak (HE13)
**Inferred actual**: Sep 6 solar peak = 31,914 MW (HE12). Sep 5 astronomical conditions nearly identical (<0.5% change). With clear-sky conditions typical of Texas heat waves, Sep 5 peak was likely 29,000-32,000 MW.

**Magnitude of error**: -2,000 to -4,500 MW on peak solar
**Root cause**: The Sep 4 briefing anchor was Enverus Sep 4 forecast (peak ~27,580 MW). The heat wave clear-sky pattern means actual solar output consistently exceeds climatological September averages. I applied a "no change" adjustment and used the Enverus figure as-is, instead of recognizing that actual ERCOT solar output during heat waves runs 10-15% above September baseline forecasts (cloud cover suppressed throughout heat event).

**Impact**: Lower solar estimate → less deep duck curve predicted → less negative price risk predicted at HE10-11 → net load trough was underestimated. This partially offset the underestimate of evening net load.

### Miss 2 — Wind estimate significantly too high (SIGNIFICANT)

**Predicted**: 12,000-14,500 MW 24h avg (central ~13,000 MW, holding Sep 4 Enverus 13,943 MW)
**Inferred actual**: Sep 6 wind avg = 7,954 MW. If Sep 5 was in the same atmospheric regime (Labor Day weekend anticyclone), Sep 5 wind avg was likely 8,000-12,000 MW, not 12,000-14,500 MW.

**Magnitude of error**: Likely +2,000-5,000 MW overestimate on 24h wind average
**Root cause**: Held the Sep 4 Enverus wind figure (13,943 MW) as the central case with a wide band. The Sep 4 Enverus figure reflected a daytime recovery that may not have materialized — the Sep 3 learnings already identified a systematic anticyclone deepening risk. The Sep 4 briefing was generated before Sep 3 learnings were written (sequencing lag), but more importantly, the Sep 5 forecast was generated without any new wind data and simply carried forward Sep 4 as the baseline.

**Impact**: Wind overestimate → net load evening peak (HE20) underestimated → DA price estimate for HE20 likely too low. If actual wind was 8-10 GW instead of 14-17 GW at HE20, the net load at HE20 was 5-7 GW higher than predicted, which would shift the DA price at HE20 well into the upper end of or beyond the $85-140/MWh range.

**Lesson** (new, not in prior reviews): During multi-day heat wave anticyclone conditions, the wind floor should be treated as having a LEFT-SKEWED distribution — there is more probability mass below the mean than above, because blocking patterns tend to intensify over weekends when sea-land temperature differentials are at peak. The baseline should not be the most recent Enverus forecast from a prior day; it should be anchored closer to the lower quartile of recent BidClose observations.

### Miss 3 — Net load evening peak (HE20) underestimated

**Predicted**: 52,000-57,000 MW
**Inferred actual**: Likely 55,000-65,000 MW range, based on Sep 6 HE20 net load of 64,987 MW at higher load (82,779 MW) and similar wind weakness. Sep 5 had lower load (~74-76 GW peak) but wind was also likely weaker than assumed.

**Root cause**: Compounding of Miss 1 (load was correct) and Miss 2 (wind was too high). If wind is 5 GW lower than estimated at HE20, net load peak is 5 GW higher. Upper end of predicted range (57,000 MW) was probably violated.

## Most Significant Structural Finding

**DEGRADED cycle wind assumption is the primary failure mode**

In a degraded cycle (no new production data), the load estimate can be reasonably derived from the prior weekday with a seasonal weekend adjustment. However, wind in Texas heat waves is highly path-dependent: the anticyclone pattern determines whether daytime wind floor is 1,000 MW or 5,000 MW, and this cannot be inferred from the prior day's forecast without new BidClose or synoptic data.

The Sep 4 Enverus wind (13,943 MW) reflected a relatively favorable overnight wind pattern. When no new data is available, the correct assumption in a multi-day heat wave is NOT to carry forward the most recent figure — it is to apply a downward bias toward the recent BidClose minimum (which on Sep 3 was the GR_WEST floor of 443 MW with total ERCOT wind depressed). The appropriate range for Sep 5 wind, given the Sep 4 history, should have been 7,000-13,000 MW, not 12,000-14,500 MW.

**Process fix**: In any DEGRADED cycle during an active heat wave, apply a default -20 to -30% haircut to the prior Enverus wind figure for the daytime window (HE8-17). This should become the new low-end anchor, not the optimistic scenario.

## Actions for Next Cycle (Sep 6 and Beyond)

1. **Heat wave DEGRADED wind protocol**: When generating a DEGRADED cycle forecast during an active heat wave, use the formula:
   - Wind central case = MIN(prior Enverus avg, lowest recent BidClose COPHSL avg) * 0.85
   - Wind low case = recent GR_WEST daytime floor * 8 (ERCOT regional ratio proxy)
   - Do NOT anchor the central case at the most recent Enverus figure without this haircut.

2. **Solar upward bias correction in heat waves**: Texas clear-sky conditions during heat waves systematically produce solar output 10-15% above September seasonal baselines. When Enverus solar is used as the anchor, add +5 to +10% to the peak estimate. Target for Sep 5 should have been 28,500-30,000 MW, not 27,000-27,500 MW.

3. **Net load evening peak sensitivity check**: Always compute the HE20 net load under a "wind bear case" (wind at GR_WEST floor * regional ratio). If the bear case net load exceeds 60,000 MW, flag HE20 as a scarcity risk hour regardless of the base case.
