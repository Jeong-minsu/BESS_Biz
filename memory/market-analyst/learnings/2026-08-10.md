## 1. What the 08/10 Briefing Got Right (Directional Validation)

### [A] Thermal Dome Retreat Call — CORRECT (Bull scenario materialized)

The 08/10 briefing presented three scenarios:
- Bear: GR_WEST daytime trough ~3,156 MW → net load HE21 ~61,284 MW
- Base: GR_WEST daytime trough 5,156 MW → net load HE21 59,284 MW
- Bull: GR_WEST daytime trough ~7,156 MW → net load HE21 ~57,284 MW

The 08/11 STWPF shows GR_WEST daytime trough at 9,419-9,421 MW — approximately **+2.3 GW above the Bull scenario target of ~7,156 MW**. The dome's collapse was faster and more complete than even the Bull scenario predicted for 08/10. However, the "Bull scenario" framing was correct in direction: by acknowledging the possibility of GR_WEST > 6,000 MW and associating it with lower evening spike risk, the briefing positioned the market directionally correctly.

**Implication for 08/11 forecast**: The 08/10 briefing's Bull scenario is now the baseline for 08/11. The price view ($20-40/MWh at HE21 on 08/11 vs 08/10's Bear case of $55-90/MWh) reflects this scenario materialization.

### [B] Cap-Out Normalization at HE24 — CORRECT

The 08/10 briefing explicitly flagged: "HE24 = 6,991 MW cap-out — mass unit return, constructive for 08/11 reserve margin. Monitor unit return status for 08/11 briefing."

The 08/11 STWPF shows avg cap-out = 7,314 MW vs 08/10's 9,037 MW — a -1,723 MW improvement, exactly consistent with the mass return predicted from the HE24 step-down. The 08/10 briefing's cap-out watch item was validated in the very next day's data.

**Pattern to embed**: The HE24 cap-out step-down (when it appears in the daily data) is a reliable leading indicator for the next-day cap-out average. Two consecutive days (08/10 HE24 = 6,991 MW, 08/11 HE24 = 6,827 MW) both preceded the following day's cap-out averaging near the HE24 level. This pattern should be explicitly cited as a next-day cap-out estimator in future briefings.

The 08/10 briefing noted GR_COASTAL as a positive structural development (event record 3,594 MW at HE19). The 08/11 STWPF shows 3,668 MW at HE18 — a new event record, +74 MW above 08/10's record. The coastal sea breeze strengthening trend is real and persistent; the 08/10 briefing identified it correctly and it continued to strengthen. ### [D] Duck Floor as Deepening Structural Risk — PARTIALLY FORESHADOWED

The 08/10 briefing cited "negative RT briefly at HE10 if solar overperforms" as a non-base-case possibility at 26,477 MW net load. The 08/11 net load at HE10 = 19,981 MW makes negative prices not a tail scenario but a base case. ## 2. What the 08/10 Briefing Missed or Underestimated

### [MISS-1] Magnitude of Duck Floor Deepening — SIGNIFICANT UNDERESTIMATE

The 08/10 briefing did not produce a quantitative forecast for 08/11's duck floor. But its structural framing implied the floor would deepen modestly with continued dome retreat. The actual magnitude (-6,496 MW deeper, from 26,477 to 19,981 MW) exceeds any reasonable extrapolation from the 08/10 analysis:
- 08/10 to 08/11 GR_WEST wind improvement: +4,263 MW at HE14-15 daytime trough
- Wind improvement at HE10 (duck floor hour): +6,893 MW (11,997 vs 08/10's 5,104 MW — the GR_WEST afternoon trough is not the floor hour; the duck floor at HE10 benefits from morning-level GR_WEST winds before the thermal diurnal effect sets in)
- Solar at HE10: nearly identical (27,027 vs 27,663 MW)

The dominant driver of the 08/11 duck floor deepening is the sustained high overnight-into-morning GR_WEST levels (11,176-11,997 MW at HE09-10 vs 08/10's 8,441-7,908 MW). The 08/10 briefing focused its GR_WEST scenario analysis on the afternoon trough (HE13-15), but the duck floor hour (HE10) benefits more from the morning-level GR_WEST, which is harder to predict directly from the STWPF daytime trough metric alone.

**Improvement action**: Add a "HE10 GR_WEST" reading (separate from the "daytime trough" which occurs at HE14-15) to the wind analysis section. The morning-level GR_WEST at HE09-11 is the dominant wind contributor to the duck floor, not the afternoon trough.

### [MISS-2] Net Load Ramp Magnitude — CONTINUING UNDERESTIMATION

The 08/10 briefing flagged the HE19→HE20 ramp as "+10,159 MW, new event extreme." The 08/11 data shows +11,309 MW — +1,150 MW steeper, another new event extreme. The solar cliff at HE19→HE20 continues to deepen (-13,469 MW on 08/11 vs -13,103 MW on 08/10, -366 MW more). This pattern (ramp becoming slightly steeper each successive day) is structurally driven by August solar trajectory — as the season progresses past the summer solstice, the solar exit time can shift slightly earlier, steepening the cliff for a given forecast date.

**Pattern to monitor**: The solar cliff at HE19→HE20 will likely remain at the -13,000 to -14,000 MW level for the rest of August. This should be treated as a structural 08/11-onward constant, not a one-day anomaly. The GR_COASTAL sea breeze (3,500-3,700 MW at HE17-19) partially buffers the cliff but cannot offset a -13,469 MW solar drop.

### [MISS-3] GR_NORTH Recovery Rate — CORRECTLY ANTICIPATED BUT STILL PENDING

The 08/10 briefing correctly forecast that GR_NORTH recovery would lag GR_WEST by "one to two days." On 08/11, GR_NORTH is still only 1,033-2,404 MW — improving from 08/10's 443-2,166 MW but far below normal levels. The lag is real and the 08/10 prediction was directionally right. The 08/11 briefing maintains this watch item for 08/12.

## 3. Methodology Gaps Identified

### [GAP-1] Duck Floor Hour Analysis (HE09-12) vs Afternoon Trough Analysis (HE14-15)

The 08/10 briefing (and prior briefings) structured wind analysis around the "daytime trough" at HE13-15 (afternoon, when thermally-driven diurnal wind suppression peaks). However, the duck floor in ERCOT occurs at HE09-12 (when solar peaks), not at HE14-15. The wind level at the duck floor hour (HE09-12) is the GR_WEST morning profile, not the afternoon trough.

**Example (08/11)**:
- GR_WEST HE10 (duck floor): 11,997 MW (morning-level, high)
- GR_WEST HE14-15 (afternoon trough): 9,419-9,421 MW (diurnal minimum)

The difference (11,997 vs 9,421 MW = 2,576 MW) is not captured if analysis focuses only on the afternoon trough. A briefing that correctly predicts "GR_WEST daytime trough 9,419 MW" may still underestimate the duck floor depth by ~2,576 MW of wind supply at the actual floor hour.

**Recommendation**: Add a dedicated "Duck floor wind snapshot" row to the wind analysis: "GR_WEST at HE09, HE10 (duck floor hour), HE11" — separate from the "GR_WEST daytime trough" row. These two metrics serve different analytical purposes.

### [GAP-2] Negative Price Event Protocol — Not Previously Established

The 08/10 briefing mentioned negative prices as a non-base-case tail scenario. The 08/11 data produces the first likely negative-price day of the heat event series. The existing briefing template (and BESS operating view) does not have an established protocol for negative-price charging windows. Specifically:
- Charge bid-price strategy at negative prices differs from neutral/positive price charging
- AS participation dynamics at the duck floor with negative energy prices differ from normal
- "Best charge window: HE09-12" analysis did not previously distinguish between positive-price charging (where you minimize cost) and negative-price charging (where you maximize revenue)

**Recommendation**: Add a "Negative Price Protocol" sub-section to the BESS Operating View when duck floor net load < 22,000 MW (roughly the negative-price threshold based on 08/04 anchor extrapolation). Specify: bid charge prices at the most negative feasible value; coordinate AS participation at HE09-12; target maximizing clearing rate over bid precision.

### [GAP-3] Solar Cliff -dX/dt Tracking

The briefing currently reports the HE19→HE20 solar cliff as a single MW value (-13,103 MW on 08/10, -13,469 MW on 08/11). Adding the day-over-day delta of the solar cliff (-366 MW steeper) and a rolling 3-day trend would allow earlier identification of whether the cliff is structurally widening (August seasonal effect) or stabilizing. A widening cliff increases the probability of RT > DA at HE20 and should be flagged explicitly.

### [GAP-4] Price Anchor Staleness Correction Not Applied to 08/10 Briefing

The 08/08 learning file (written retroactively on 08/09) recommended: "08/10 브리핑부터 08/08 P&L을 신규 앵커로 사용." However, the 08/10 briefing still used the 08/04 anchor primarily (with 08/08 data noted but not integrated into price estimates). This is because the 08/08 learning was an ex-post retroactive document written concurrently with the 08/10 briefing. The 08/11 briefing uses both 08/04 and 08/08 anchors explicitly.

**Going forward**: The 08/08 P&L anchor (DA avg $27.45/MWh; HE10 DA $18.98; HE21 DA $36.82; HE22 DA $37.39) is now the primary structural reference for the current market environment. The 08/04 anchor remains useful for net-load-to-price interpolation at low net-load levels (HE10 at 23,639 MW is closer to 08/11's 19,981 MW than any other available reference point). Both anchors should be cited in each briefing when applicable.
