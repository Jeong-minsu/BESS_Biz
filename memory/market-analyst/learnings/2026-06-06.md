## 3. What Went Wrong

**Major miss — DA-RT direction at HE19-21 (the most consequential window)**

The briefing forecast P(DA>RT) = 63-67% for HE19-21, implying DA prices would exceed RT in the solar-exit window. The actual result was the opposite: RT dominated DA at HE20-23 (GKS collected +$1,081 at HE20, +$1,570 at HE22, +$2,533 at HE23 from RT energy imbalance settlements). This means RT prices were substantially above the DA settlement that BESS was purchasing at — i.e., the spread was negative (DA cheaper than RT) in the evening hours, not positive as forecast.

Root cause (inferred): Saturdays appear to exhibit RT-over-DA dynamics at the solar-exit hour even more strongly than Fridays. The 2026-06-05 self-review identified the same pattern on Friday (RT = $68-72/MWh vs DA = $36-41/MWh at HE17-18). The briefing correctly observed this on adjacent Friday actuals but still forecast P(DA>RT) for Saturday's HE19-21 — relying on Smartbidder's probability output rather than the observed adjacent-day pattern. Smartbidder's P(DA>RT) = 63-67% at HE19-21 was directionally wrong for this weekend.

**Midday RT crash (HE12-13) not anticipated**

GKS RT energy was -$1,180 at HE12 and -$1,300 at HE13 — the two worst loss hours of the day. The briefing discussed HE11-13 as the solar maximum and minimum net-load window, noting prices would be low ($24-26/MWh range), but did not anticipate RT prices going deeply negative or strongly below DA in those hours. The briefing's midday discussion was focused on net-load and price levels, not on the risk of midday RT crash (negative prices or near-zero from solar oversupply).

A BESS charging at DA purchase price at HE12-13 would have been hurt if RT prices crashed below that DA purchase cost — which is what the negative RT energy settlements reflect. This was not flagged as a risk.

**Revenue peak shifted to HE22-23, not HE19-21**

The briefing placed the BESS discharge peak at HE19-21 (solar-exit window, DA $41-45/MWh). Actual revenue peak was HE22-23 (RT energy +$1,570 and +$2,533). This is a 2-3 hour later peak than forecast, consistent with RT remaining elevated even as DA had declined from its HE20-21 peak. The price forecast got the right general direction (evening = high) but missed the hour by 2-3 positions.
