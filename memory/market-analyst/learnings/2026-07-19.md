## 3. Root Cause Analysis: Why Was DA So Much Lower Than Forecast?

The central puzzle: actual RTSPP in HE21-22 ($32-34) aligned well with the Smartbidder RT base forecast, but DASPP in HE17-21 ($14-17) was $15-20 below the Smartbidder DA forecast ($28-34). This asymmetry points to a **DA-side supply surplus that did not manifest in RT**.

**Most likely explanations (in order of confidence):**

1. **DA market incorporated higher wind than Yes Energy COPHSL** — The three-way wind forecast divergence (YE 9,902 MW vs AG2 12,471 MW vs Enverus 11,202 MW) means DA auction participants bid assuming more wind supply. If market participants used 12-15 GW average wind (closer to AG2/Enverus), every 3 GW of additional wind at near-$0 offer price could suppress DA evening clearing prices by $10-20/MWh on a tight-reserve summer day. Smartbidder likely used a wind input closer to YE COPHSL, underestimating supply in the DA auction.

2. **BESS operators bid as DA virtual buyers in evening hours** — If significant storage capacity bid as virtual buyers (load in DA) during HE17-21 in anticipation of RT premium, this would add load in the DA and reduce the apparent clearing price for generation. This is exactly the DART virtual strategy GKS executed (buying HE04-06, HE24), but others may have done it on the evening hours too.

3. **Sunday load underperformance vs YE 82 GW forecast** — Sunday demand patterns can run softer than weekday equivalents even in heatwave conditions. If actual Sunday load was closer to 78-80 GW rather than 82 GW, the ~2-4 GW demand miss would compound the wind supply surplus, pushing evening DA prices down further.

4. **Solar overperformance lingering into HE18-19** — The YE model showed solar fading from HE17 onward, but actual clear-sky conditions on July 19 (Sunday, low cloud cover) may have sustained 10-15 GW of solar output through HE18, suppressing net load in the pre-peak transition window.

**Bottom line**: The Smartbidder model's DA forecast is most reliable when its underlying supply/demand assumptions (especially wind and storage behavior) align with actual DA auction inputs. On days with large wind forecast divergence (>2 GW source gap), Smartbidder DA prices are likely to overestimate if the market bids on the high-wind scenario.

Use AG2/Enverus as the DA-relevant supply reference, not YE COPHSL.

## 9. Summary (4-line)

The DA forecast for 2026-07-19 was deeply wrong in evening peak hours (HE20-21 overforecast by $12-19/MWh, directional error on DA-RT spread) because the Smartbidder DA model used Yes Energy's conservative wind forecast (9.9 GW) while DA market participants appear to have bid on AG2/Enverus's higher wind scenario (12-15 GW), suppressing evening DA clearing prices to $14-17/MWh. The RT forecast was accurate for key discharge hours (HE21 $32.72 actual vs $32.02 forecast, $0.70 error). Non-Spin was approximately right for HE22 (+10%) but significantly underforecast for HE23 ($11.00 actual vs $6.44 forecast, +71%). Key protocol change: when AG2 wind > YE COPHSL by 2+ GW, apply a $5-15/MWh downward DA adjustment for evening hours, treat Smartbidder DA premium signals as unreliable, and assume RT premium (negative spread) is more likely.
