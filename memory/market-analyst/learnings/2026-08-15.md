## 3. Root Cause Analysis — Compounding Failures and Escalating Gaps

### [RCA-1] Dead-Calm Abrupt vs Gradual Collapse (Now Fully Confirmed)

As documented in the 08/14 self-review ([RCA-1]), the wind collapse was abrupt rather than gradual. The 08/15 self-review adds: the collapse did NOT plateau after hitting 3,826 MW on 08/16. It has continued to 1,325 MW on 08/17. This is now the sixth consecutive day of decline, with each day exceeding the prior day's severity. The "3-7 day persistence" estimate from the 08/16 briefing appears optimistic at 3 days — we are at Day 6 with no recovery signal in the 08/17 BIDCLOSE (HE24 = 6,330 MW is not a wind recovery signal; GR_WEST HE24 = 8,410 MW on 08/17 shows diurnal recovery but not synoptic recovery).

**Implication**: The dead-calm anticyclone appears to be a blocking ridge of the type that can persist 7-14 days or more. Without AG2 NWP data, the synoptic scale is entirely opaque. The 3-7 day estimate in the 08/16 briefing was based on August climatology (typical anticyclone lifetimes in Texas), not model-specific data.

### [RCA-2] Monday WDPEAK Demand Amplifier

The 08/16 briefing was written for Sunday WEPEAK. It did not directly address the Monday demand increment. The Monday load avg (74,917 MW) is 2,654 MW above Sunday's, adding directly to net load already at a series record. The compounding of Monday demand return + continued wind dead-calm has pushed HE21 net load to 74,512 MW, well above any calibrated reference. This interaction was flagged in general ("Monday will add load") but not quantified in the 08/16 briefing.

### [RCA-3] Thermal Non-Response to Price Signals

For the third consecutive production briefing, the generator fleet has not responded to rising price expectations by bringing outaged capacity back online. The 08/17 cap-out (8,153 MW avg) is essentially unchanged from 08/16 (8,157 MW) despite the expectation that extreme Monday WDPEAK prices would motivate returns. The only evidence of price-responsive return is the HE07 step-down (-420 MW), which is immaterial relative to the total outage. The fleet appears to be in a maintenance cycle where the fixed costs of plant restart and inspection completion are preventing price-responsive dispatch. The critical open question (unanswerable without P&L) is: at what DA clearing price does the thermal fleet begin returning in volume?

1. **Dead-calm anticyclone has now persisted 6 days (08/12–08/17) with no synoptic-level recovery signal**: The "3-7 day persistence" estimate may be underestimating the blocking ridge duration. Without AG2 NWP data, every additional day of dead-calm is a complete surprise. Add "Day 7+ wind stagnation" to the bear scenario set for any briefing that shows GR_WEST below 5,000 MW.

2. **HE24 cap-out predictor has failed 7 consecutive times; the failure magnitude is INCREASING**: Failures have grown from ~700 MW (08/10) to +2,187 MW (08/16→08/17 overnight). The predictor is not merely noisy — it is systematically directionally wrong. REMOVE this predictor from the briefing's predictive framework. Instead: when HE24 < 7,000 MW, note the reading as a lower bound reference but explicitly state "predictor has 0-for-7 track record in the post-dome period; treat as unvalidated."

3. **Monday WDPEAK demand amplifier must be explicitly quantified in Sunday briefing**: The 08/16 (Sunday) briefing for Monday 08/17 should have included an explicit "+2,500-3,000 MW avg weekday increment" note and estimated Monday's HE21 net load at ~74,000-76,000 MW based on Sunday's 67,918 MW + weekday increment. This would have raised the alarm about Monday's unanchored pricing territory.

4. **Evening GR_WEST at HE21 continues to decline (08/16: 6,902 MW → 08/17: 4,803 MW)**: Per the 08/14 learning, dead-calm conditions suppress evening wind recovery. The 08/17 HE21 GR_WEST at 4,803 MW is 2,099 MW below Sunday's 6,902 MW. The 5-9 GW evening range identified in 08/14 learnings ([GAP-B]) was too optimistic — actual is now 4-7 GW range. Update the dead-calm evening GR_WEST assumption to 3,000-7,000 MW range when daytime trough is sub-2,000 MW.

5. **All-day price floor elevated — "cheap charging window" no longer exists in dead-calm anticyclone**: Monday's duck floor (HE10 = 34,323 MW) carries an estimated $20-32/MWh price floor. The concept of a $10-14/MWh charge window (used in 08/12 BESS optimization) does not apply in the current dead-calm regime. This has significant BESS dispatch implications: the gross spread may be widest of the series ($40-80/MWh), but the charge-side cost is also the highest, reducing round-trip efficiency.

6. **Smartbidder DEGRADED Day 22 — Opportunity cost is now maximum across the entire post-dome period**: DA price distribution on 08/17 (74,512 MW net load at HE21) has the widest confidence interval of the series. A Smartbidder forecast would narrow the $65-110+/MWh range to perhaps ±$15/MWh. Escalate Smartbidder restoration to emergency priority.
