## 2. NL Ramp Prediction Bias — Systematic Assessment

### Issue definition

The congestion framework uses Yes Energy NET_LOAD_FORECAST_BID_CLOSE as the primary NL ramp input. Two sources of bias have been identified across the tracked cycle history:

**Bias A — YE solar understatement inflates NL trough and ramp magnitudes.**
Yes Energy COPHSL is confirmed to understate actual solar generation by approximately 4,000–5,500 MW in summer peak months (documented across multiple cycles, confirmed as systematic in the 2026-06-10 forecast data quality note). This means:
- The YE-reported NL trough (e.g., 16,351 MW at HE10 for 2026-06-10) understates the depth of overgeneration — actual NL trough is likely 4,000–5,500 MW lower than YE reports.
- Conversely, the YE NL level at peak (HE18-22) is approximately accurate since solar is near zero then.
- The NL ramp HE17→HE21 magnitude (+17,524 MW as reported by YE for 2026-06-10) is compressed: the actual ramp from the true NL trough to the evening peak is larger than what YE reports, because the true midday NL is lower. This means the HE10-to-HE21 ramp is understated by roughly the same 4,000–5,500 MW solar understatement.

**Implication**: The framework uses NL ramp HE18→HE21 (not the full trough-to-peak ramp) as the HOUSTON_IMPORT calibration signal. From HE18 onward, solar is already declining steeply, so the YE vs actual solar gap is smaller at HE18 than at HE11. The HE18→HE21 ramp bias is estimated at 1,000–2,000 MW (the solar understatement at HE18 is ~21,694 MW YE vs ~24,000–26,500 MW Enverus-adjusted, a gap of 2,000–5,000 MW, partially closing by HE21 at 666 MW YE vs ~1,500 MW Enverus-adjusted). Net: YE HE18→HE21 ramp (+17,009 MW for 2026-06-10) understates the true ramp by roughly 1,000–3,000 MW. All NL ramp brackets used in HOUSTON_IMPORT calibration are therefore slightly conservative on the upside.

**Bias B — NL ramp brackets were calibrated against cycles with the same YE solar understatement, so the relative ordering is consistent but the absolute magnitudes are systematically compressed.**
Since every historical comparison cycle also used YE solar, the relative calibration (e.g., "2026-06-09 ramp was +22,931 MW, which is the cycle high") holds for inter-cycle ranking even if every absolute number is understated by the same solar bias. The calibration brackets are internally consistent but not calibrated against true grid NL. When Enverus solar STPF is eventually parsed and integrated, all NL ramp brackets will need upward revision of approximately 2,000–4,000 MW across each boundary.

**Bias C — Monday load upside risk was not captured in the ramp signal.**
The 2026-06-08 MISS (HIGH call, no realized constraint) was partly attributed to Monday load not materializing at forecast levels. The YE load forecast does not carry explicit day-of-week uncertainty bounds. On Monday high-load forecasts, a downside scenario of -2,000 to -4,000 MW load miss should be factored as a base case risk. This is a structural gap in the heuristic — the NL ramp signal at HE18→HE21 incorporates the YE load forecast at face value, but Monday load forecasts in summer carry higher downside uncertainty than mid-week days.

**Action for next cycles**: Tag Monday cycles with an explicit "load downside scenario" that discounts HOUSTON_IMPORT P(binding) by 3-5 percentage points from the heuristic output to reflect the historically higher load forecast miss rate on Monday return-to-work days.

## Key Learnings Summary

1. **HOUSTON_IMPORT HIGH call ceiling rule is not operationalized.** The 2026-06-08 learning established that HOUSTON_IMPORT should be capped at MEDIUM-HIGH (20-35%) absent Non-Spin DA confirmation. The 2026-06-09 forecast issued HIGH (28-45%). This is a process compliance miss — the rule existed but was not enforced because Non-Spin DA is absent from the pipeline. Resolution path: either (a) add a hard ceiling in the daily template to prevent HIGH calls without Non-Spin DA or (b) explicitly document the override rationale when the cap is exceeded.

2. **NL ramp calibration brackets are internally consistent but systematically understated** due to YE solar understatement (~4,000–5,500 MW). All brackets need upward revision when Enverus solar is confirmed parsed. The HE18→HE21 ramp brackets (the primary HOUSTON_IMPORT input) are understated by approximately 1,000–3,000 MW. This is a lower-priority correction than the midday SCI brackets (understated by ~4,000–5,500 MW).

3. **Monday load downside risk is not incorporated into NL ramp uncertainty bounds.** A Monday-specific downside scenario (-2,000 to -4,000 MW load) should be applied as a P10 scenario on Monday HOUSTON_IMPORT calls. This may have explained the 2026-06-08 miss.

4. **Enverus solar STPF parsing confirmation is the single highest-priority data quality improvement** for congestion forecast accuracy (higher priority than hub-pair LMP for the midday WEST_TO_NORTH constraint; comparable priority for the evening HOUSTON_IMPORT constraint). The YE COPHSL solar understatement is a known, quantified, and persistent source of SCI and NL ramp error across all 20+ tracked cycles.

5. **YE vs Enverus divergence should be flagged at the constraint table level**, not only in the data quality narrative. Downstream consumers cannot accurately assess probability range reliability without knowing whether the SCI estimate is on a YE-only basis (10-15% understated) or an Enverus-corrected basis.
