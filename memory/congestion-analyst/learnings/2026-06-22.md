## 5. Improvement Points

**Finding 1 (persists — 3rd cycle): Midday constraint MCC sign error at GKS**

For the third consecutive day (Jun 20, Jun 21, Jun 22), the HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY_LOCAL call produces a wrong MCC sign at GKS. The forecast assigns a positive MCC (generation premium) when the constraint binds. In practice, GKS in midday solar hours is operating as a load (DART charge), not as a dispatch generation unit. The actual MCC direction depends on GKS shift factor relative to the constraint — if GKS is downstream (load side) of the constraint, the MCC is negative for a load node even during binding. The heuristic incorrectly treats GKS as a generation-side node for all constraint scenarios.

**Action (immediate, Stage 0 patch)**: For HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY_LOCAL, flip the GKS MCC sign assumption. During solar-era midday, GKS is a net load node (charging) — MCC should be shown as negative (load penalty) or near zero, not positive. Remove the +$2 to +$6 GKS MCC estimate for this constraint until the PTDF sign for GKS relative to HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY_LOCAL is confirmed from network data.

**Finding 2 (persists — 3rd cycle): WEST_TO_NORTH onset 2-3 hours earlier than HE23-24 anchor**

The overnight West constraint onset is HE20-22 across Jun 20, Jun 21, Jun 22. The SCI-anchor (HE24 GR_WEST reading) consistently lags the actual onset. Despite the action item being identified on Jun 20 and reconfirmed on Jun 21, the forecast window was not updated for Jun 22 (still HE23-24). This failure to implement the correction costs meaningful precision on the 10-18% LOW call — the overnight stress is real and recurring, but the probability and window are not updated.

**Action (required before next cycle)**: Shift WEST_TO_NORTH forecast window to HE20-24 permanently for summer weekdays. Upgrade P(binding) from LOW 10-18% to LOW-MEDIUM 20-28% when GR_WEST HE20-21 forecast exceeds 12,000 MW.

**Finding 3 (new): HOUSTON_IMPORT timing bias — HE21-22 not HE19-20**

On Jun 22, the forecast window was HE19-22 with peak emphasis on HE19-20 (highest NL ramp hour). The actual stress materialized at HE21-22. The NL ramp rate peak (d(NL)/dt maximum) does not correspond to the maximum constraint binding probability — the constraint binds after the ramp has progressed and the system has absorbed load, typically 1-2 hours after peak ramp rate. For NL ramps centered on HE18-19, the binding window should be shifted to HE20-23.

**Action**: Shift HOUSTON_IMPORT binding window 2 hours forward from NL ramp peak. For Jun 22 ramp centered HE18-19, the forecast window should have been HE21-23, not HE19-21. Implement as a rule: HOUSTON_IMPORT binding window = [NL_ramp_peak_HE + 2, NL_ramp_peak_HE + 5].

**Finding 4 (new): P50 lambda anchor too low for evening/overnight stress hours**

Across Jun 21-22, when binding stress does materialize in evening/overnight, the implied RT prices are above P50 ($22-$37 realized vs $10-$22 forecast P50) but below P90. The P50 anchor appears to systematically underweight the realized lambda when the stress is genuine. This is a calibration issue in the heuristic range.

**Action (Stage 0 patch)**: For evening constraint scenarios where multiple stress triggers are simultaneously active (high NL ramp + low reserves + cap-out), revise P50 upward to $30-$40/MWh. The current $22 P50 for HOUSTON_IMPORT was calibrated on precedent days with single triggers only.
