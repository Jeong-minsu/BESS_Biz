## 5. Key Learning Items (preliminary, to be finalized post-settlement)

**L25 — HOUSTON_IMPORT uncertainty quantification when cross-source gap >5,000 MW**

When YE-Enverus NL ramp divergence exceeds 5,000 MW, the headline P(binding) range should reflect the full scenario distribution explicitly:
- YE-implied scenario (higher ramp): state probability
- Enverus-implied scenario (lower ramp): state probability
- Probability-weighted blended call

Jul 9's single "MEDIUM-HIGH 35-55%" range partially collapsed the two scenarios. A better format would have been: "Base case (YE realized): HIGH 50-65%. Downside case (Enverus realized): LOW-MEDIUM 18-25%. Blended probability: MEDIUM-HIGH 38-52%." This preserves scenario transparency for downstream agents.

**L26 — WEST_TO_NORTH first-series-activation: probability calibration deferred to post-settlement**

The LOW-MEDIUM 25-35% call for WEST_TO_NORTH was the first activation in the calibration series. Without NMMS constraint data (Stage 0), the actual ERCOT constraint flow limit and shift factors are unknown. If Jul 9 settlement shows binding: the 15,000 MW GR_WEST heuristic is directionally valid and the probability call was appropriate. If non-binding at GR_WEST 15.3-15.7k MW: the heuristic threshold may be too aggressive and should be revised upward (e.g., 15,500 MW minimum before calling LOW-MEDIUM).

**L27 — RegDown HE18 $2.13/MWh is the strongest over-generation AS signal in the post-W25 series**

Jul 9's RegDown spike at HE18 = $2.13/MWh was the highest single-hour RegDown reading in the calibration series. For reference, Jul 11's afternoon RegDown (HE15-19) = $1.54-1.76/MWh — elevated but distributed. The HE18 spike pattern (concentrated over-generation concern at the solar-peak-to-ramp transition) appears specific to deep-NL-trough days (18,030 MW) where the solar-demand imbalance peaks RIGHT at the beginning of the evening ramp. Days with NL trough above 28k MW show a distributed afternoon RegDown profile instead. This distinction may be useful for Stage 1 feature engineering.

**L28 — Cross-source solar methodology on deep-trough days: systematic investigation needed**

On Jul 9, the 6,480 MW NL ramp gap was traced to a 5,655 MW divergence in HE18 NL starting point (YE: 31,359 MW, Enverus: 37,014 MW). On Jul 11, the gap is 3,095 MW but reverses direction (YE HE18 NL HIGHER than Enverus by 3,095 MW). The fact that the cross-source ramp gap reverses sign between Jul 9 and Jul 11 suggests the YE solar subtraction methodology has different behavior at different solar penetration levels or day-of-week patterns. Stage 1 data engineering should systematically document the YE vs Enverus HE18 NL divergence pattern as a feature candidate for the HOUSTON_IMPORT binding model.
