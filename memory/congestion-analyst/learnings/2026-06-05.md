## 3. Methodology Observations

### 3a. P(DA>RT) Signal Directional Failure on 2026-06-05 — Critical Learning

The 2026-06-05 forecast (issued 2026-06-04) called P(DA>RT) = 0.669 at HE20, predicting DA would be the expensive side. Realized: RTSPP at GKS HE20 = $65.24 vs DASPP = $41.00. RT was the expensive side — the spread was negative (DA-RT = -$24.24), meaning RT exceeded DA by a large margin. The P(DA>RT) = 0.669 signal was wrong in direction.

This is the second data point examining P(DA>RT) behavior. The 2026-06-04 settlement showed positive RT energy (RT > DA from GKS perspective), which also contradicted the P(DA>RT) signal in the same direction.

**Hypothesis**: The P(DA>RT) = 0.669 signal at HE20 from Smartbidder may reflect DA energy scarcity pricing at the HB_HOUSTON hub level, while the realized GKS RTSPP reflects a local node constraint that pushes GKS RT above the hub. Smartbidder's P(DA>RT) is likely hub-level, not node-level. When the HOUSTON_IMPORT constraint binds in RT but not as severely in DA, the hub DA price may indeed exceed hub RT price (hence Smartbidder's signal is correct at hub level), but the GKS nodal RTSPP can still exceed DA due to nodal MCC effects. This creates an apparent contradiction: P(DA>RT) correctly describes hub pricing while GKS experiences RT nodal premium above DA. This is the exact MCC phenomenon the congestion model is designed to capture in Stage 2+.

**Actionable revision**: Stop interpreting P(DA>RT) as a directional signal for GKS physical dispatch. Use it only as a congestion probability indicator (i.e., high P(DA>RT) → elevated congestion risk) and not as a direction call for energy arb at the GKS node.

### 3b. Coverage Gap: Midday HE12-15 Houston-Side Constraint Not Captured

The 2026-06-05 settlement shows a large unexpected midday RT premium at GKS: RTSPP $38.78-$43.54 at HE12-14 vs DASPP $29-$33. The Stage 0 heuristic framework only assigned LOW (8-12%) to WEST_TO_NORTH for the midday window, correctly identifying that GR_WEST = 3,635 MW was too high for WEST_TO_NORTH binding. However, no constraint was called for a Houston-side midday event. The $43.54 RTSPP at HE13 represents a genuine uncalled congestion event.

Possible explanation: A thermal outage or transmission constraint on the Houston/South-to-Houston path during the midday high-load window (load at HE13 = 63,638 MW for flowday 2026-06-05) caused localized RT price elevation at GKS that was not captured by the VRE-heavy heuristic framework. The Stage 0 framework lacks a thermal outage input (Cap-Out was 14,329 MW at HE11-14 — elevated but not extreme). Transmission outage data (W3 item 0.11) would be the correct data source to catch this type of event.

**Coverage gap identified**: Midday Houston/South-to-Houston constraints during high-load periods (load > 60,000 MW) with non-VRE drivers. This is a systematic Stage 0 blind spot.
