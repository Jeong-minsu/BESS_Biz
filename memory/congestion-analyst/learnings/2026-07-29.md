## 5. Stage 0 Model Improvement Points

Three observations for Stage 1 feature engineering:

1. **Consecutive binding-zone weeks create T+2 settlement queue congestion**: Jul 27, Jul 28, Jul 29 all carry MEDIUM-HIGH binding probability. Combined with Tenaska DEGRADED status, the T+2 queue has backed up to 3 pending settlements plus overdue settlements from Jul 20-25. Stage 1 infrastructure design should include a settlement validation buffer that flags when more than 3 consecutive high-probability cycles have outstanding T+2 status — this creates calibration risk if model parameters are updated before settlements confirm.

2. **Single-source YE-only cycle should flag Enverus gap adjustment**: When Enverus is absent, the YE NL ramp reading systematically overstates the effective ERCOT NL ramp by 3,741-4,626 MW (measured from learnings/2026-07-17.md). A simple correction factor should be applied in Stage 1: when Enverus is absent, SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT probability is attenuated by applying an effective ramp of (YE ramp - 4,184 MW midpoint) as the calibration input, with the full YE ramp as the upside scenario. For Jul 29, adjusted central estimate would be +21,985 - 4,184 = +17,801 MW — just below confirmed binding floor (+17,935 MW). This would slightly downgrade the call to MEDIUM 28-42%, reflecting the Enverus gap uncertainty more explicitly.

3. **WDPEAK day ordering within heat-wave week matters for duck curve depth**: Jul 27 (Mon) EXTREME (21,706 MW) → Jul 28 (Tue, zero-data) → Jul 29 (Tue) MILD (29,970 MW). The 8,264 MW trough difference between Monday and Tuesday illustrates that the day-of-week position drives wind availability and solar generation timing more than the heat wave per se. Stage 1 feature engineering should include: (a) lagged NL trough from prior day as an input feature; (b) rolling 3-day trough trajectory (deepening vs. shallowing) as a regime indicator. A "shallowing trajectory" following a EXTREME/DEEP Monday predicts a higher probability of MILD or NEGLIGIBLE trough on Tuesday-Wednesday.
