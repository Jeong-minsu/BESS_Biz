## 4. Learning Points

### L1. Saturday SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT binding probability underestimated for day-5 heatwave

The Jul 25 Saturday precedent (non-binding, called 22-30%) was applied too heavily. August 1 is:
- Heat wave day 5 (Jul 28 start) — 5 consecutive high-load days accumulate thermal mass in buildings
- Saturday immediately following the highest-congestion WDPEAK week in Stage 0 history (Jul 27-31)
- The forecast explicitly identified the day-5 heat soak risk but placed it in the risk section rather than incorporating it into the central probability

**Calibration update**: For Saturdays in a multi-day heatwave (day 3+), the LOW-MEDIUM 18-30% baseline should be upgraded to LOW-MEDIUM to MEDIUM 25-40%. The Jul 25 Saturday (day 1 of the prior heat wave) is not a valid analog for day-5 conditions. A better analog is any day-3+ Saturday in a sustained heatwave period.

### L2. Saturday duck curve timing is shifted approximately 2 hours later vs weekday forecast window

Weekday forecast calls Finding 7 at HE09-11 (CT 08:00-11:00). On Saturday, the actual negative price signal appeared at HE11-12 (CT 10:00-12:00). The offset is ~2 hours. Structural explanation: Saturday morning load does not ramp with commercial/industrial demand; the load profile stays flatter in the morning, delaying the NL trough to later in the morning when solar reaches its peak. Solar generation does not observe weekends — peak solar is still near the same clock time — but the load profile shifts the NL minimum to HE11-12 instead of HE09-10.

**Calibration update**: For Saturday forecasts, shift the duck curve window from HE09-11 to HE11-13. The charge window recommendation to bess-optimizer should be HE10-13 for Saturdays (not HE09-12 as for weekdays). This is consistent with the prior finding (learnings/2026-07-29.md §5 point 2) that GKS MCC lags the NL trough by ~1 hour even on weekdays — so on Saturdays, the NL trough at HE11-12 (vs weekday HE10) produces GKS MCC minimum at HE12-13.

### L3. RT-only binding is a distinct pattern not captured in the current framework

The Aug 1 binding event was EXCLUSIVELY an RT phenomenon — DAM did not clear binding, and GKS had zero DA energy position at HE19-21. This RT-only binding pattern has different implications:
- bess-optimizer needs RT flexibility at HE19-21 to capture the spike (no DA commitment to DA sell at those hours)
- The unconditional E[MCC HE19-22] estimate (+$0.5 to +$2.5/MWh) was too conservative — realized RT MCC was much higher
- The P50=0 (called because of low DAM binding probability) was technically correct for DAM; the RT binding probability was HIGHER than the DAM binding probability because the RT market responds to actual physical conditions (heat soak load realization) while DAM clears on forecasts

**Stage 1 implication**: The binding model should separately estimate P(binding DAM) and P(binding RTM | non-binding DAM). Saturday events may have structurally higher P(binding RTM | non-binding DAM) than weekdays because the DAM is more likely to underprice Saturday evening congestion risk.

### L4. Zero-data cycle fundamentally limits probability calibration

The zero-data cycle had CI of ±15-20 ppt. The actual outcome (binding vs non-binding) is statistically consistent with either the call (18-30%) or the upgraded call (30-42%). With 20 ppt CI, the call is nearly uninformative for any specific Saturday. The structural direction (duck curve present, evening congestion possible) was correct; the specific probability was unresolvable without NL ramp data.

**Operational action**: Data recovery (retry YE API after rate-limit window) is the highest-priority action in any zero-data Saturday cycle before 10:00 CT DAM cutoff. Even a single YE NL ramp reading would reduce CI from ±20 ppt to ±10-12 ppt, potentially changing the bess-optimizer DA sell quantity at HE19-22 from zero to 50+ MW.

### L5. HE21 Non-Spin MCPC signal is an independent binding indicator

HE21 Non-Spin settlement: $938.40 (vs typical $34-208 range on non-binding days this week). The NS MCPC was exceptionally high at HE21 specifically — coinciding with the RT energy spike. High NS MCPC is a real-time market signal that the system is capacity-constrained. In future cycles with real-time data availability, monitoring the NS MCPC trajectory entering HE19-20 can serve as an independent early warning of SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT binding onset. This is not available at forecast time (D-1) but is relevant for intraday updates and RT dispatch decisions.
