## 5. Key Delta Observations

### Delta 1 — Cross-Source NL Trough Gap (4,944 MW) on Aug 30: Highest Single-Day Gap Since Jul 18

The Aug 30 NL trough cross-source gap (YE 19,917 MW vs Enverus 24,861 MW = 4,944 MW) is the second-largest NL trough gap in the Aug 2026 Stage 0 series. Only Jul 18 had a larger gap (6,402 MW, documented in learnings/2026-07-18.md). The tight solar agreement (564 MW, < 2%) eliminates solar as the primary driver — the divergence is primarily in the wind NL component.

Investigation: The YE vs Enverus NL trough gap is driven by wind estimates. At HE10 (NL trough), ERCOT-total wind at YE vs Enverus diverges. If Enverus carried ~4,000-5,000 MW more wind at HE10 than YE, that alone explains the 4,944 MW NL gap given tight solar agreement.

**Learning update**: When solar cross-source gap is TIGHT (< 800 MW) but NL trough cross-source gap is LARGE (> 3,000 MW), the divergence is wind-driven, not solar-driven. In this scenario, the E[MCC] call should note which source carries more wind at the trough hour and which is historically more accurate for Sunday WEPEAK wind trough. From learnings/2026-07-18.md: Enverus systematically underestimates late-evening NL ramp (overstates wind at HE18-22) — if this pattern extends to midday, Enverus would show higher midday wind and lower NL trough, making YE the closer-to-actual source on WEPEAK Sunday midday. This would favor the YE EXTREME scenario as the more probable central estimate.

**Action**: For future Sunday WEPEAK duck curve calls with tight solar agreement and large NL gap, weight YE NL trough as P50 central estimate (not midpoint of both sources) when the wind divergence direction is consistent with the Enverus-overestimates-wind pattern documented in learnings/2026-07-18.md.

### Delta 2 — Aug 30 is a Key Calibration Inflection: Late-August → Early-September Duck Curve Step-Change

Comparing Aug 30 (Sunday WEPEAK, NL trough 19,917 MW YE / 24,861 MW Enverus) with Sep 1 (Tuesday WDPEAK, NL trough 32,127 MW YE):
- Sep 1 NL trough is 12,210 MW shallower than Aug 30 (YE-to-YE comparison)
- This is the single largest 2-day NL trough step in the Aug-Sep 2026 series

Partially explained by:
1. Day-of-week effect (Sunday WEPEAK → Tuesday WDPEAK raises load floor, reduces duck depth)
2. Wind recovery pattern: Aug 30 saw very high Sunday overnight wind (SCI 0.370-0.478 overnight, SCI 0.207 daytime trough) vs Sep 1 which shows SCI 0.100-0.174 daytime and 0.342-0.442 overnight (step-down overall)
3. Solar: Sep 1 solar peak 30,797 MW vs Aug 30 33,395 MW — solar declining as days shorten in September
4. Load: Sep 1 85,477 MW peak vs Aug 30 87,126 MW — both in summer peak season but Sep load slightly lower

**Learning**: Aug 30-Sep 1 step-change is consistent with established seasonal transition signal. The Stage 0 duck curve tier model (MILD/MODERATE/SEVERE/EXTREME) transitions from EXTREME/SEVERE on late-August weekends to MILD on early-September weekdays as a structural seasonal pattern. This should be incorporated into forward-look commentary beginning Sep 1.

### Delta 4 — Cap-Out Estimation vs. Actual

The Aug 30 forecast estimated cap-out at ~9,200-9,600 MW (NEGLIGIBLE, below 10,000 MW dual-trigger). The actual Aug 30 cap-out as reported in the filed Aug 30 data was 11,293-11,298 MW (from the hourly table in `shared/data/forecasts/congestion/2026-08-30.md`). This is well above the 10,000 MW dual-trigger.

**Critical error identified**: The forecast estimated cap-out at 9,200-9,600 MW but actual was 11,293 MW — a 1,693-2,093 MW underestimate. This should have triggered HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY LOW probability (not NEGLIGIBLE).

Root cause: At filing time (Aug 29), the YES Energy CSV column `ERCOT (TOTAL_RESOURCE_CAP_OUT)` for Aug 30 was not explicitly stated in the headline analysis — the "~9,200-9,600 MW" estimate was a heuristic guess rather than reading the actual CSV value.

**Action**: Always explicitly read the `ERCOT (TOTAL_RESOURCE_CAP_OUT)` column from the YES Energy CSV before assigning HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY probability. Never use a heuristic cap-out estimate when the actual value is available in the data file. In this case, the actual cap-out of 11,293 MW exceeds the 10,000 MW dual-trigger and should have elevated HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY to LOW 8-14%.
