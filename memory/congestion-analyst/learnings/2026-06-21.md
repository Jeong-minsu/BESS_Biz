## 4. Improvement Points

**Finding 1 (carries over from 2026-06-20): Evening constraint timing — solstice-specific wind behavior**

On the solstice (June 21), wind can surge in the evening hours as thermal gradients shift with extended daylight. The record GR_WEST 18,103 MW forecast (and possibly exceeding it) may have sustained system-wide price suppression through HE20-21, overriding the HOUSTON_IMPORT signal. The HIGH call for HOUSTON_IMPORT was based on NL ramp — but the NL ramp may have been offset by wind exceeding the forecast. This is the same structural risk flagged in the 2026-06-21 forecast (Risk table: "GR_WEST +3,000 MW surge → extreme West depression, negative prices overnight"). That tail scenario may have actualized in the evening hours instead.

**Action**: When GR_WEST forecast exceeds 15,000 MW AND NL ramp is simultaneously HIGH, the evening HOUSTON_IMPORT call should be haircut by 10-15 ppt to account for wind-suppression spillover into the evening. Current heuristic does not handle simultaneous high-wind + high-ramp scenarios.

**Finding 2 (new): WEST_TO_NORTH onset is consistently 1-2 hours earlier than HE22-24 anchor**

For the second consecutive day (2026-06-20 had HE21 RT spike; 2026-06-21 shows HE21-22 DA NS spike), the overnight West constraint activates at HE21 CT, not HE22-24 as modeled. The SCI-based trigger (HE24 reading) lags the actual binding onset by 1-2 hours. The SCI calculation uses end-of-day GR_WEST (HE24) but the constraint starts binding when the system transitions from daytime to overnight load profile, which occurs around HE20-21 on summer days with late sunset.

**Action**: Shift WEST_TO_NORTH forecast window to HE20-24 (extend by 2 hours) on any day where GR_WEST HE20-21 forecast exceeds 14,000 MW. Already identified in 2026-06-20 learning (action item 2) — this cycle confirms the pattern is systematic, not isolated.
