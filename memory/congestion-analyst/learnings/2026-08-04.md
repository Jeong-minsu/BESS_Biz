## 4. Learning Points

### L1. Aug 4 overnight West wind was a local maximum in the Stage 0 Phase 2 series

The Aug 4 overnight GR_WEST (SCI 0.461-0.492) confirmed as a series maximum for overnight West wind in the August period by the Aug 5 D+1 data (SCI 0.367-0.419). The step-down is structurally large (−0.042 to −0.073 SCI per hour). This pattern — single-day overnight spike followed by step-down — has not been directly calibrated before in the overnight West model. The Aug 4 forecast correctly identified the MEDIUM-HIGH threshold crossing; the Aug 5 step-down confirms the differentiation was real, not noise.

**Stage 1 feature implication**: GR_WEST overnight (HE01-06) has high intra-week variability. A "D-1 vs D-2 overnight delta" feature (identified in Aug 3 learning L1) would have flagged the Aug 4→Aug 5 step-down. The 2-day trajectory (Aug 3 SCI 0.327-0.345 → Aug 4 SCI 0.461-0.492 → Aug 5 SCI 0.367-0.419) is a V-shape pattern: surge followed by partial reversion. Single-day forecasts without trajectory context cannot anticipate these patterns.

### L2. Solar cliff magnitude is structurally persistent across Aug 3-5 while duck curve depth varies

Over three consecutive WDPEAK days:
- Aug 3: NL trough 26,395 MW (MODERATE); ramp +18,665 MW
- Aug 4: NL trough 23,639 MW (SEVERE deep); ramp +21,473 MW
- Aug 5: NL trough 25,190 MW (SEVERE upper); ramp +21,560 MW

The ramp signal (+21,473 MW Aug 4 vs +21,560 MW Aug 5, effectively identical) demonstrates that the evening solar cliff is driven by the NL(HE21) level and the HE18 NL base — both controlled by largely the same thermal load and solar forecast for the Wednesday window. Meanwhile the duck curve trough varies significantly day-to-day based on the solar HE10 forecast and daytime GR_WEST. This decoupling — persistent strong evening ramp, variable midday trough depth — is a recurring pattern in the August series that affects the multi-window dispatch strategy. The two signals are partially independent.

**Operational implication**: The strong evening solar cliff (ramp +21,473-21,560 MW) on Aug 4-5 is the dominant revenue signal for GKS if SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT confirms binding on either day. The duck curve (Aug 4 deep, Aug 5 shallower) affects charge-cycle economics but does not change the evening discharge setup. Treating these as correlated would be an error.

### L3. The MEDIUM probability tier for SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT at ramp 21,000-22,000 MW (Enverus gap unadjusted) may be systematically conservative for RT binding

Both Aug 4 (+21,473 MW) and Aug 5 (+21,560 MW) are called MEDIUM 28-42%. If either confirms RT binding at T+1, the running pattern would be: confirmed binding floor = +17,935 MW (Jun 17), confirmed binding at +21,473-21,918 MW (Jul 1 anchor, and potentially Aug 4-5). This establishes that the 21,000-22,000 MW range is a near-confirmed-binding zone in the YE single-source framework. The Enverus gap correction (which consistently pulls the adjusted ramp to below the floor) may be overly conservative for DA binding and even more so for RT binding. Until Enverus data is restored, the MEDIUM 28-42% call for ramps above +21,000 MW should carry a parenthetical note: "P(RT binding) estimated 35-50% (10-15 ppt above DA call)."

### L4. The PANHANDLE_EXPORT_345 "near-miss" on Aug 4 (437 MW from threshold) is a one-time event in Stage 0 Phase 2; Aug 5's 2,590 MW margin makes it immediately irrelevant

The Aug 4 LOW 5-12% PANHANDLE call was unprecedented in Stage 0 Phase 2 — no prior cycle in the post-W25 period (since late June) had GR_WEST within 1,000 MW of the 15,000 MW threshold overnight. Aug 5 reverts to a 2,590 MW margin (effectively NEGLIGIBLE). This confirms the Aug 4 event was driven by a specific wind surge (overnight GR_WEST peaked at 14,563 MW, SCI 0.492). If Aug 4 binding at PANHANDLE is confirmed at T+1, it would be the first PANHANDLE_EXPORT binding event in Stage 0 Phase 2 — a high-priority calibration result.
