### Pattern-by-Pattern Congestion Interpretation

**Overnight HE01-06 — No West Congestion Materialized**

RT exceeded DA at GKS by $0.97-$8.45 per hour. Under the Stage 0 framework, WEST_TO_NORTH_345 binding suppresses GKS RT prices (negative MCC). The opposite materialized: GKS was more expensive in RT than in DA overnight. Two candidate explanations:
1. WEST_TO_NORTH did NOT bind in RT on Saturday overnight (weekend load too low to create North-side scarcity; West generation surplus went unsuppressed or was absorbed).
2. Other RT conditions (generation shortfall, load response) drove RT prices above DA-cleared levels, masking any West suppression effect.

The HE02 RT spike (+$6.30 premium) is particularly notable given that Aug 8 is a Saturday — lower load should ease, not tighten, overnight RT. This suggests a supply-side deviation in RT not reflected in DA (possible forced generation outage between DA clearing and RT operation).

**Duck Curve HE09-12 — Absent Negative MCC**

RT > DA at GKS by $2-$4 throughout the duck curve window. The Stage 0 framework expects GKS to experience negative MCC (price suppression) when solar surplus + West wind creates West-to-North congestion. On Aug 8 Saturday, no such suppression is visible. Possible explanations:
1. Saturday load was sufficiently low that the West generation surplus cleared without binding the relevant constraint.
2. GKS's PTDF position on the duck-curve-binding constraint is weaker than assumed, and other factors dominated GKS nodal pricing.
3. The constraint that drives GKS negative MCC during weekday duck curves did not bind on Saturday because the constraint flow stayed within limit (load mix difference between weekday and weekend).

This is a critical calibration point: the duck curve GKS MCC signal may be substantially weaker on weekends than on weekdays. Saturday Finding 7 tier ≠ same GKS MCC magnitude as WDPEAK equivalent.

**Afternoon HE13-17 — Unexplained RT Premium (+$2.83 to +$10.76)**

The afternoon RT premium is the day's most significant and least-explainable congestion event. HE16 stands out: RT $41.63 vs DA $30.87 (+$10.76). Summer Saturday afternoon (HE16 = 3 PM CDT) is a peak solar hour with high air conditioning load. Candidate drivers:
1. Unplanned thermal generation outage between DA and RT, reducing supply into the GKS area.
2. Higher-than-DA-forecast load in the Houston area during Saturday afternoon heat.
3. Solar underperformance (actual GHI below forecast) in the GKS service area, reducing in-area generation.
4. Local transmission constraint into GKS node that binds in RT but was not cleared in DAM.

The HE16 magnitude (+$10.76) is comparable to SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT-level congestion events. This pattern deserves investigation as a potential recurring Saturday structural feature.

**Evening HE18-21 — SOUTH_HOUSTON DAM-Binding / RT-Non-Binding Regime**

HE19 is the clearest congestion signal of the day: DASPP $29.77 vs RTSPP $12.03 → DA-RT spread +$17.74. This is consistent with SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT binding in the DAM clearing (elevated DA prices at GKS) but NOT binding in RT (actual Saturday load insufficient to sustain constraint binding; solar cliff ramp below the RT binding threshold).

HE19-21 spreads (+$17.74, +$10.80, +$6.28) form a decaying pattern as the solar cliff completes and the load-based pressure resolves. This "DAM-binding / RT-non-binding" regime is a new data point in Stage 0 calibration — prior SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT events were assessed primarily on whether RT binding occurred, but Saturday may systematically exhibit DAM-bound / RT-relaxed behavior.

**Implication for future Saturday calls**: The SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT call for Saturday WEPEAK should explicitly distinguish P(DAM binding) from P(RT binding). On Saturdays, P(DAM binding) may be comparable to WDPEAK (DAM clears the constraint based on DA forecast), while P(RT binding) is materially lower (actual RT load falls short of DA forecast). This creates a systematic positive DA-RT spread at GKS in the HE19-21 window on Saturdays with moderate-to-high ramp forecasts — a recurring DART opportunity.

### Key Causal Chain

The HE10-11 opportunistic RT charging (-$2,172.50) established the physical constraint cascade that drove HE12-17 losses (-$5,318.83):

1. HE10-11 (RT cheap, $21-24/MWh): BESS charged 96 MWh opportunistically in RT. SOC fills.
2. HE12 (first DA sale hour): BESS still consuming 4.79 MWh in RT despite 50 MWh DA sale. Physical generation = 0. Short ~55 MWh at $24.35/MWh.
3. HE13-17 (DA sales 50/50/30/30/30 MWh; RT > DA $3-$11): BESS discharging in RT but below DA schedule. SOC was not fully depleted — 81 MWh physical generation across 6 hours vs 190 MWh DA schedule. Short positions settled at high RT prices.
4. HE19 (RT $12.03, cheapest of day): BESS recharged again at very cheap prices. Rational for HE22-24 discharge.
5. HE22-24 (RT $34-37/MWh): BESS discharged profitably, recovering +$3,167 on the cycle.

**Net assessment of HE10-11 opportunistic charging decision**: The HE22-24 recovery (+$3,167) likely required the HE19 recharge more than the HE10-11 charge. The HE10-11 charge primarily financed the HE12-14 discharge (which was insufficient), costing -$2,172 while creating the HE13-17 underperformance cascade (-$5,319 gross). A better dispatch strategy — delay opportunistic charging until HE18/19 when RT was cheaper ($12.03/MWh HE19 vs $21.77/MWh HE10) — would have avoided the cascade at lower cost.

**Congestion contribution to RT losses**: The afternoon RT premium (HE13-17, RT > DA $3-$11) amplified the cost of the physical underperformance. If RT had equaled DA in those hours, the HE12-17 shortfall cost would have been near-zero.
