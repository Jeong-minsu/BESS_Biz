## 5. Key Learning Items

**L16 — Post-holiday NL ramp structural low extends into second business day**

Jul 6 issued the lowest NL ramp in the July series (+13,645 MW). The Jul 7 D+1 forecast (issued today, Jul 6) shows an even lower YE ramp (+13,056 MW), setting a new series low on the second consecutive business day. The post-Independence Day holiday demand recovery appears slower than pre-holiday model expectations, as the loaded forecast (YE WDPEAK) implies a full Monday return to demand levels but the actual NL ramp remains structurally depressed.

Implication: For any multi-day holiday weekend (Independence Day 2026 = Saturday + observed Monday), the first 2 business days carry a structural NL ramp suppression factor. This is distinct from the single-day holiday discount (applied to Jul 4 itself). The multi-day post-holiday suppression should be built into Stage 1 calendar feature engineering.

Action: Add to Stage 1 feature backlog — "multi-day holiday weekend NL ramp recovery lag" indicator (T+1 and T+2 business days after observed federal holiday weekend).

**L17 — Finding 7 NL trough is deeper on Tuesday (Jul 7) than Monday (Jul 6) despite similar day type**

Jul 6 NL trough: 35,301 MW at HE10 (weekday, load 83,111 MW peak)
Jul 7 NL trough: 32,206 MW (YE) / 31,157 MW (Enverus) at HE10 (weekday, load 83,961 MW peak — slightly HIGHER than Jul 6)

Despite Jul 7 having a higher gross load peak, its NL trough is 3-4k MW deeper. The difference is driven by higher solar penetration at HE10 on Jul 7 (24,954 MW YE vs Jul 6's HE10 solar — not recorded directly, but implied by the trough difference). Tuesday solar appears higher than Monday solar in the July 6-7 series.

Implication: Day-of-week is not the dominant variable for Finding 7 magnitude on consecutive weekdays — solar forecast at HE10 is the dominant variable. The heuristic of "Monday weekday = reduced Finding 7" does not automatically apply Tuesday-Friday. Each day requires independent solar level assessment.
