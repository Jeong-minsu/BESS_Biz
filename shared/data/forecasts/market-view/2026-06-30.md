Estimated P(RT > $100 at HE20-21) moderately elevated (qualitatively 25-40% based on reserve margin geometry); RT spike could reach scarcity pricing ($2,000-5,000/MWh) in a tail scenario.

- **Thermal cap-out 8,038 MW average, peaking 8,518 MW at HE11-12:** Roughly 8 GW of dispatchable capacity is unavailable throughout the day. This is a material headwind against reserve margin when net load hits 51 GW at HE21. The bottom of cap-out (6,646 MW at HE24/midnight) provides no comfort for the evening peak window.

- **Evening solar cliff + load persistence:** Dallas temperatures 95-96°F (feels like 99°F) with no precipitation and moderate humidity keeps AC load elevated well into the evening. Load at HE20 80,316 MW / HE21 77,521 MW while solar is essentially zero after HE21. This overlap — high load, no solar, partial wind recovery — is the defining structural risk of the day.

- **West Texas wind daytime trough:** GR_WEST averages 15,623 MW but drops to 14,258 MW at HE15. This is approximately 1,365 MW below its daily peak, reducing the wind offset against peak demand precisely when solar is also past peak. The GR_COASTAL evening recovery to 3,000 MW (HE18) partially compensates.

- **Enverus vs Yes Energy divergence on net load:** Enverus net load average of ~37,670 MW is approximately +6,600 MW above Yes Energy's 31,003 MW. The primary driver is Enverus projecting lower wind (avg ~22,050 MW vs Yes Energy 23,673 MW, a -1,620 MW gap) concentrated in the daytime trough. Enverus also projects slightly higher solar (+1,850 MW at peak), which partially offsets. If Enverus wind is correct, net load at HE13-16 would be ~1,600-2,000 MW tighter than Yes Energy suggests — raising midday prices toward the $35-50 range rather than $20-35.

- **AG2 WSI load vs Yes Energy load:** AG2 WSI projects 6/30 peak at approximately 85,100-85,672 MW (HE16-17 half-hourly readings) vs Yes Energy BIDCLOSE peak of 83,165 MW at HE17. The gap is approximately +1,900 to +2,500 MW. This is a meaningful upward risk to load — if AG2 is correct, the evening ramp net load level would be ~2,000 MW higher, further compressing the reserve margin and elevating spike risk.
