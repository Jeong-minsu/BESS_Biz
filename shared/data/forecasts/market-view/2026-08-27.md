The practical consequence is zero meaningful wind displacement of overnight load, keeping overnight net load elevated at 54,073-58,289 MW across HE02-07, compared to prior-normal August overnight net loads of 35,000-45,000 MW.

- **Cap out acceleration signals thermal fleet heat stress**: Evening cap out of 8,550 MW is 1,632 MW above Day 7 (6,918 MW) and appears uniformly elevated across all hours (HE01-07 at 8,659 MW vs Day 7's 7,412 MW). This pattern — rising outages across all hours, not concentrated in a single unit's outage window — is consistent with accumulating heat stress failures (cooling system degradation, bearing overheating, fuel supply chain issues) rather than planned maintenance. If this trend continues into Day 9, cap out could approach 9,000-9,500 MW, significantly reducing available thermal capacity. The ERCOT Operating Guide's conservative reserve margin watch level (typically ~15%) would be approached if cap out exceeds ~9,500 MW with load near 85-87 GW.

- **Evening net load moderation is real but not a relief event**: The 3,616 MW improvement in HE21 net load vs Day 7 reflects load decay (-1,860 MW peak) combined with marginally more offshore/coastal wind at HE21 (8,512 MW — similar to Day 7). The scarcity window (HE19-22) remains the primary revenue window; it is less extreme than Day 7 but still elevated relative to any pre-heat wave day.

- **Midday duck deepens modestly**: HE10 net load = 41,232 MW (Day 7: 42,908 MW, -1,676 MW). Solar is 432 MW stronger on average and peaks 1,555 MW higher (HE14 = 30,714 MW). Wind at midday is similar (~2,086-2,564 MW at HE10-12). The deeper duck improves the charge-side economics for BESS operators, but the thermal commitment dilemma persists: over-committing for the evening ramp means negative price exposure at HE10-11; under-committing risks RT shortage at HE20-22.
