## Key Drivers

- **Monday commercial/industrial load return (+2,684 MW avg vs Day 11 Sunday)** is the primary demand driver. The heat wave has not abated — peak load at 89,093 MW approaches series highs. Sustained residential cooling demand combined with commercial ramp produces a classic Monday "double peak" pattern where both midday and evening prices are elevated vs the weekend.

- **Thermal outage declining trajectory (HE07: 11,243 MW → HE24: 7,013 MW = −4,230 MW)** is the single most constructive fundamental shift of the series. Units that registered Saturday/Sunday planned maintenance are returning Monday afternoon. This mechanically improves reserve margins at HE21-24 and moderates late-night price pressure, but the HE19-21 window still faces the full ~9,989-10,007 MW outage burden.

- **GR_WEST midday suppression persists (HE14-15 floor: 4,738-4,823 MW)** despite the overnight surge to 14,690 MW at HE01. The gap between overnight and midday GR_WEST wind is ~9,952 MW — a diurnal amplitude that is structurally consistent with residual anticyclonic flow being reinforced by mesoscale effects (daytime land-breeze suppression in the Permian Basin). GR_WEST's overnight recovery to 14+ GW is bullish for DART/AS positions in early morning hours but not a reliable signal for afternoon price suppression.

- **GR_COASTAL series-record peak at HE18 (3,175 MW)** provides the strongest sea-breeze buffer of the heat wave at the exact moment the solar cliff commences. Coastal sea breeze is driven by land-sea temperature contrast and Monday land temperatures remain extreme. This buffer mitigates (but does not eliminate) the HE19→HE20 ramp stress — GR_COASTAL is already declining to 3,093 MW at HE19 and 2,735 MW at HE20.

- **Net load ramp severity (HE18→HE20: +16,932 MW in 2 hours)** remains a series-level event despite being slightly less extreme than Day 11 (+17,475 MW). The combined solar cliff (−13,776 MW) and declining-but-still-high outages (9,989-10,007 MW) make HE19-20 the primary RT spike risk window for the 5th consecutive day.
