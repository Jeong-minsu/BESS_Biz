## Key Drivers

1. **Evening solar falloff + sustained high gross load = steep net-load ramp.** The 17 GW net-load ramp in HE18-21 is the structural price driver. Gross load stays above 76,582 MW through HE20 while solar collapses from 21,694 MW (HE18) to 666 MW (HE21). Wind provides only partial offset (+2,500 MW over the same window). This ramp shape is well-forecast across all three sources (Yes Energy, Enverus, AG2) — it is a high-confidence driver.

2. **Thermal outage stack at 12 GW creates limited reserve headroom.** With ~12 GW offline and gross load approaching 79 GW at peak, the effective reserve margin depends critically on wind performance in GR_WEST and GR_SOUTH. If the 12,536 MW outage level (HE9) persists into the evening ramp (currently projected to ease to ~11,700-11,900 MW HE18-20), any further unplanned outages in the 1-2 GW range could trigger scarcity pricing in RT. This is the primary upside RT risk not captured in Smartbidder's base-case RT forecast.

3. **Solar depth creates a midday inversion risk.** Net load trough near 16,351 MW (HE10) with 26-28 GW of solar on the grid (vs. ~22 GW wind) means midday marginal price is set by fast-ramping gas peakers cycling off or curtailment. Enverus's higher solar forecast (28,103 MW peak) deepens this trough further. Risk of negative or near-zero DA prices HE10-11 if solar is at the Enverus upper end — Smartbidder's $15.36 floor may be optimistic if dispatch models are not capturing full solar saturation.

4. **West Texas wind recovery is overnight, not evening.** GR_WEST STWPF is at its weakest 12,160-12,342 MW in HE14-HE16, recovering to 17,054 MW only at HE24. This means the late afternoon ramp receives minimal incremental wind help from the largest wind zone. GR_COASTAL and GR_SOUTH wind picks up in the evening but the combined addition is only ~1,500 MW. This structural dynamic supports the positive DA-RT spread in HE19-20.

5. **AS market is the cleanest signal for the day.** Non-Spin and ECRS pricing in HE20-22 at $9.19 and $2.90 respectively are elevated relative to recent baselines, signaling model anticipation of tight conditions. The multi-product AS spike (Non-Spin + ECRS + RRS + RegUp all elevated HE20-22) is unusual and points to co-optimization opportunity for BESS: discharge into energy + sell Non-Spin/ECRS capacity simultaneously. The BESS charge window is well-defined as HE8-HE13 (solar suppressed prices $15-18, RegDown elevated to $0.66-1.34 providing partial offset on charging cost).
