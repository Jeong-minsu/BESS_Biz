## Headline

Sunday June 7 is a moderate-demand summer day (YE avg ~60.8 GW, near Saturday's level) with strong wind overnight and robust solar during the day. The primary market story is an unusually steep evening net-load ramp: net load rises +25,703 MW from the HE11 solar trough (18,380 MW) to the HE21 peak (44,083 MW), and the 3-hour sunset window HE18-21 alone accounts for +14,041 MW of thermal ramp requirement. Smartbidder DA consensus is $31/MWh at HE20-21 (flat vs prior day's $31-44/MWh range — moderated by higher wind), with DA-RT spread positive throughout the day (+$1.70/MWh average). P(DA>RT) is elevated during the entire HE16-23 window (64-66%), pointing to a persistent DA-expensive regime in the evening. Non-Spin AS spiking to $8.26/MWh at HE21 is the clearest value signal for BESS AS positioning. The primary risk is a 14.6 GW capacity outage bloc sitting through the evening ramp.

## Key Drivers

1. **Strong wind + robust solar = structurally low prices, but sharp evening ramp**: Today's total renewables are materially higher than Saturday (ERCOT wind mean ~17.8 GW vs ~6.9 GW on June 6; solar mean ~10 GW similar). High wind suppresses the off-peak price floor to $16-17/MWh in the solar trough hours (HE10-12), and the overnight trough will be pushed even lower as wind builds to 25.5 GW by HE24. Yet the evening net-load ramp (+14,041 MW in 3 hours, HE18→HE21) is as steep as any day, driven by the simultaneous solar collapse and sustained load above 67 GW. Gas generation must absorb the full net-load ramp despite a 14,500 MW outage bloc.

2. **HE19-21 is the singular value window for both energy and AS**: DA prices step from $25.61 (HE19) to $31.17 (HE20) and AS Non-Spin leaps from $1.32 (HE19) to $8.26 (HE21). The net-load ramp from 33,390 MW (HE19) to 44,083 MW (HE21) is the mechanical driver of both. Any BESS positioning for this day should concentrate discharge capacity and AS offers in this 3-hour block. Outside this window, prices are unremarkable.

3. **14.6 GW outage bloc sitting through the evening ramp — tail risk**: The capacity outage level (mean 14,611 MW) is the highest in recent briefing history. With load at 67-72 GW and outages at ~14.5-14.7 GW in HE16-21, effective online capacity margin is thin. An unscheduled outage of 1-2 GW during the HE19-21 window would move prices significantly above Smartbidder's $31/MWh forecast — potentially $40-55/MWh territory. This is the clearest upside tail risk for RT prices.

4. **Wind forecast disagreement is the primary uncertainty**: The 3-source wind range (AG2 16,589 MW to Enverus 18,695 MW mean) spans a 2,106 MW corridor. For overnight hours in particular, Enverus projects 17.7-17.8 GW vs AG2's 13.6-15.4 GW (HE1-6) — a gap that translates directly to overnight net-load and DA clearing price. If AG2's lower wind verifies, overnight prices will be firmer ($26-30/MWh range) and the HE20-21 evening spike could be amplified. If Enverus' higher wind verifies, overnight DA may fall toward $18-22/MWh and the evening spike is dampened. The wind disagreement is the dominant source of price uncertainty today.

5. **Sunday DAM characteristics**: Sunday DAMs historically have fewer participants and lighter AS procurement activity than weekdays. This adds noise to AS price formation (particularly ECRS and Non-Spin) around the Smartbidder forecasted values. Non-Spin at $8.26/MWh (HE21) is at a level where clearing is highly probable even in a thin DAM, but the precise clearing level carries wider confidence intervals than a weekday equivalent.

## Risks and Watch

1. **Primary risk — thermal outage surprise at HE20**: Capacity outage at 14,499-14,759 MW through the evening ramp is the highest outage level seen in recent briefings. Any unplanned additional outage of 1-2 GW during HE19-21 compresses reserve margin to near-critical and pushes DA RT prices well above the $31/MWh Smartbidder base. Probability assessment: low per-event, but elevated outage baseline makes the tail fatter.

2. **Wind realization vs AG2 low scenario**: AG2 WindCast projects ~1,190 MW less wind than YE (overnight hours especially 2-3 GW lower). If AG2 verifies, overnight RT prices will be meaningfully firmer than Smartbidder's overnight forecasts (~$24-26/MWh), shrinking the overnight charge window for BESS. Watch real-time wind output at HE1-3 for early verification signal.

3. **Enverus solar upside scenario**: Enverus solar mean +1,161 MW vs YE. If Enverus is correct, midday net load falls by ~1.1 GW, further suppressing HE10-12 prices (below the already-low $16-17/MWh). This widens the daily price spread but doesn't change the evening peak hours. Timing of solar exit (HE21 in both sources) appears more stable than the magnitude.

4. **Smartbidder AS Non-Spin $8.26/MWh at HE21**: This level is unusually high for a Sunday. If it reflects the tight-reserve signal correctly, it offers significant AS revenue in the HE20-22 window. If the outage profile relaxes or wind comes in higher than expected (Enverus scenario), this value could deflate to $3-5/MWh range at clearing.

5. **RegDown HE15-18 elevated ($2.54-2.73/MWh)**: This is the highest RegDown value window, coinciding with solar decline onset. BESS with capacity available in this window (not yet committed to discharge) can capture meaningful RegDown revenue before the evening energy/Non-Spin window opens. Note the trade-off: committing to RegDown in HE15-18 reduces available discharge capacity for HE20-21 energy.
