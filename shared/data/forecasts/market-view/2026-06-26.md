## Bullet 6 — DA-RT Spread Signals and Key Risks

**P(DA<RT) / P(DA>RT) dominant signals by window:**
- HE01-09: P(DA<RT) consistently 0.62-0.70 dominant → RT > DA expected; off-peak hours favor long RT or avoid DA over-commitment.
- HE14-18: P(DA>RT) moves to 0.54-0.59 dominant → solar hours see DA bid-up vs soft RT; short DA or avoid charging in DA for these hours.
- HE19: essentially 50/50 — P(DA<RT) 0.582 vs P(DA>RT) 0.576. Spread direction uncertain; +$2.88 DA-RT observed but model probability nearly flat.
- HE20-21: P(DA>RT) 0.636-0.639 dominant → DA premium confirmed, short DA signal.
- HE22: P(DA<RT) 0.625 dominant → RT recovers above DA, position reversal risk.

**Key risks to flag:**
1. **Solar forecast divergence HE13-16**: Enverus peaks at 30,988 MW vs Yes Energy 28,499 MW — if Enverus is correct, real-time net load in this window is lower, RT prices stay softer, and the early evening ramp starts later. This compresses the HE18-19 price spike window.
2. **Wind daytime trough (HE14-15)**: GR_WEST at 11,175 MW at HE15 is the system's weakest RE point, coinciding with maximum load. Any thermal forced outage in this window could tighten reserve margin significantly. Current outage at ~9,961-10,051 MW (no large change detected intraday).
3. **HE19 spread direction ambiguity**: Repeat of the pattern flagged in 2026-06-22/24 learnings. P-values near 50/50; actual spread realization depends on gas ramp speed vs solar cliff timing. Plan for both scenarios.
4. **Non-Spin delay pattern**: Historical pattern in learnings suggests Non-Spin spike can shift 1-2 hours later than model peak. Smartbidder already shows HE22-23 as peak; if further 1h delay occurs, HE23-24 becomes the primary AS window.
5. **AG2 load gap**: AG2 WSI D+1 average ~68,400 MW vs Yes Energy 69,250 MW (-850 MW). Directionally AG2 is the lower load view. If AG2 is correct, afternoon peak load relief slightly reduces HE16-17 scarcity risk but doesn't materially change the HE20-22 ramp-driven price dynamic.
