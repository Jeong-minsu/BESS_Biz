## Market Overview

July 6 is the first full workday after the Independence Day holiday weekend. Commercial and industrial load returning to normal levels combined with Texas summer afternoon temperatures drives gross demand to 83 GW. The session is structurally a classic July Texas pattern: solar suppresses net load and prices through midday, but the late-afternoon solar cliff creates a severe net-load ramp that stresses the thermal fleet with wind generation at a daytime minimum. ERCOT outage levels are elevated at 6.7 GW on average, concentrated in the midday maintenance window when prices are lowest.

## Key Drivers

- **Post-holiday Monday demand return**: Commercial/industrial load coming back from July 4 extended weekend; typical Monday load signature at full summer heat levels. Risk that some sites extend holiday, creating modest load downside if realized.
- **Solar-to-thermal handoff severity**: 29 GW of solar at noon collapses to near-zero by 9 PM. The rate of solar decline (roughly −4 GW/hr from HE17 to HE20) requires thermal fleet to ramp at full capability. GR_WEST wind below 500 MW at HE14 means wind provides zero buffer.
- **6.7 GW of scheduled outages**: Midday maintenance window (HE13-14 peak at 6,721 MW) is a 6–7% reduction in thermal fleet. While outages are expected to begin returning by HE17, any slippage in outage restoration extends scarcity exposure.
- **HE21 scarcity signal**: $58.64 DA backed by $13 Non-Spin and $6 ECRS is not a routine price — it reflects genuine reserve tightness expectation as the system transitions from ~50% solar-backed supply to 100% thermal + limited wind. Reserve margin in the HE20-22 window is the key variable.
- **Enverus upper-bound risk**: Enverus net load at HE21 (66,879 MW) is 1,644 MW above Yes Energy (65,235 MW). If Enverus is accurate, the scarcity event is more severe. Neither source captures potential last-minute unit outages.

## Risk Factors

**Upside (higher prices / tighter market):**
1. **Unplanned outage in HE18-22 window**: Any unexpected trip of a large thermal unit during the solar cliff would push scarcity pricing beyond $58 in HE21 and could trigger ORDC/scarcity adder
2. **Post-holiday load surprise**: If return-to-work demand is stronger than forecast (Enverus +1.3 GW scenario), reserve margin in HE21 could tighten further; data center load adds also unforecastable upside
3. **Wind underperformance vs STWPF**: GR_WEST forecast at 492 MW HE14 and 999 MW HE17 — if actual is weaker, the thermal ramp gap widens

**Downside (lower prices / looser market):**
1. **Wind outperformance or early pickup**: If Coastal or South wind develops faster in the HE17-19 window (ahead of STWPF forecast), the solar handoff would be partially cushioned
2. **Extended-holiday demand softness**: Some commercial sites may extend to Tuesday, shaving 500-1,500 MW from expected Monday morning recovery
3. **Outage restoration ahead of schedule**: HE13 outages (6,721 MW) restoring into HE16-17 earlier than expected would improve reserve margin ahead of the ramp
