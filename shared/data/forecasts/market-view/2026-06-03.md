## Key Drivers

- **Demand step-down vs June 1-2**: 24h avg 61,787 MW vs 65,429 MW June 2 (-3,642 MW); load peak 74,263 MW vs ~79,559 MW June 2 (-5,296 MW). The HE21 net-load peak (56,867 MW) is essentially unchanged from June 2 (56,761 MW) because wind is also weaker, but the afternoon shoulder is lower, creating a sharper, more concentrated scarcity peak at HE19-21 rather than a sustained high-load plateau.

- **Wind daytime collapse (HE01 13,049 MW → HE09 2,667 MW) creates brief mid-morning tight period**: Load is ramping (51,616 MW HE07 → 55,348 MW HE09) but wind is at system trough and solar is only partially deployed. Smartbidder captures this as the HE07 price spike ($38.67/MWh). This window is distinct from the evening scarcity event and may create a secondary BESS opportunity or a non-trivial RT spike risk during the charging window.

- **Evening wind recovery (9,421-9,837 MW in HE18-19) limits scarcity depth**: Unlike June 2 where evening wind was weaker, June 3 wind returns to ~9.5-9.8 GW by the HE18-19 ramp onset. GR_COASTAL (1,481 MW HE18) and GR_SOUTH (1,530 MW HE21) contributions are additive to the system. This is the primary structural reason the HE21 DA peak ($48.39/MWh) is lower than a structurally similar but lower-wind day would produce.

- **Elevated outage (13,351 MW avg, 12,448 MW at HE21) tightens effective reserve**: More units offline than June 2 despite softer demand means the grid is closer to scarcity pricing at the HE21 net-load peak. Non-Spin spike to $31.82/MWh at HE21 reflects this — ORDC adder conditions are plausible.
