## Key Drivers

- **Solar-to-wind transition gap**: The 2-hour window HE18→HE20 is structurally the most exposed period: solar drops 24,002 → 775 MW (-23,227 MW), wind only gains 8,396 → 11,100 MW (+2,705 MW), while load drops modestly 83,780 → 78,819 MW. Net load spike of +17 GW is driven almost entirely by solar exit, not load. This is the classic "duck curve" ramp that BESS is positioned to serve.
- **Thermal outage level at 9 GW**: Late-summer scheduled maintenance keeps capacity offline through the morning (9,267 MW at HE01–HE07) with partial recovery by late evening (7,905 MW at HE24). Net effective thermal capacity approximately 75–76 GW against an 84.6 GW peak — reserve margin in the 10–12% range, not alarming but not comfortable if any unplanned trips occur.
- **Wind trough during afternoon peak**: GR_WEST at only 2,925 MW (HE13) rising to 3,596 MW by HE17 — essentially at diurnal minimum when the AC load is peaking. This removes the wind cushion that would otherwise moderate prices during HE15–HE18.
- **September end-of-summer conditions**: Dallas overnight lows ~82–87°F (KDFW AG2 weather), daytime highs likely near 95–98°F in West Texas areas. AC load remains significant but declining seasonally from August peaks. Load profile consistent with a typical late-September hot weekday — peak load below August extremes but still elevated.
