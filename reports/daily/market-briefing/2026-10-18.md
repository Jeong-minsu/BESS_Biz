## Key Drivers

1. **October duck curve amplitude**: With 31.8 GW solar at midday and evening load still near peak (51.6-52.0 GW), the net load ramp of 30.9 GW over 7 hours is the dominant structural force. This creates the extreme DA price differential between midday and evening.

2. **25 GW thermal outage block**: Autumn maintenance window is compressing thermal headroom. During the HE17-21 ramp, every available thermal unit is needed. Outage levels are highest at HE13-14 (25,666 MW) when solar is still covering demand, but units scheduled to return online by HE20-22 are critical. Any outage extension into the evening ramp amplifies upside price risk.

3. **GR_SOUTH wind collapse HE15-17**: GR_SOUTH wind drops to 58-66 MW in HE15-17, coinciding with the highest P(RT>DA) window. This creates south-zone congestion pressure at the exact moment load is building and solar is fading. Refer to congestion-analyst for LCRA/South constraint binding probability and MCC spread impact on GKS node.

4. **Solar forecast divergence (Yes Energy vs Enverus)**: 2,300 MW spread at peak introduces uncertainty into the depth of the midday price trough and the speed of recovery. Yes Energy's deeper solar view implies a lower midday floor (~$17.96 DA) and potentially more aggressive price recovery slope. If actual solar is closer to Enverus estimate, midday prices may settle slightly higher ($19-22 range) and the net load ramp at HE18-19 may be marginally smoother.

5. **Load forecast gap**: 3,375 MW gap between Yes Energy (51,987 MW) and Enverus (48,612 MW) at HE20 is a material uncertainty for the peak-hour price level. Upside load scenario (nearer YE) is directionally consistent with upside DA price risk at HE19-20 beyond the Tyba P50.
