## Key Drivers

- **Strong wind + solar combination suppresses mid-day prices:** The combined 17.9 GW wind average plus peak 27.3 GW solar crushes net load to 19.5 GW at HE10 — the lowest net load of any weekday this week. DA prices below $20 for HE9-10 reflect the market pricing in this renewable surplus; BESS charging window is clearly defined (HE8-11, sub-$20 DA).

- **Tight HE19-22 solar-exit ramp is the primary risk window:** A 20.3 GW net load increase over 3 hours (HE18→HE21) with GR_WEST wind still in its afternoon trough makes this the highest uncertainty period. Smartbidder P(DA<RT)=0.63 at HE19-21 signals modest but actionable RT upside. Non-Spin and ECRS DA forecasts of $12-13/MWh and $3.3/MWh at HE21-22 suggest the market is already pricing some scarcity probability into those hours.

- **Outage improvement:** 10,712 MW average outage (down ~1,600 MW from yesterday's 12,337 MW) means reserve margins are meaningfully wider. This reduces the probability of extreme RT spikes even in the HE19-22 ramp window, consistent with the relatively contained DA price of ~$32/MWh at the peak rather than $100+.

- **Saturday demand softness:** Load peaks at 76.3 GW vs. prior day's 77.8 GW and the 24h mean is ~1.9 GW lower. The Saturday industrial demand reduction gives the market additional buffer vs. workweek conditions, further containing price extremes.

## Risks / Watch

- **Evening ramp concentration (HE19-22):** The 20.3 GW net load ramp over 3 hours is the single highest execution-risk window. RT spike if wind acceleration lags or solar exits faster than the 843 MW HE21 forecast. Primary BESS discharge window. AS value (Non-Spin $12-13, ECRS $3.3/MWh DA forecast) makes this the AS-heavy window as well.
