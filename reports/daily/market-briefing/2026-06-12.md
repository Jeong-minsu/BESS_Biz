## Key Drivers

- **Evening ramp concentration:** The dominant risk is the HE20–HE21 solar cliff (23.5 GW collapse) coinciding with the peak thermal demand shoulder. Net load spikes 5.1 GW in one hour. This is the single largest intra-day transition and is where RT pricing can deviate sharply from DA if thermal response or wind acceleration is delayed.

- **GR_WEST afternoon softness:** Wind drops to 3,129 MW at HE13 and is only 6,674 MW at HE17 — well below the overnight potential of 12,755 MW. During the late-afternoon peak window (HE15–HE19) when load is at its highest (77+ GW), the primary wind relief is still ramping, keeping thermal on the margin longer and sustaining price pressure.

- **Mild temperature vs. heavy outage:** DFW temperatures for 06/12 forecast at 86–88°F (well below the 95°F+ threshold for reserve alerts) and Houston IAH similar. Load is strong at 77.8 GW peak but reserve stress is not acute at these temperatures. The 12.3 GW outage stack is the more significant structural constraint.

- **Solar vs. Enverus disagreement:** Enverus solar STPF peaks ~1,800 MW higher than Yes Energy COPHSL bidclose, and the Enverus net load is ~1,700 MW above Yes Energy at the 24h mean level. If Enverus is correct, the mid-day net load floor is lower (~33.8 GW vs. 35.5 GW) and the solar exit ramp is correspondingly steeper. This has bearish DA mid-day implications and would amplify the HE20–21 transition spike risk.
