## Key Drivers

1. **Seasonal regime shift:** First full month post-summer. Texas cooling load is minimal; electric heat has not yet ramped. Load at 53.7 GW is ~17 GW below peak-summer levels. This creates abundant headroom for renewables to exceed demand midday.

2. **GR_WEST wind strength with Coastal/South variable:** GR_WEST runs strong most of the day at 16.4 GW avg. Coastal and South wind zones are both weak overnight (172 MW and 760 MW minimums respectively) but recover in the afternoon. GR_WEST late-day decline (from 18.8 GW at HE5/6 to 9.9 GW at HE24) coincides with the evening reserve tightening and is a key driver of the NSPIN spike.

3. **23 GW thermal outages:** High maintenance season outages limit the thermal fleet's ability to cover the post-solar ramp. If the ramp is sharper than expected (closer to Yes Energy's +30 GW scenario), reserve margins could tighten further and drive RT above Tyba P50 in HE18-20.

4. **Evening ramp + reserve risk:** The solar-to-thermal handoff at HE16-18 coincides with the highest P(RT>DA) window. GR_COASTAL wind also collapses near-zero at HE2-3 and is variable in the evening — any unexpected weakness adds RT upside pressure in SOUTH/HOUSTON settlement points.

5. **Solar forecast divergence (5.3 GW at peak):** This is the largest model disagreement and the primary source of uncertainty. Yes Energy's bullish solar view implies curtailment risk and potential REGDN congestion midday. Enverus's more conservative view keeps prices positive. Until actual solar output is observable post-sunrise, the BESS charging window (HE10-14) should be approached as a distribution with tails in both directions.
