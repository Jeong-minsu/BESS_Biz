## Key Drivers

- **Series-record midday duck (28,394 MW net load at HE10) is driven by convergence of peak solar and improved wind**: Solar peaks at 33,090 MW (HE12) — the series maximum. Wind at HE10 = 5,234 MW provides material additional displacement that was absent on Days 7-9 (1,821-2,086 MW). Load at HE10 = 63,821 MW is the lowest Saturday load reading of this heat wave period. All three factors converge to drive net thermal residual to territory not seen in ERCOT during peak summer since large-scale solar deployment began. Negative pricing at HE10-11 is not just likely — it is structurally overwhelmingly probable without Smartbidder quantification.

- **Series-record solar cliff (-12,868 MW) + series-record 1-hour net load surge (+9,094 MW) at HE19→HE20 is the primary RT risk**: Despite stronger wind buffering at HE20 (12,478 MW vs Day 9's 9,714 MW), the thermal ramp requirement of +9,094 MW in a single hour with 9,353 MW of capacity offline is the most severe dispatch stress of the heat wave. GR_COASTAL, the primary short-term buffer, provides only 1,903 MW at HE19 and 1,646 MW at HE20 — declining from its HE18 peak of 1,981 MW at precisely the wrong moment. Any thermal unit failure during HE19-20 will remove supply from an already-constrained window. BESS operators should treat HE20 as the primary RT spike risk hour.

- **Cap out rebound to 9,419 MW midday negates Day 9's planned-return narrative**: Day 9's declining cap out trajectory (8,262 → 6,971 MW) was interpreted as planned maintenance unit returns. Day 10's HE01 = 9,020 MW and HE08-16 = 9,419 MW represents a 2,049-2,448 MW increase from Day 9's final hours. Three explanations are possible: (a) units that returned on Day 9 evening tripped again overnight under sustained thermal stress; (b) Saturday planned maintenance registrations for units that were running through the weekday peak overwhelmed the returning units; (c) new heat-stress failures developed overnight. Regardless of cause, available thermal capacity at HE20 is constrained by the highest cap out of the series. The combination of high cap out and the series-record ramp requirement creates the highest reserve adequacy risk of the entire event.

- **GR_WEST overnight recovery validates Day 9 forecast and signals ridge weakening**: Day 9 BidClose forecast GR_WEST HE24 = 7,991 MW. Day 10 HE01 = 7,608 MW in the carryover — within 383 MW of the forecast, confirming that the evening ramp anticipated on Day 9 did materialize. Day 10 GR_WEST HE24 = 12,573 MW represents a further extension of the recovery. This is the strongest evidence yet that the anticyclonic ridge is weakening: GR_WEST is producing >12 GW overnight, approaching pre-heat-wave August norms. Midday suppression persists (HE10 floor = 2,572 MW) but is 1,709 MW less severe than Day 9's extreme floor of 863 MW, suggesting partial penetration of sea breeze or synoptic flow into the region during daytime. If this trajectory continues, Day 11 (Sunday Aug 30, second weekend day) could see further midday improvement and a materially different overnight profile.

- **Saturday structure creates the widest DA price corridor of the series**: The combination of deep negative midday ($-60 to $-15/MWh at HE10-11) and elevated evening ($55-95/MWh at HE20) produces a HE10-to-HE20 DA spread of potentially $115-155/MWh — the widest intraday spread of any heat wave day. However, the uncertainty range around both ends of the spread is also the widest (no Smartbidder, single-source data, 8 consecutive cycles without AG2/Enverus cross-validation).
