## Key Drivers

- **Wind halving is the structural pivot of this day**: The 6,199 MW decline in 24h wind average (13,917 → 7,718 MW, -45%) is the dominant driver of every key market variable. It raises the midday net load trough by 9,092 MW (reducing negative price potential), raises the evening net load by 3,304 MW (amplifying scarcity potential), and reduces the coastal buffer at the solar cliff. This is not a localized event — GR_WEST, GR_NORTH, GR_COASTAL, and GR_SOUTH all show synchronous weakness, consistent with a regional ridge or weak pressure gradient event.

- **GR_WEST collapse to 918 MW at HE11**: The second-consecutive day of extreme GR_WEST midday weakness. Yesterday's floor was 2,658 MW; today's is 918 MW — approaching the Aug 21 crisis level. GR_WEST typically constitutes 60-70% of ERCOT overnight wind but falls to less than 1% of ERCOT installed wind capacity by early afternoon. Any transmission constraint on GR_WEST at these low output levels is latent risk that activates when evening wind attempts to ramp (5,006-5,310 MW by HE23-24 — modest recovery but not a strong ramp).

- **Series-record evening net load (72,329 MW at HE21)**: This is the defining risk of the day. With 6,918 MW offline and thermal capacity constrained, the effective operating reserve at HE21 is thin. The sequential load build — HE19 (63,730) → HE20 (71,545) → HE21 (72,329) — means scarcity does not concentrate in a single spike hour but persists for 2-3 hours with peak at HE21. Thermal commitment decisions from the 10:00 CT DAM window will be critical: under-commitment means RT scarcity; over-commitment presses into negative prices at HE10.

- **Solar cliff (HE19→HE20: −10,658 MW) initiates but wind does not offset**: The solar exit is slightly less severe than Tuesday (-10,658 vs -13,384 MW), but the wind recovery at HE20 is only 7,087 MW (vs Tuesday's implied ~14,000 MW) — meaning thermal generation must absorb far more of the transition. GR_COASTAL ramp to 2,471 MW at HE19 (vs 3,250 MW Tuesday) provides less cushion. The net load surge of 7,815 MW in one hour (HE19→HE20) demands rapid online capacity dispatch.

- **Cap out HE07 step-up (7,757 MW) warrants monitoring**: Cap out rises from 7,412 MW (overnight) to 7,757 MW at HE07 — a 345 MW step-up at the start of the business day, before falling back through HE08-16. This pattern is atypical (usually cap out declines or stays flat through the day peak). Possible cause: a unit scheduled to return from maintenance is decommitted again, or a new forced outage registers at HE07. Without Enverus unit-level data, cause is unknown. If this unit remains offline into the evening, it adds to the 6,918 MW evening cap out figure (though the HE07-08 step appears to partially reverse).

- **Heat wave Day 7 — load decay pattern confirmed**: Peak load is 1,633 MW below Tuesday despite being Day 7 of an extreme heat event. The pattern of declining peak (every successive day shows ~1-2 GW lower peak vs prior day) is consistent with progressive demand response activation and building thermal mass accumulation. The decay does not solve the evening scarcity because wind weakness more than compensates in net load terms.
