## Key Drivers

- **Demand step-down vs June 1-2 changes the market structure**: At 61,787 MW avg (YE) vs 65,429 MW on June 2, gross load is meaningfully softer. The net-load peak of 56,867 MW at HE21 is similar to June 2 (56,761 MW at HE21) because both the load decrease and the wind decrease approximately offset, but the afternoon shoulder (HE15-18) is lower, compressing the window in which marginal thermal plants price-set. The result is a shorter, more sharply defined scarcity peak at HE19-21 rather than a broad plateau.

- **Wind daytime collapse is the primary midday price-support driver**: ERCOT wind drops from 13,049 MW at HE01 to a trough of **2,667 MW at HE09** — a swing of over 10 GW within 8 hours, occurring precisely as load is ramping and solar is only partially deployed. The HE07-09 window (wind 2,667-3,835 MW, solar 221-11,631 MW, load 51,616-55,348 MW) creates a brief mid-morning tight period where YE net load exceeds 43,000-47,000 MW with limited renewable support. Smartbidder captures this as the **HE07 price spike at $38.67/MWh**.

- **Evening wind recovery is a meaningful bearish offset to the scarcity peak**: Unlike June 2 where wind was weak through the evening, June 3 wind recovers to 9,421-9,837 MW in HE18-19. This partially supplies the demand surge and limits the net-load peak to 56,867 MW rather than the ~59,000-61,000 MW that would result if wind stayed at its daytime trough. The GR_COASTAL sea breeze (1,458-1,481 MW at HE18-19) provides the earliest contribution. GR_SOUTH (1,463-1,530 MW at HE20-21) sustains support through the price peak. This is a material mitigant vs a scenario with flat wind overnight levels.

- **Elevated outage (13,351 MW avg) tightens effective reserve margin despite soft load**: With 13,970 MW offline at HE09-11 and 12,448 MW still offline at HE21, more capacity is unavailable than on June 2. At the HE21 net-load peak of 56,867 MW with 12,448 MW offline and installed ERCOT capacity of approximately 88-90 GW, effective operating reserve is in the low single-GW range — consistent with ORDC adder territory. This is the structural explanation for the Non-Spin spike to $31.82/MWh at HE21.

- **Bimodal solar sources (Enverus peak 27,002 MW vs YE 23,169 MW) creates midday forecast uncertainty**: Enverus shows a higher, earlier solar peak (+3,833 MW, one hour earlier at HE13 vs YE HE14). If Enverus is correct, the HE11-13 net load is meaningfully lower, price suppression is deeper through midday, and the BESS charging window can extend further into HE12-13. The Enverus solar avg (11,169 MW) exceeds YE (8,995 MW) by over 2 GW — a larger divergence than typically seen in this series and warrants attention. The Enverus solar publisher timestamp (06:45 CT) and YE (12:11 UTC / 07:11 CT) are both pre-DAM, so neither has an obvious vintage advantage.

## Risks / Watch

- **Non-Spin HE21-23 spike severity ($31.82 → $20.33 → $21.34/MWh)**: The AS structure is highly concentrated in the HE21-23 window. If the HE21 outage remains at 12,448 MW and wind recovery is weaker than forecast (GR_WEST is the swing variable), Non-Spin could print above $31.82 and create an ORDC adder cascade. Conversely, a 1-2 GW upside wind surprise in HE21 would compress the AS spike materially. BESS should be positioned to capture the HE21 Non-Spin opportunity.

- **Elevated outage HE09-11 (13,970 MW) — return timing risk**: The peak maintenance window coincides with the solar plateau and should not stress the grid. However, any unit that fails to return before HE17 when load reaches 73,724 MW extends the outage into the price-formation window and steepens the scarcity curve. Monitor ERCOT outage board in the HE15-17 window.

- **Enverus-YE solar peak gap (+3,833 MW)**: Larger than the typical 1,000-2,000 MW divergence seen in prior days. Given that Enverus solar was closer to actuals on June 1 (per prior learnings), the upside solar scenario (deeper midday trough, cheaper charging) is the higher-probability path. If solar prints near Enverus (27 GW vs YE 23 GW), the HE11-13 charging window is meaningfully cheaper. Flag for bess-optimizer: price the two scenarios and weight toward the Enverus solar assumption.

- **Enverus load peak at 72,140 MW vs YE 74,263 MW**: A 2,123 MW gap in the evening peak hour is nontrivial for price setting. Enverus's lower load peak view would imply moderately lower HE17-19 prices than Smartbidder's $35-50/MWh range and a slightly less tight reserve situation. This is partially offset by Enverus's higher wind forecast, which also implies less price stress.

- **DA-RT spread at HE19 (+$15.41) most extreme since briefing series started**: Smartbidder's forecast implies RT will undershoot DA by $15.41 at HE19. While P(DA>RT)=62.6% confirms the direction, the magnitude is notable. Per prior learning (June 1 review), the 60-70% P range has produced reversals. If the wind recovery is stronger than forecast, the HE19 RT price could be softer than the forecast $34.77, widening the DA-RT spread further. If wind is weaker, RT could spike above DA, flipping the signal. The HE19 DA price of $50.18/MWh is also the second-highest DA hour — a risk for any long-DA position.
