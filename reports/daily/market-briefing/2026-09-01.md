- **Wind retreat (-4,160 MW avg vs Aug 30) is the dominant structural change from yesterday**: The anticyclonic suppression partially reasserts. GR_WEST falls to a midday floor of 2,988 MW (HE15) — almost exactly the same level as the heat wave's worst suppression days (Days 7-10 midday floors were 863-2,572 MW; today at 2,988 MW is similar in character). This means the afternoon supply cushion that yesterday made the evening ramp manageable is now gone. The HE20 wind buffer is 12,430 MW (vs 16,748 MW yesterday) — a 4,318 MW reduction at exactly the most critical hour.

- **Net load HE20 = 64,515 MW is the tightest thermal dispatch requirement of the week**: With solar essentially gone (2,576 MW) and wind at 12,430 MW, approximately 63-65 GW must come from thermal + storage resources against a backdrop of 7,925 MW offline. The single-hour ramp +6,385 MW (HE19→HE20) with limited wind acceleration represents a dispatch coordination challenge. Cap out declining through the evening (8,784 → 7,845 MW over 24 hours) is a positive signal, but returns are slow and gradual.

- **First full commercial weekday of the post-heat-wave transition adds morning load without the wind recovery of Day 11**: Commercial A/C load ramps sharply from HE5 (59,404 MW) through HE16 (85,477 MW) = +26,073 MW. The heat wave's residual thermal demand (sustained dry soil heat, elevated building thermal mass from 11+ days of 90°F+ temperatures) means the commercial cooling load ramps harder than a typical Tuesday — baseline effective temperature likely remains 2-3°F above climatological September 1 normal.

- **GR_COASTAL sea breeze peaks exactly at HE18-19 (3,058-3,141 MW) — useful but limited**: The coastal sea breeze ramps helpfully at the onset of the solar cliff (HE18) and peaks at HE19, providing 3.1 GW of cover at the highest-stress ramp onset. However, it declines to 2,930 MW at HE20 and 2,566 MW at HE21. The net coastal contribution at HE20 is significant but not enough to offset the 4,318 MW shortfall from GR_WEST underperformance vs yesterday.

- **Thermal outage improvement (-3 GW) provides meaningful reserve restoration**: The drop from 11,058-11,298 MW (Aug 30) to 7,845-8,784 MW (Sep 1) restores approximately 3 GW of thermal capacity — the equivalent of a large combined-cycle block returning. This is the primary reason HE20 does not require ORDC scarcity pricing under the base case (adequate headroom of ~2-7 GW at HE20). However, the improvement is incremental (units returning during Sep 1 daytime) and any forced outage event during the cliff hour could still breach the scarcity threshold.
