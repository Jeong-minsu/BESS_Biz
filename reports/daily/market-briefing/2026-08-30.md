## Key Drivers

- **Anticyclonic ridge fully retreating — wind recovery is the structural inflection of the heat wave**: The ridge's midday suppression of GR_WEST wind (which drove 863-2,572 MW midday floors on Days 7-10) is gone on Day 11: GR_WEST floor = 6,209 MW at HE15-16. Total ERCOT wind never drops below 10,916 MW (HE14) on Sunday, versus Day 10's midday floor of 5,234 MW. This structural shift means the primary heat wave market dynamic — extreme midday duck curve followed by severe evening ramp — persists in duration but is mitigated on the charge-price depth at midday (net load 19,918 MW is still extreme but wind makes the evening ramp less unmanageable) and the evening price ceiling (wind at 16,748 MW at HE20 limits the scarcity premium vs what would have occurred without recovery).

- **Net load trough of 19,918 MW (HE10) is likely without precedent for an ERCOT peak-summer day**: To contextualize: pre-heat-wave August peak-summer days typically trough at 35-50 GW net load. Day 7 of this heat wave (the prior non-heat-wave-context record) was 33,816 MW. Day 10 was 28,394 MW. Day 11 is 19,918 MW. At this level, every dispatchable generator that can reduce to zero output will do so; thermal units committed for the HE20 ramp will be at minimum generation, exporting negative-valued energy into a system with 42 GW of renewable production. The LaaR/ERS floor behavior and committed minimum-load obligations will dominate midday price formation. Negative prices across the full HE09-13 window are structurally certain.

- **Series-record solar cliff (-14,349 MW HE19→HE20) + record net load surge (+11,169 MW) against series-record cap out (11,058 MW) = the highest single-hour dispatch stress in this heat wave**: Despite wind at 16,748 MW at HE20 providing more buffer than any prior cliff event (Day 10 HE20: 12,478 MW, Day 9: 9,714 MW), the sheer scale of the one-hour transition is the dominant risk. ERCOT must ramp committed thermal units ~11 GW in 60 minutes while the highest thermal outage of the series (11,058 MW) limits available headroom. GR_COASTAL peaks at HE18 (2,808 MW) and is declining at HE19-20 (2,748 → 2,486 MW) — the coastal sea breeze provides its best buffer just before the cliff but wanes during the critical window. GR_SOUTH builds steadily through HE21-22 (2,515-2,571 MW) but does not peak until after the crisis hour.

- **Sunday demand structure: load peak shifts one hour later (HE18 vs HE17 Saturday), morning trough slightly lower (HE08 = 55,373 MW vs HE07 = 55,918 MW yesterday)**: The absence of commercial/industrial morning demand on a second consecutive weekend day deepens the morning trough slightly vs Saturday. Afternoon and evening load is almost identical (within 300 MW hour-for-hour vs Day 10 for HE14-22), confirming that the heat wave load is now dominated by residential cooling load that is agnostic to weekday/weekend distinction. The later peak timing (HE18 vs HE17) creates a marginally wider window for solar + wind to hold net load below 45 GW in shoulder hours.

- **Cap out jump to 11,293-11,298 MW (+1,961 MW vs Day 10's prior series record) is the primary thermal risk multiplier**: Three likely contributing factors: (1) units that ran continuously through the weekday heat wave and through Saturday are registering Sunday planned maintenance outages; (2) heat-stress-induced forced outages that were not apparent until Saturday night; (3) possible failed starts on units that were offline and attempted restart during Day 10 planned returns. The slight declining trajectory in the evening (11,298 → 11,058 MW from HE07 to HE19-24, -240 MW) is a mild positive signal, indicating some partial returns are still scheduled for Sunday afternoon, but the pace is far slower than what would be needed to materially change the reserve picture for HE20.
