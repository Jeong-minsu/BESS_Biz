Any unplanned forced outage in HE17-21 could spike net load well above the 48,887 MW YE peak, activating ORDC scarcity pricing above the modeled $39/MWh.

- **Wind front creates a bifurcated intraday price structure — cheap overnight, moderately expensive evening**: The overnight wind peak (HE01 = 16,530 MW) drives DA prices to $21-23/MWh in HE02-04. As wind fades through the morning and solar ramps up, prices suppress through HE07-12 ($23-26/MWh). The late-day wind recovery (GR_WEST building from 3,635 MW at HE14 to ~4,855 MW at HE17 and GR_COASTAL sea-breeze HE18-19 = 1,653 MW) partially offsets the solar exit, moderating the evening price spike relative to a wind-flat scenario. For BESS: the overnight-to-peak arbitrage is the cleanest spread (HE02-03 charging at ~$22-23/MWh, HE20-21 discharging at ~$37-39/MWh — approximately **$15-17/MWh raw spread**).

- **Net-load peak timing is HE21 (YE: 48,887 MW) vs HE20-21 (Enverus: 51,602 MW at HE21)**: The Enverus net-load peak is +2,715 MW above YE at HE21, driven by Enverus seeing lower evening wind combined with higher midday solar (which exits earlier, leaving more thermal obligation by HE19-21). The DA price peak at HE20 = $39.40/MWh (Smartbidder) aligns better with Enverus's view of HE20-21 tightness than with YE's HE17-based gross load peak. Watch whether the Enverus HE20 solar exit pattern (Enverus solar = 4,419 MW at HE20 per YE, but Enverus's higher solar means the exit is 1h earlier and more dramatic) triggers a repeat of the HE20-vs-HE21 peak shift observed in the June 1-3 cycle.

- **Friday demand step-down narrows the scarcity window**: At 69,097 MW peak vs 72,316 MW Thursday, the demand side is approximately 3,200 MW softer. This reduces the probability of a deep HE20-21 scarcity event. Smartbidder's DA peak of $39.40/MWh is ~$9/MWh below Thursday's $48.62/MWh peak, consistent with the demand reduction plus the higher wind backing. The P(DA>RT) at 67% for HE20 (vs 65% Thursday HE21) is similar, suggesting the structural DA premium in the evening window is unchanged even as the absolute price level is lower.

- **GR_COASTAL sea-breeze timing (HE18 peak = 1,653 MW) provides a brief moderating offset during the price ramp**: The GR_COASTAL wind surge at HE18 (1,653 MW, the highest coastal hour) coincides with the early evening price ramp, suppressing the ramp-onset price by an estimated 100-200 MW of net-load relief.
