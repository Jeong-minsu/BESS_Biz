## Key Decision Rationale (5 bullets)

1. **Full midday charge (HE10-11)**: Net load at 31,061 MW with 26,892 MW solar flood is the lowest-cost charging window of the day. GKS MCC −$1.5/MWh adds further discount. P(duck binding 20-30%) means there is a real chance prices approach zero or negative — charging here locks in the best economics.

2. **Concentrated discharge HE19-20 (not HE17-18)**: HE20 net load at 66,002 MW is the highest of the day; solar completely exits (775 MW vs 24,002 MW at HE18). The 2-hour "duck tail" HE19-20 is structurally the highest-value window. HE17-18 net load (45,618-49,024 MW) is meaningfully lower; spending SoC early dilutes peak capture.

3. **Full DA commitment at peak (100 MW HE19 + 70 MW HE20)**: Sep 16 showed that under-bidding DA while physically dispatching creates catastrophic RT imbalance when RT >> DA. With 170 MWh deliverable from full SoC, setting DA bid = physical capability eliminates imbalance risk entirely.

4. **ECRS (not NonSpin) during idle HE12-18**: Sep 16 ECRS cleared at $760/$600/$388/MW-h (HE22-24) vs NonSpin at $23-280/MW-h. ECRS is the higher-value product for a battery with full SoC and 30-min response capability. Wind trough (GR_WEST minimum 2,925-4,109 MW HE12-18) structurally tightens the AS market, supporting ECRS clearing.

5. **Skip overnight charging/AS**: Sep 16 overnight RT was $39-67/MWh (expensive); charging in RT overnight was the root cause of the battery entering peak discharge with inadequate SoC and expensive basis cost. Sep 18 DA bid for HE10-11 at estimated $13-15/MWh effective is far superior to overnight RT charging.
