**Spread convention**: spread = DA − RT. Positive → DA expensive → short DA / long RT.

**Energy spread (HE10)**:
- No Smartbidder P(DA>RT) available. Aug 20 realized spread: +$3.57/MWh avg (DA slightly above RT).
- For HE10: charging at DA price in the negative zone means DA is likely at parity with or below RT (which also goes negative). Strategy: commit DA charge. If DA and RT are both negative, DA commitment ensures we capture the negative price; RT imbalance would be neutral or small.
- Bid: charge at ≤ $15/MWh in DA. This is a conservative ceiling; structural case supports negative clearing.

**Energy spread (HE20-21)**:
- Structural case (Yes Energy + AG2 aligned): RT likely equals or exceeds DA at HE20-21 due to rapid net load ramp (RT prices more volatile). Spread = DA - RT likely negative at HE20 (DA < RT at scarcity events).
- Decision: Commit primarily to DA for certainty. HE20 at 100 MW ensures execution floor at $70+. If RT > DA at HE20, we lose optionality — but avoid the risk of RT miss (no DA commitment = lower floor).
- HE21 at 75 MW DA: conservative approach leaving SoC fully depleted. If RT premium is large (>$30 above DA), this will underperform Stack C (RT-only). Accept this risk.

**ECRS spread (DA vs RT)**:
- Aug 20 showed ECRS revenue concentrated in overnight/late-evening hours, consistent with DA ECRS clearing + potential RT deployment.
- DA ECRS offering is recommended: provides clearing certainty and baseline revenue. RT ECRS residual captures tail events.
- No AS spread data (Smartbidder degraded). Aug 20 pattern is used as the prior: ECRS DA preferred.

**Overall DA-RT signal**: Weak DA-over-RT signal (no Smartbidder). Commit energy to DA for execution certainty given the extreme structural case. ECRS in DA for clearing certainty.

## Decision Rationale Summary

1. **Why HE10 charge and not HE09**: HE10 net load (32,947 MW) is 4,373 MW lower than HE09 (37,320 MW). Saturday + solar saturation makes HE10 the structurally negative hour. Charging at HE09 ($5-15 est.) instead of HE10 ($-15 to $0 est.) costs $500-1,500 more with no SoC benefit (100 MW max same either hour). HE10 is clearly superior.

2. **Why discharge at HE21 (75 MW) and not HE19 (0 MW)**: At P50 prices ($88/MWh HE19 vs $72/MWh HE21), the marginal value comparison is HE19 50 MW × $88 = $4,400 (if we discharge there instead of HE21) vs HE21 75 MW × $72 = $5,400. HE21 wins by $1,000 at P50. If HE20 solar cliff is severe and HE21 sustains high prices, this margin grows. If HE19 prices spike >$108, the calculus reverses — adjust intraday via RT optionality.

3. **Why 80 MW ECRS (not 85 or 100 MW)**: 80 MW leaves headroom in the 100 MW inverter capacity for settlement/imbalance handling and avoids over-committing. Aug 20 realized 77-85 MW; 80 MW is consistent with the observed dispatch pattern.

4. **Why no Reg-Up/Down**: Regulation requires sustained SoC management around a setpoint and frequent subhour dispatch, which conflicts with the large energy arbitrage plan (full charge → full discharge within 12 hours). Reg-Up/Down opportunity cost is negative given the energy stack design.

5. **Why no RRS**: Historically non-clearing for GKS. Aug 20 showed 0 MW RRS. Not included.
