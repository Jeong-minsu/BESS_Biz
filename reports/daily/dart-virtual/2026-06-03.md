## Risks

1. **HE19 reversal risk — wide spread, lower P**: The +$15.41 raw spread at P=62.6% is anomalously wide. If the DA market reflects a structural mis-pricing (e.g., DA bidders over-priced the evening ramp in the auction), RT could also be elevated (wind underperformance), narrowing the realized spread. Prior learning: reversals occur in the 60-70% P range. Adverse scenario: realized spread at HE19 = −$5 to $0 (RT spike). Capped loss at 50 MW × $5/MWh = $250.

2. **HOUSTON_IMPORT congestion at HE20-22 (22-30% P)**: If HOUSTON_IMPORT binds, RT at HB_HOUSTON rises above the hub average by +$3 to +$14 conditional on binding. E[adverse MCC impact] = ~$1.6/MWh across HE20-22. At 50 MW × 3 hours × $1.6/MWh = $240 adverse impact — small relative to gross EV but non-trivial. Stage 0 heuristic uncertainty retained.

3. **GR_WEST wind overperformance during HE19-21**: If wind realizes above the AG2 West 5,520-5,541 MW forecast (e.g., 6,500+ MW), RT prices are suppressed, which widens the DA-RT spread further (favorable) — this is asymmetric upside. However, if wind underperforms (4,500 MW or below), RT spikes and spread narrows or inverts. The 2026-06-03 wind trough (HE09 = 2,667 MW) is the lowest since HE06, so wind recovery reliability through HE19-21 matters.

4. **Load peak uncertainty — Enverus 72,140 MW vs Yes Energy 74,263 MW at HE17**: The 2,123 MW divergence creates HE17-18 price uncertainty. Enverus view implies moderately softer HE17-18 DA prices, which could reduce the pre-ramp spread at HE17-18. Impact: primarily on MED positions (HE17, HE18) — potential for HE17 spread to be +$4-5 instead of +$6.26, borderline MED/skip territory.

5. **Non-Spin HE21-23 spike severity**: Non-Spin spiking to $31.82/MWh at HE21 indicates potential scarcity condition. If the scarcity is severe enough that SCED dispatch triggers emergency pricing (RT energy price = System Lambda + ORDC ≈ AS prices), RT at HE21 could approach or exceed DA ($48.39). P(RT>DA at HE21) = 1 − 67.8% = 32.2% — non-trivial. However, Smartbidder's P(DA>RT)=67.8% already incorporates AS spike probability; this risk is partially embedded in the P estimate.

6. **Data vintage / Tenaska unavailable (10th cycle)**: No confirmed RT settlement data for model recalibration. +20% bias correction and P estimates derived from structural model without empirical validation for 10 consecutive cycles. Hit rate and win:loss ratio remain proxy-based.

7. **Solar source divergence at HE20 (Enverus 6,115 MW vs Yes Energy 4,555 MW)**: Smaller divergence than yesterday's 4,223 MW gap, but still meaningful. If Enverus is correct (higher solar at HE20), net load at HE20 is lower → weaker spread. Market briefing notes Enverus solar has been more accurate historically. Directional impact: more solar = tighter HE20 spread. Partially offsetting: higher solar at HE20 also means a steeper cliff at HE21 (from 6,115 to 544 MW), strengthening HE21.
