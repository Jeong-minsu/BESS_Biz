## Why (Broader Thesis)

### Midday Positions (HE09-14 at HB_WEST): NEW REGIME — West Surplus DART

Today is the first cycle in the 16-cycle tracked series where a SHORT DA / LONG RT position at HB_WEST during the midday window is recommended. The structural basis:

1. SCI exceeds 1.0 throughout HE09-15 — VRE exceeds net load in aggregate. This is a physical overgeneration condition requiring either curtailment or export. WEST_TO_NORTH is the primary export path. When the export path saturates (binds), West-node RT prices decline toward zero or go negative. DA at HB_WEST, set the prior day in the DAM, will have priced the overgeneration partially (DA prices are $16-20/MWh) but cannot go below zero (in a simplified forward market). RT at the West hub will reflect the actual binding — potentially $0-8/MWh. The spread = DA_HB_WEST − RT_HB_WEST = positive, SHORT DA signal.

2. The congestion-analyst explicitly designated "Short-DA-WEST / Long-RT-WEST for HE09-15" as the primary DART signal of the cycle. This is the first time the congestion-analyst has issued a primary DART directional recommendation for the West hub. The HIGH binding probability (40-60%) is the highest constraint probability in the 16-cycle series.

3. The P estimates at HB_WEST (62-66%) are derived by applying a congestion uplift to the system P(DA>RT) (system values: 0.577-0.600 in the midday window). At 40-60% WEST_TO_NORTH binding probability, a simple Bayesian adjustment: P(DA>RT at HB_WEST) ≈ P(system DA>RT) × P(no binding) + 1.0 × P(binding) ≈ 0.590 × 0.50 + 1.0 × 0.50 = 0.795 (upper) or 0.590 × 0.60 + 1.0 × 0.40 = 0.754 (lower). These are upper bounds. A more conservative adjustment that accounts for partial binding (shadow price positive but RT not at zero) gives P(HB_WEST DA>RT) in the 62-68% range. The conservative 62-66% range used in the table is appropriate.

4. Sizing rationale: HE11 (peak SCI) and HE10, HE12 (near-peak) are HIGH 50 MW. HE09, HE13, HE14 are MED 25 MW because they are at the edges of the SCI peak (lower binding probability at the ramp-in and ramp-out of the surplus window).

## Risk Points

### Risk 1: HB_WEST spread estimates may overstate the actual DA-RT divergence (PRIMARY RISK for new positions)

**Nature**: The spread estimates for HE09-14 at HB_WEST are model-derived from the congestion-analyst's MCC projections. Smartbidder does not provide HB_WEST nodal spreads. The E[MCC HB_WEST] = -$7.0/MWh (range -$1.5 to -$20.0) is a Stage 0 heuristic estimate — no PTDF matrix, no historical hub-pair LMP calibration. The spread estimates (+$6 to +$12) could be overstated if: (a) ERCOT curtails wind before the constraint reaches its thermal limit (reducing RT impact), (b) shadow price is lower than estimated because the constraint binds partially, or (c) DA prices at HB_WEST are also suppressed via DA nodal adjustment (reducing the spread even if RT is low).

**Mitigation**: MED sizing at HE09, HE13, HE14 limits exposure at the tails of the SCI window. The 0.80 confidence factor applied to HB_WEST positions reduces the risk-adjusted EV to account for this estimation uncertainty. The direction (SHORT DA) is unlikely to be wrong given the physical conditions — the main uncertainty is spread magnitude.

**Stop-loss consideration**: If ERCOT reports wind curtailment notices in real-time before 10:00 CT (DAM cutoff), it indicates the constraint may bind with lower shadow price than assumed. In that scenario, consider reducing HE11-12 to MED (25 MW) each.

### Risk 2: WEST_TO_NORTH constraint does not bind — RT stays close to system level

**Nature**: If the WEST_TO_NORTH constraint does NOT bind during HE09-15 despite the extreme SCI reading, RT at HB_WEST will be near the system RT level (~$14-17/MWh).
