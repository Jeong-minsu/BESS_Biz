## Headline

July 12 (Sunday) is a high-consequence summer peak day. Three converging factors drive elevated
congestion risk:

1. **West wind surplus overnight → West export path stress (HE1-6)**: GR_WEST wind forecast at
   8,933 MW at HE1, declining to ~4,380 MW by HE6. West zone generation (wind + thermal) is
   likely to press against WEST-to-NORTH transfer limits through the pre-dawn hours.

2. **Solar-driven deep duck curve midday (HE10-14)**: System net load bottoms at ~32,322 MW
   (Yes Energy) / ~31,644 MW (Enverus) around HE10. West wind simultaneously at its nadir
   (~1,834 MW HE10). Solar concentrated in South/West zones creates export pressure southward
   with potential South-to-Houston import constraint binding.

3. **Evening peak stress — very tight reserves (HE17-21)**: Load peaks at ~78,259 MW (Yes Energy)
   / ~76,312 MW (Enverus) around HE17-18. Solar drops from ~24,558 MW (HE16) to 0 by HE21
   while net load surges to ~59,381 MW — the highest net load of the day. With 7,304 MW of
   forced outages system-wide, thermal headroom is estimated at ~3,300-5,000 MW above net load.
   Houston-area import constraints and North Central import paths are at highest binding risk.

## Net Load Ramp Analysis

Large ramps stress the grid and increase constraint binding probability at transition hours.

**Morning solar ramp (load-decreasing):**
- HE8→HE9: Net load -7,745 MW (Yes Energy) in 1 hour — fastest ramp of the day
  (solar surges from 6,766 to 19,901 MW per Enverus)
- HE9→HE10: Net load -3,171 MW additional
- Risk: voltage regulation and reactive power constraints on fast solar zones

**Evening ramp (load-increasing):**
- HE18→HE19: Net load +1,292 MW (modest — solar still 10,145 MW)
- HE19→HE20: Net load +4,768 MW (Yes Energy) / +7,054 MW (Enverus) — solar crashes from
  10,145 MW to 1,184 MW per Enverus; worst single-hour stress
- HE20→HE21: Net load +4,107 MW (Yes Energy) — solar reaches zero; wind must absorb ramp
- HE19-21 combined: +8,875 MW net load increase over 2 hours per Yes Energy

The HE19-21 window is the highest binding constraint risk period of the day.

## Risks

1. **West wind forecast bust (downside)**: GR_WEST STWPF shows 8,933 MW HE1. If actual wind is
   -1,500 MW vs forecast, overnight WEST_TO_NORTH binding probability drops from 25-35% to 10-20%
   but evening reserve margin tightens further.

2. **Load forecast divergence (Yes Energy vs Enverus)**: Peak hour discrepancy of ~1,900-4,800 MW
   between YE (78,259 MW) and Enverus (76,312 MW). If YE forecast materializes, evening constraint
   binding risk escalates by ~5-10 percentage points across all periods.

3. **7,304 MW forced outages**: This is the sustained outage level across most of the day.
   If any additional unit trips during HE17-21 evening peak, reserve margin falls below 1,500 MW
   and price spike / scarcity events become likely — constraint shadow prices could be extreme.

4. **Solar over-performance midday**: Enverus solar peak is 29,309 MW vs Yes Energy 26,875 MW
   (+2,434 MW divergence). If solar exceeds forecast in West/South zones, midday WEST export
   constraint binding probability increases and MCC for West hub deepens negative.

5. **Evening wind ramp timing**: Enverus wind recovers from 2,592 MW (HE11) to 10,741 MW (HE19).
   If this recovery is delayed by 1-2 hours, the HE19-21 stress window extends and HOUSTON_IMPORT
   binding duration increases.
