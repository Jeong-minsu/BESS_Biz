## Headline

2026-06-22 is a **high-congestion-risk weekday** with three concurrent stress factors:
1. **Extreme load peak**: 81,081 MW (YE BIDCLOSE HE17) — among the highest in the June series; summer weekday AC load at full strength.
2. **Severe evening net-load ramp**: HE18→HE21 net load rises from 34,560 MW to 54,199 MW (YE), a +19,639 MW ramp — exceeding the series record set on June 21 (+18,627 MW). Solar exit is abrupt and wind recovery is partial during this window.
3. **Heavy thermal outage loading**: ~11,300–12,550 MW out (YE TOTAL_RESOURCE_CAP_OUT), with peak outage loading HE04 at 12,550 MW, persisting above 11,300 MW for most of the operating day.

**Primary congestion driver**: HOUSTON_IMPORT constraints (south-to-Houston transmission paths) during the HE19-22 evening ramp. The NL ramp of +19,639 MW materially exceeds the ~9,000 MW MEDIUM threshold used in this series. Per the 1.8x right-tail calibration rule (active when NL ramp condition is met — Smartbidder Non-Spin DA unavailable this cycle to verify the second condition), HOUSTON_IMPORT is the highest-priority constraint.

**Secondary driver**: WEST_TO_NORTH — overnight wind GR_WEST (YE BIDCLOSE) declines from ~17,575 MW at HE01 to a daytime trough of ~6,911 MW at HE14, then recovers to ~10,958 MW at HE21. The overnight HE23-HE24 window shows GR_WEST ~12,027–12,272 MW (YE) with NL at 47,969–43,546 MW — SCI ~0.25-0.28. This is the LOW threshold zone; lower confidence than the June 17 overnight event (GR_WEST 16,805 MW).

### HOUSTON_IMPORT Commentary

The NL ramp of +19,639 MW (HE18→HE21, YE) is the most extreme ramp in the current 31-cycle series. For context:
- Prior series high: 2026-06-21 (solstice) at +18,627 MW — which drove a HIGH (38-55%) call
- June 18 (record load day): NL ramp +17,935 MW → HOUSTON_IMPORT HIGH (35-50%)

The +19,639 MW ramp surpasses both prior HIGH-tier events by a margin. However, the MEDIUM threshold is calibrated at 9,000 MW and the heuristic P(binding) scale must acknowledge that very large ramps concentrate load-following on South Texas and Gulf Coast generators, pushing flows above Houston path limits. The HIGH 40-58% assignment reflects:
1. NL ramp exceeds the series' confirmed HIGH trigger levels
2. No Smartbidder Non-Spin DA available to confirm condition (2) — if $4+/MWh at HE20-21, 1.8x right-tail multiplier would apply to conditional lambda P90 (would push P90 from $75 → ~$135)
3. Enverus net_load_fc is structurally lower than YE net load for this evening window — but both confirm ramp well above MEDIUM

If Smartbidder Non-Spin DA had been available and showed >$4/MWh at HE20-21 (structurally likely on a +19,000 MW ramp day), the conditional P90 lambda would shift to ~$135/MWh and P(binding) would revise upward toward 55-70%.

## Risk Scenarios

### Upside Risk (Higher Congestion)

1. **Wind forecast bust (downside)**: If AG2/Enverus wind forecast is 1,000-2,000 MW too high for GR_SOUTH or GR_COASTAL during HE19-21, the already-record NL ramp increases further. Each 1,000 MW miss on wind adds ~1,000 MW to the ramp burden. HOUSTON_IMPORT probability approaches 70% in a -2,000 MW wind bust scenario.

2. **Load exceeds forecast by 2,000+ MW**: Enverus and YE are within ~2% at peak. However, if heat index in Houston exceeds forecast (currently 99-100°F "feels like") and morning clouds delay, load could spike to 83,000+ MW — analogous to the June 18 record day (84,362 MW). At that level, HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY_LOCAL probability rises to HIGH and HOUSTON_IMPORT P90 lambda approaches $150-200/MWh.

3. **Thermal outage extension**: Any forced outage additions on top of the current 11,300-12,550 MW cap-out baseline directly increases binding probability for both HOUSTON constraints. Each 1,000 MW additional outage is worth roughly +3-5 percentage points on HOUSTON_IMPORT P(binding).

### Downside Risk (Lower Congestion)

1. **Morning clouds delay load ramp**: AG2 KIAH shows 74-88% cloud cover through HE08-09 with slight precip risk (0.02"). If overcast persists, load morning ramp softens and midday NL stays elevated (delayed solar ramp) — HOUSTON_SOUTH_MIDDAY_LOCAL risk reduces. However, this does not materially affect the evening constraint.

2. **GR_WEST stays suppressed all day**: GR_WEST minimum at HE14 is 6,911 MW. If West wind stays weak through HE22-24 (GR_WEST <10,000 MW rather than the forecast 12,027-12,272 MW), WEST_TO_NORTH overnight risk drops to NEGLIGIBLE. YE and AG2 are broadly aligned on the evening recovery; this scenario would require a significant forecast miss.
