## Headline

2026-06-08 (Monday) is a **high-load summer peak day with a dominant afternoon/evening congestion profile**. The regime is structurally different from both 2026-06-06 (evening-scarcity dominant) and 2026-06-07 (extreme midday VRE overgeneration dominant). Key characteristics:

1. **Load is the highest in the tracked series**: Peak forecast 77,446 MW at HE17 (Yes Energy) — exceeding the prior series high of ~79,559 MW on 2026-06-02 in that tracked sequence. For the recent June run, this is the cycle peak.
2. **GR_WEST is in a two-regime day**: Trough at HE15-16 of 12,016–12,157 MW — still elevated vs the 1,827 MW floor that triggered the MEDIUM-HIGH on 2026-06-06, but importantly this is a Monday return-to-work day with a strong afternoon demand ramp that will stress the South-to-Houston and import paths in the evening.
3. **Net load trough at HE10-11** is 18,359–18,404 MW — similar to 2026-06-07 (18,380 MW at HE11), confirming significant midday VRE overgeneration. However, GR_WEST at trough (12,016–12,157 MW at HE15-16) is slightly lower than 2026-06-07's midday floor (~10,200 MW) but higher than 2026-06-06's extreme trough (1,827 MW).
4. **Evening net load ramp is extreme**: NL rises from 27,261 MW at HE17 to 46,583 MW at HE21 — a ramp of +19,322 MW in 4 hours. This is the steepest evening NL ramp in the tracked cycle by a wide margin. The driver is the solar cliff on a very high load day.
5. **Cap-out is elevated throughout the day**: 15,368 MW at HE11-12 — the highest in the tracked dataset (prior series high was ~14,724 MW). Elevated derating reduces effective supply, increasing congestion probability in the evening window.

**Overall Day Assessment**: 2026-06-08 is a **HIGH-priority congestion day** with the primary signal in the **late afternoon / evening window (HE17-22)** driven by an extreme NL ramp and high load, and a **secondary WEST_TO_NORTH signal in the midday window (HE09-16)** driven by continued VRE surplus. The evening signal is the strongest in the current June tracked cycle given the combination of (a) highest NL ramp magnitude, (b) highest load peak, and (c) highest cap-out level.

### 1. HOUSTON_IMPORT_345 / SOUTH_TO_HOUSTON — Evening Solar Cliff + NL Ramp (HE17-22) — PRIMARY

**Heuristic P(binding): HIGH (30-50%)**

Basis and assumptions:
- **NL ramp magnitude (YE HE18→HE21): +18,367 MW**. This exceeds the prior calibration ceiling of 12,000 MW (MEDIUM-HIGH threshold). Applying the MEDIUM-HIGH bracket (12,000+ MW ramp → base 25-38%) and adding an upgrade modifier for the ramp exceeding 18,000 MW, the estimate escalates to HIGH (30-50%). No prior cycle has been calibrated at this NL ramp level, so the upper bound is uncertain.
- **Load peak: 77,446 MW at HE17**. This is the cycle high and approaches June-season record territory. High absolute load increases baseline import demand on South-to-Houston paths.
- **Cap-out: 15,368 MW at HE11-12** (cycle high). Elevated derating reduces the effective dispatchable supply available to follow the evening ramp. A system with 15,368 MW capacity out of service has fewer resources to cover the sharp NL ramp — increasing constraint binding probability.
- **Solar cliff (YE): -18,513 MW at HE19→HE21**. With estimated Enverus correction, the cliff is approximately -23,000 MW over 2 hours. This is an extremely sharp supply withdrawal that must be replaced by thermal generation and wind, stressing import paths on the South and Houston corridors.
- **Evening wind (partial offset)**: GR_WEST rises from 12,362 MW to 14,497 MW at HE21; ERCOT wind from 20,214 to 23,191 MW. The wind offset (+2,977 MW ERCOT-wide) is real but insufficient to absorb the full solar cliff (-18,513 MW YE). Net supply stress after wind offset: approximately -15,000 MW — the largest net supply stress in the tracked cycle.
- **Day-of-week (Monday)**: Mondays in ERCOT tend to have higher-than-forecast load due to commercial/industrial return-to-work demand. For a summer Monday with 77,446 MW forecast peak, realized load could exceed 79,000 MW, further elevating evening congestion probability.
- **No Smartbidder AS data available this cycle**: The Non-Spin DA forecast and P(DA>RT) signals that were strong leading indicators in prior cycles (e.g., $12.19/MWh Non-Spin at HE21 on 2026-06-06) cannot be read for 2026-06-08. This is a meaningful data gap. The absence of the Smartbidder signal does not reduce the physical congestion signal (NL ramp, cap-out, load peak), but it removes the AS scarcity leading indicator. The HIGH estimate is based entirely on the physical fundamental signals.

**Caveat on data gap**: The absence of Enverus solar STPF means the solar cliff magnitude may be underestimated by ~4,500 MW (YE COPHSL bias). If Enverus solar was available, the adjusted solar cliff HE19→HE21 would be ~23,000 MW, which would elevate the P(binding) further. The HIGH (30-50%) estimate is conservative on the upside — if Enverus solar is confirmed consistent with the YE NL figures, the upper bound could reach 50-60%.
