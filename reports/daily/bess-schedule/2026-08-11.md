## Key Strategic Decisions (Rationale)

**1. Overnight charge rejected (HE01-06) despite MEDIUM-HIGH W2N**
W2N MEDIUM-HIGH overnight (SCI 0.424-0.469) provides E[GKS MCC] -$4 to -$9/MWh. After MCC, overnight GKS node = ~$6-14/MWh. Duck floor GKS node = -$22/MWh. Differential of $28-36/MWh per stored MWh makes overnight charging economically dominated. Consistent with 08/10 decision logic but the margin is now even larger (EXTREME vs MODERATE duck floor).

**2. HE09 charge deferred to HE10**
HE09 NL 27,817 MW (MODERATE-SEVERE). GKS node ~$1/MWh. Charging HE10-12 only vs starting at HE09: Option B earns $1,016 more at P50 by maximizing MWh at the most negative prices (HE10 at -$22, HE11 at -$19 rather than spending capacity on HE09 at $1). Battery can achieve full SoC in HE10-12 window alone.

**3. HE18-19 pre-discharge held (base plan)**
Finding 3 pattern: SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT binding onset is +2h from raw NL calculation window → HE20-22 is the primary DA window, not HE18-19. GKS MCC at HE18 is near-zero (no-trade zone). Market-analyst's HE18 early-start recommendation acknowledged but deferred to Finding 3 discipline. HE19 reserved as RT contingency only (Stack B trigger: RT > $38/MWh).

**4. Negative-price bid discipline**
HE10-11 require BESS to bid as a BUYER at NEGATIVE prices. Standard $0 default bid would still clear (any price ≤ clearing will clear), but the optimal strategy is to bid the most negative feasible price to maximize probability of clearing while capturing the full depth. Without Smartbidder's P(clearing < $X) distribution, bid conservatively at -$10/MWh (HE10) and -$8/MWh (HE11) as floors, not $0. Smartbidder restoration critical for precise bid optimization.

## Risks

**Risk 1 (HIGH): Duck floor less negative than base case**
If DA HE10 clears at -$5/MWh rather than -$15/MWh (bear scenario: GR_WEST -2 GW), charge revenue drops from $4,100 to ~$1,800. P50 total drops to ~$10,800. Still positive revenue from charging but the structural inversion value is reduced. Directional conclusion (negative prices at HE10) holds in all reasonable scenarios (NL 19,981 MW against 7,669 MW cap-out is historically extreme). Bear: P10 ~$8,400.

**Risk 2 (MEDIUM-HIGH): HE20 solar cliff under-commitment spike**
The -13,469 MW solar exit at HE19→HE20 (new event extreme) is the largest single-hour solar cliff of the series. If thermal generators under-commit to the HE20 DA market (anchoring on "dome collapse = softer prices"), RT could spike significantly above DA at HE20 precisely when GKS BESS is locked into a DA discharge position at $38 floor. Without Smartbidder P(RT>DA), this risk is unquantifiable. Mitigation: monitor ERCOT fast-ramp advisory at HE18-19. If thin commitment observed, shift HE20 from DA to RT position (Stack B variant).

**Risk 3 (MEDIUM): SOUTH_HOUSTON_IMPORT does not bind (base case = non-binding)**
Non-binding probability = 50-65% (base case). If SOUTH_HOUSTON does not bind at HE20-22, GKS MCC = near-zero (not +$2/MWh), reducing discharge revenue by ~$400 at P50 ($13,103 → ~$12,700). The energy dispatch itself is not affected — only the MCC uplift disappears. Still economically optimal to discharge at HE20-21 regardless of SOUTH_HOUSTON outcome.
