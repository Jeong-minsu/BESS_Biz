Physical DEC thesis: The -11,922 MW solar dropout forces rapid thermal ramp-up in RT dispatch. DA prices for HE20 are set before the extent of the RT ramp is revealed. Historical September ERCOT pattern: at Scarcity Cliff hours (NL > 60k MW, steep solar exit), RT clears above DA because: (1) the thermal ramp speed required in RT exceeds the DA commitment curve, and (2) intra-hour dispatch surprises add RT premium. DEC captures the gap when RT > DA.

WDPEAK factor: Post-Labor Day return-to-office amplifies commercial load (89,007 MW peak HE17). DA bidders on WDPEAK days are more likely to price scarcity risk aggressively in advance. This creates two competing effects: (a) if DA is bid up to a spike price and RT is below → DEC loses; (b) if RT still exceeds the elevated DA bid → DEC wins with a large spread. The net WDPEAK effect is uncertain; the primary risk is that efficient DA pricing reduces the RT-DA spread. This is explicitly noted in Risks.
