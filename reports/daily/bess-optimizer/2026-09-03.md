## 7. Rationale

**Why HE15 and HE19 (not HE20)?**
HE20 has the highest DA price ceiling ($100–180/MWh) but the planned SoC = 0 after HE19 discharge. Choosing HE19 vs HE20 is the key decision. HE19 is preferred because:
1. Wind at HE19 = 11,290 MW (strong buffer); at HE20 = 13,988 MW (even stronger). DA-RT spread is slightly positive at both, but more certain at HE19 (less wind uncertainty).
2. RT spike probability at HE20 (6–12% > $300) creates a left-tail risk for RT dispatch — but the BESS has no SoC to capitalize on that spike either way if dispatched at HE19.
3. If SoC were reserved for HE20 instead of HE19: sacrifice $10,179 (certain HE19 DA) for $10,360 (slightly higher HE20 DA midpoint) — marginal gain of $181 doesn't justify the additional hour of SoC hold risk and missed NS revenue window.

**Why 87 MW (not 100 MW) at HE15?**
87 MW symmetric split allows equal dispatch at both peak hours from 174 MWh stored (87+87=174). 100 MW at HE15 + 74 MW at HE19 earns: $11,000 + $8,510 = $19,510 (at midpoints $110/$115) vs 87/87 split: $9,570 + $10,005 = $19,575. Essentially equal, but symmetric split is simpler to manage SoC-wise and avoids de-rating at HE19.

**Why no AS during HE14–18?**
Capacity sharing constraint and negative EV. At these hours, any MW offered to NS/ECRS competes with energy dispatch. NS deployment risk (SoC consumed) precludes guaranteed execution at HE15 and HE19. Structural analysis shows energy arbitrage value ($105–115/MWh) exceeds AS capacity payment ($0.30–10/MW/h) by 10–350x.

**Congestion impact on charge hours (HE02–03)**:
GKS MCC -$0.4 to -$1.2/MWh expected during HE01–03 WEST_TO_NORTH window (P(binding) 12–18%, conditional lambda $18–28). This REDUCES the effective nodal charge price by $0.4–1.2/MWh, making HE02–03 even cheaper than the HB price. Favorable for charging. Congestion analyst weight: LOW (single-source), but directional signal is valid.

**Duck curve**: NEGLIGIBLE (NL trough 38,775 MW vs 32,000 MW MILD boundary). No midday price discount available for opportunistic charging. Sep 1 benefited from cheap RT midday at $25–29/MWh — Sep 3 has no equivalent opportunity.
