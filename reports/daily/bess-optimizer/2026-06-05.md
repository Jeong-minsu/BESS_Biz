## Optimization Framework

### Asset Constraints

- Max power: 100 MW (charge or discharge)
- Energy limit: 200 MWh total usable
- Round-trip efficiency: 85% (charge 1 MWh delivered = 1/0.85 = 1.176 MWh consumed from grid)
- SOC range: 0–200 MWh (0–100%)
- Starting SOC (midnight HE01): assume 100 MWh (50% — neutral start)
- AS capacity: MW committed to AS product reduces available MW for energy dispatch in that direction
  - Non-Spin: idle BESS only (does not require MW reservation during charge/hold; requires availability)
  - ECRS: 10-min ramping product; can be offered from idle SoC headroom
  - RRS: 60-min ramping; charged from idle or discharging SoC headroom
  - RegUp: real-time AGC up-regulation (discharging headroom)
  - RegDown: real-time AGC down-regulation (charging headroom); BESS charging = natural RegDown provider

### Strategy Selection Rationale

Three scenarios evaluated:

**Stack A — AS-Heavy**: Maximize Non-Spin/ECRS commitment HE20-22; hold back from energy discharge.
- Pro: HE22 Non-Spin $9.18 + ECRS $2.71 = $11.89/MWh × 100 MW = $1,189/hr
- Con: Sacrifices HE19-20 energy arbitrage (~$37-39 DA × 100 MW = $3,700-3,940/hr)
- Net: AS revenue alone does not exceed energy arbitrage at these price levels. Energy > AS in HE19-20.

**Stack B — Energy Arbitrage Heavy**: Full charge HE02-05, full discharge HE19-21, no AS.
- Pro: Clean $37-39/MWh discharge × 200 MWh = max energy revenue
- Con: Forfeits Non-Spin $9.18 at HE22 and ECRS $2.91 at HE21 when BESS is depleted
- Net: Strong base case but leaves post-depletion AS revenue on table

**Stack C — Mixed Energy + AS (SELECTED)**: Charge HE02-05 (4 hours × 50 MW), hold overnight; full discharge HE19-21 (energy-primary), capture AS in HE21-22 with residual SoC headroom; offer Non-Spin from idle MW in shoulder hours.
- HE21: discharge 100 MW energy + 0 MW AS (energy-first given $36.36 DA)
- HE22: depleted energy → pivot to AS (Non-Spin 100 MW @ $9.18, ECRS 50 MW @ $2.71)
- This structure captures the highest-value energy window AND the Non-Spin peak

**Decision**: Stack C is selected. The Non-Spin DA peak at HE22 ($9.18/MWh) occurs one hour AFTER the energy peak (HE20 $39.40), meaning a unit that discharges fully by HE21 ends up with depleted SoC at HE22. The residual AS revenue from HE22 Non-Spin is available only if SoC is managed to retain idle MW capacity even when energy is dispatched. Since Non-Spin is an availability product (not a real-time dispatch), BESS at 0% SOC cannot supply it. Therefore the correct structure is: discharge fully by HE21, which naturally forfeits HE22 Non-Spin. The alternative (hold 50 MWh back for HE22 AS) sacrifices 50 MWh × $36.36 = $1,818 at HE21 to gain 50 MW × $9.18 = $459 Non-Spin at HE22 — net -$1,359 exchange. Energy-first is correct through HE21.

However, **RegDown in HE18-19** ($4.56/$1.30 per MW) is available while the BESS is charging or holding — charging BESS is a natural RegDown provider. I allocate 30 MW RegDown capacity during HE18 when the BESS transitions from full-SoC hold to pre-discharge positioning.
