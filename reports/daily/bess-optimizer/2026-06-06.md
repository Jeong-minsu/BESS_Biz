## DA-RT Spread Strategy

**Energy: DA > RT — strong signal → DA commitment for discharge**

The DA-RT spread at HE20-21 is +$9.55/+$10.19, with P(DA>RT) = 63.4%/66.9%. This is the strongest DA-dominance signal of the current tracked cycle (exceeds the prior bests in late May). Saturday market thin-liquidity characteristics typically amplify DA-RT spread in the evening peak — fewer RT participants means RT prices can be volatile but often undershoot DA commitments. DA commitment at HE20-21 is the correct venue choice.

**Charge: DA overnight — slight RT>DA signal ignored on risk grounds**

HE01-02 show DA-RT spread of -$0.56 to -$0.27 (slight RT>DA), with P(DA>RT) around 55%. In principle this suggests charging in DA captures slightly cheaper than RT. However, the spread is tiny ($0.27-$0.56) and the overnight wind uncertainty is real (if GR_WEST underperforms its 7,225 MW peak forecast, RT prices rise). DA purchase locks in the charge cost at known price. We buy DA at HE01-02 to eliminate RT charge cost uncertainty.

**NonSpin AS: DA venue — HIGH clearing probability during hold hours**

NonSpin clears in DA at HIGH probability during HE01-24 per market-analyst AS table. The DA Non-Spin offer captures the full capacity payment without RT deployment risk. All NonSpin offers are placed in DA.

**ECRS: DA venue — MEDIUM clearing probability HE06-08**

ECRS at $0.35-0.98/MWh during HE06-08 clears in DA at MEDIUM probability. 20 MW offered in DA at each of these hours as incremental revenue alongside NonSpin. The total expected ECRS revenue is small ($34) but carries zero opportunity cost given 200 MWh SOC is full and idle.

**RRS: Not offered**

RRS prices ($0.11-$1.31/MWh) are consistently below the NonSpin prices across all hours. Since NonSpin and RRS compete for the same "idle discharge capability," NonSpin dominates RRS at every hour. RRS = 0 throughout.

**Reg-Up / Reg-Down: Not offered**

RegUp prices ($0.49-$2.85/MWh) are above NonSpin in some hours (notably HE21 $2.85/MWh) but require two-sided headroom (must be able to move both up and down from setpoint). At SOC=0 (HE21), there is no upward discharge headroom and no downward charge headroom on the discharge schedule. During the hold phase (SOC=200), RegDown ($0.62-$2.60/MWh) would require pre-committing to absorb additional power — i.e., charging beyond the fill target, which is not possible when SOC=200. RegUp during idle hours requires capability to increase output, which at SOC=200 and not discharging means starting a discharge — this conflicts with the hold strategy. Regulation products are structurally incompatible with the full-charge-hold-then-discharge-to-empty cycle. Not offered.
