## Rationale and Key Decisions

### 1. Why charge at HE09 instead of HE10

HE10 ($16.84) is $0.20/MWh cheaper than HE09 ($17.04). The incremental charge savings from using HE10 = $0.20 × 97.6 MWh = $20. However, charging at HE09 frees HE10 for NonSpin capacity offer (80 MW × $0.924 = $74 at full SOC). Net gain from shifting charge to HE09: +$74 NonSpin at HE10 − $20 extra charge cost = +$54. Marginal improvement but directionally correct.

The congestion-analyst also notes that the VRE overgeneration signal is strongest at HE10-11 (SCI = 1.97-2.16). The midday charge window is reliable across HE09-12; concentrating charge at HE09 (start of the window) captures cheap DA while still operating within the same RT savings opportunity.

### 2. Why 2-hour discharge HE19-20 rather than 3 hours including HE21

Three-hour alternatives (e.g., 65+65+27 MW across HE19-21) yield approximately $4,849 energy revenue vs the 2-hour 100+56.7 MW approach yielding $4,880. The 2-hour approach is marginally better ($31 more) because HE19 and HE20 have identical price ($31.17 vs $31.10, near-flat) while HE21 ($30.00) is $1.10 lower. Maximum discharge at the peak hours dominates. No production benefit from extending to 3 hours.

### 3. Why NonSpin at HE22 ($8.261) is not captured

To offer 50 MW NonSpin at HE22, SOC must be at least ~50 MWh at end of HE21 (30-minute deployment capability = 25 MWh minimum at 50 MW). Achieving SOC=50 at end of HE21 requires reserving 50 MWh during HE19-20 discharge. That reservation reduces HE19-20 energy revenue from $4,880 to approximately $3,443 — a sacrifice of $1,437 to gain $413 (50 MW × $8.261) in NonSpin. Net: −$1,024. NonSpin at HE22 does not justify the energy sacrifice.

### 4. Why no RegDown at HE15-18 ($2.54-$2.73)

After charging at HE09, SOC = 190 MWh = SOC_MAX (95% of 200 MWh). RegDown for a BESS means increasing charging rate (absorbing more power). At SOC_MAX, there is no headroom to absorb additional charge. Offering RegDown against a full battery constitutes a false capacity offer. Skipping RegDown here is a physical constraint, not a price decision.

### 5. Why no second discharge cycle (double-cycle day)

The best "second peak" for discharge would be HE18 (DA $25.61, the next-highest price outside HE19-21). The spread from charge (HE10 $16.84) to an HE18 discharge is $8.77/MWh. Using the battery for a first cycle at HE18 means arriving at HE19 with insufficient SOC (would need to re-charge between HE18 and HE19, but there are no cheap hours between HE18-19). Holding the full SOC for the HE19-20 window earns $14.10/MWh spread vs $8.77 for the early-cycle option. Single cycle is correct.
