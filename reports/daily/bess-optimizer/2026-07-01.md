## Risks

1. **Evening ramp RT spike (HE20-21)**: P(DA>RT)=0.635-0.652 means there is a 35-37% probability RT exceeds DA in these hours. If RT spikes to $50+ (ORDC event), we would forgo premium RT revenue by having settled DA. Mitigation: DA premium is $10-12/MWh — an RT spike would need to exceed DA by this margin before the DA position is net worse. Given no confirmed scarcity signals today (outage only 7,649 MW avg, below 10,000 MW trigger), this risk is moderate.

2. **HOUSTON_IMPORT_345 binding (upside)**: If HOUSTON_IMPORT binds in HE20-22 (P=38-55%), GKS node MCC could add +$3-6/MWh to effective LMP above Smartbidder hub price. This is unmodeled upside — Stack D actual revenue could reach $4,900-5,100 in that scenario. Congestion analyst classifies this as MEDIUM-HIGH probability.

3. **Solar suppression negative MCC HE9-11 (downside)**: Congestion analyst projects GKS node MCC = -$3.0 in HE9-15 (Finding 7, confirmed 4+ cycles). If realized, effective charge price for HE9-11 drops by ~$3/MWh — charge would cost ~$225 less than modeled ($3/MWh x 75 MWh average). This is modestly favorable for the charge position, not adverse.

4. **Smartbidder price forecast error (both directions)**: HE20-21 DA prices of $35-37/MWh are model forecasts. Actual DA clearing could deviate. If DA clears at $30 (downside scenario), energy arbitrage drops from $2,984 to approximately $2,584 (-$400). If DA clears at $42+ (upside from HOUSTON_IMPORT binding), energy arbitrage could reach $3,400+.

5. **AS clearing below forecast**: Smartbidder AS forecasts are point estimates; actual AS clearing prices can deviate significantly. The HE22 NonSpin $11.74 forecast is high — if NonSpin clears at $5/MWh, HE22 AS revenue drops from $740 to approximately $350. Total AS revenue downside: -$400 to -$600 vs base case.

6. **Overnight RT spikes (HE1-7)**: P(DA<RT)=0.62-0.68 overnight. BESS is idle at SoC=0 in these hours and offering only AS. No energy exposure. If ORDC spikes occur in HE2-3, we miss potential RT discharge revenue but cannot act (battery is empty). This is an accepted limitation of the 2-cycle strategy; a second charge-discharge cycle would require charge in HE4-6 and discharge in HE7-8, which at $22-26/MWh adds minimal arbitrage on top of ORDC uncertainty.

## Alternative (Conditional) — Stack C: Mix Strategy

**Trigger**: If GR_WEST wind actual in the 07:00-08:00 CT range is tracking above 13,000 MW (close to Enverus high-end forecast), net load evening ramp could be attenuated and HE20-21 DA prices may clear lower than forecast. In this scenario, the marginal value of the third charge hour (Stack D vs Stack C) diminishes.

**Stack C schedule**: Same charge HE10-11 (2 hours, −100 MW each → 170 MWh stored), discharge HE21 (100 MW) + HE20 (70 MW). Adds afternoon AS: 40 MW ECRS + 30 MW RRS + 10 MW RegUp in HE13-19, and 50 MW NonSpin + 30 MW ECRS + 20 MW RegUp in HE22-24.

**Stack C expected revenue**: $4,545 (vs Stack D $4,724, delta = −$179). The $179 difference equals the marginal gain from the additional HE9 charge hour — if wind forecast busts upward by 1+ GW, the $179 incremental stack D benefit could turn neutral.
