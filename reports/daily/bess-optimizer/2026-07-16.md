## Strategy Rationale

1. **Narrow DA spread + RT>DA throughout**: DA top-to-bottom spread of only $9.62/MWh ($15.27 to $24.89) makes energy arbitrage marginal at first glance, but the pervasive RT premium (+$2.74 avg) tilts HE21 discharge to RT where realized price is $27.94 — creating an effective arbitrage of ($27.94 RT − $15.27/0.85 effective cost) = $10.00/MWh net margin per MWh delivered. That margin on 100 MWh (HE21) = $1,000 gross energy contribution for the discharge hour alone.

2. **Single cycle preferred over two cycles**: Starting SoC 100 MWh limits total storage headroom to 100 MWh of new charge (reaching 200 MWh full). With no strong morning price spike (HE07 estimated ~$21 DA, RT ~$24 vs. 07-13's actual $41.31), a 2-cycle option forfeits ~$900 in AS revenue during morning hours for a marginal energy gain of approximately $200. Single-cycle remains optimal under today's flat morning price structure.

3. **NS baseline is necessary but not sufficient**: Non-Spin contributes $1,693 (38% of total) and provides a floor. However, at average NS prices of $0.55/MWh midday, NS alone yields ~$2,604/day (full-day 80 MW). The energy arbitrage adds $1,868 net above the NS-only alternative ($4,472 vs $2,604), primarily from the HE21 RT discharge ($2,794 revenue in a single hour).

4. **SoC depletion at HE21 is the correct trade-off**: Holding 80 MWh in reserve for HE22–24 NS would yield $672 (80 MW × $2.8 avg × 3h). Deploying that 80 MWh at HE21 RT yields $2,235 (80 MW × $27.94). The opportunity cost ratio is 3.3× in favor of discharge. SoC depleted at HE21.

## Risks

1. **RT price disappoints at HE21**: HE21 RT forecast $27.94 carries 42–43% probability of RT < DA in that specific hour (P(DA<RT) 57–58%). If RT comes in at $24.89 (DA parity), RT discharge revenue drops from $2,794 to $2,489 (−$305). Downside scenario total: ~$4,167.

2. **Solar outperforms forecast (clouds clear earlier)**: High cloud cover (KIAH 76–95% HE09–12) underpins the cheap midday trough. If solar exceeds STPF, the midday price trough shallows ($15–$16 → $17–$19), reducing charging economics and potentially making charging HE10–11 less favorable than estimated.

3. **HE22–24 high NS window missed**: With SoC depleted at HE21, NS revenue at HE22–24 ($2.6–3.0/MWh × 80 MW × 3h = $672 foregone) represents the largest single opportunity cost. If a late-night reliability event drives NS prices above $5/MWh (Rule 3 threshold), the SoC depletion decision incurs material downside.
