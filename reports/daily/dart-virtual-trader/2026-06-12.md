## Why — Thesis

### Single Window: Evening HB_HOUSTON SHORT DA (HE19-22)

The fundamental thesis is the **solar cliff** combined with the **HB_HOUSTON positive MCC basis**.

On 2026-06-12, solar collapses from 22,804 MW at HE17 to 650 MW at HE21 — a 22,154 MW withdrawal in 4 hours. This forces the grid to replace solar output entirely with thermal and wind during the HE19-22 window. Net load spikes from 39,503 MW (HE19) to 53,034 MW (HE21), a +13,531 MW ramp in 2 hours. At peak NL of 53,034 MW, ERCOT must dispatch near-maximum thermal capacity in the Houston/South corridor, which is exactly when HOUSTON_IMPORT_345 faces maximum stress.

The DA market will price this ramp in advance ($40.23 at HE21), but DA prices often over-price extreme ramp events because all generators bid high into the thin-supply window. The congestion-analyst's MEDIUM conviction (+$4.5 hub-pair basis) indicates the historical pattern on comparable days (NL ramp 15-16 GW, NL at HE21 near 53 GW) is that DA at HB_HOUSTON exceeds RT by $4-5/MWh on average.

**Why MEDIUM and not HIGH**: Three moderating factors vs prior cycle:
1. NL ramp is +15,683 MW vs +22,491 MW yesterday — 6,808 MW step-down, the single largest driver of the conviction downgrade
2. Cap-out at HE21 is 12,166 MW vs 10,740 MW yesterday — 1,426 MW more thermal supply available; grid is less stressed
3. No Smartbidder P(DA>RT) to confirm direction — structural model only

**Why include HE19**: HE19 is the weakest hour in this window. Solar still at 15,870 MW provides a meaningful RT cushion. P(win) = 56% is at the bottom of the MED range (≥55%). The rationale for inclusion is the concentrated ramp into HE20-21: if the market is pricing the ramp correctly, DA at HE19 should already reflect the rising NL, and the basis thesis (+$4.5 unconditional) applies from HE17 onward. A 25 MW MED position captures the early ramp signal with limited downside.
