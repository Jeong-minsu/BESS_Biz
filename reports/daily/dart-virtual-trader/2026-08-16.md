## Risks

### 1. DA OVER-PRICES the cliff — HE20 risk (15-25% probability)
If sophisticated market participants price the under-commitment risk into HE20 DA bids aggressively, the DA clearing price could be higher than the $38-58/MWh range, reducing the spread magnitude or eliminating it. In this scenario, DEC HE20 P(win) falls below 50%.
- Mitigation: DA price at $45 bid cap; if DA clears near $65+, our DEC bid was under-market and didn't execute.

### 2. GR_WEST evening recovery stronger than STWPF — HE21 risk (20-30% probability)
Bidclose shows GR_WEST HE21 = 6,902 MW. If actual RT realizes at 8,000-10,000 MW (1,000-3,000 MW above forecast), additional supply reduces RT spike probability at HE21.
- Mitigation: HE21 position already sized modestly (15 MW vs 20 MW at HE20) reflecting this uncertainty.

### 3. Sunday demand realizes below forecast — systematic DEC risk
If Sunday afternoon load (HE20-21) falls 2,000-3,000 MW below forecast (Sunday demand is structurally lower than weekday and slightly more variable), NL HE21 could realize at 64-65k MW rather than 67.9k MW. This reduces but does not eliminate RT spike risk.
- Mitigation: Even at 64,742 MW (Aug 4 reference), DA was $32.16. NL ≥ 64,742 MW is still above calibration anchors → DEC signal intact.

### 4. Physical scarcity / LASA at HE21 (tail, 5-10% probability)
If ERCOT declares Emergency Condition and LASA (Load Serving Aggregation) pricing activates, RT could spike to $3,000-9,000/MWh. Our DEC position would benefit enormously, but this is an unusually high-magnitude outcome.
- Not a risk per se for our DEC position (it's upside), but DA settlement mechanics in scarcity scenarios should be monitored.

### 5. Smartbidder DEGRADED — structural risk (ongoing)
No P(DA>RT) cross-validation for 21 consecutive cycles. Aug 16 is the most important single trading day of the Stage 0 period. Self-model P=62%/59% estimates could be systematically wrong without Smartbidder cross-check.

### 6. Tenaska DEGRADED — settlement gap (ongoing)
