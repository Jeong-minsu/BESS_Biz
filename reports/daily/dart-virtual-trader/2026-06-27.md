However, simultaneously this would FURTHER reduce HOUSTON_IMPORT probability (GR_WEST export relieving South load center) — the HE20-22 basis positions would suffer if HOUSTON_IMPORT binding probability collapses.

2. **GR_WEST forecast bust (downside)**: If actual GR_WEST drops below 14,000 MW in the evening, HOUSTON_IMPORT probability reverts toward base HIGH 32-47% (W25 haircut would not apply). HB_HOUSTON premium would widen — HE20-22 basis positions benefit. But HE23 WEST_TO_NORTH basis would weaken.

This is the primary upside risk to the basis position — a strong Non-Spin clearing would confirm HOUSTON_IMPORT binding and expand the HB_HOUSTON premium significantly. No action available pre-DAM without the data.

4. **Saturday demand surprise (upside)**: If weekend AC load exceeds forecast (heat wave 95°F DFW), actual load could be 2-3 GW above 81,846 MW forecast. This would intensify the NL ramp and raise HOUSTON_IMPORT probability toward HIGH. HE20-22 basis benefits.

5. **Saturday demand surprise (downside)**: Industrial load 3-5% below forecast would compress NL ramp and reduce HOUSTON_IMPORT probability. HE20-22 basis positions would underperform.

6. **Midday RT spike (missed opportunity)**: If solar under-delivers on Saturday morning and RT spikes above DA at HE09-12 (same pattern as 2026-06-22 cloud-bust), the DEC opportunity at HE09-12 would be missed due to cap-out below dual-trigger threshold. This is a model structural limitation — the 11,500 MW cap-out threshold correctly filters most cases but will miss RT spikes driven by solar shortfall when cap-out is at the 9,987-10,159 MW level. Current risk: cap-out profile is the weakest in the June tracking series and cannot support the override. This tradeoff is accepted.
