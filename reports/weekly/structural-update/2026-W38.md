### 1-B. ERCOT Reserve Modeling Error — Adjustment Starting September ★

**[출처: texaspowercost.com; ERCOT public statements]**

A material internal discovery was made: ERCOT was purchasing approximately **3.7 GW less in backup reserves** than its own planning model required, for roughly seven months prior to September 2026. Key details:

- ERCOT will not retroactively compensate for the shortfall, but will adjust reserve procurement levels starting in **September 2026**.
- The adjustment adds modestly to AS costs in the short term but addresses a structural under-procurement gap.
- This is distinct from the 2026 AS methodology (probabilistic 8.3 GW ECRS/Non-Spin target voted August 27) — it represents a correction within the existing framework.

**BESS Impact**: Higher AS procurement volumes modestly support clearing prices for RRS, ECRS, and Non-Spin beginning in late September. Watch for upward shift in AS revenue in Q4 2026 operational data once Smartbidder is restored.

### 3-B. Solar — Extended Summer Pattern; Duck Curve Depth Varies Significantly by Day ★

**[출처: W38 daily briefings Yes Energy + Enverus data]**

| Date | Solar Peak | Net Load Trough | Duck Curve Amplitude |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sep 14 | 32.3 GW (HE12) | 20.7 GW (HE11) | 35.9 GW |
| Sep 16 | 30.4 GW (HE12, Enverus) | 32.2 GW (HE10) | 31.3 GW |
| Sep 20 | 30.3 GW (HE13) | 30.4 GW (HE10) | 39.2 GW |

Sep 14 exhibited an extreme duck (NL trough 20.7 GW) — the deepest of W38 — due to simultaneous strong wind (20.7 GW avg). The week showed high inter-day variability in duck depth, driven primarily by wind rather than solar (solar was relatively stable at 30-32 GW across the week).

September solar is tracking at **approximately 5-8% below August peak** (33.5 GW Aug 31). The seasonal decline is proceeding normally. Solar peak timing has shifted from HE12 to HE12-13 as equinox approaches (Sep 22).

**Duck curve structural note**: The September 22 autumnal equinox brings the shortest solar day of the shoulder season. Post-equinox solar peak gradually declines through October, reducing midday generation ~15-20% by month-end. The pronounced duck curve will persist through October but with modestly less extreme midday troughs.

**Structural interpretation**: September wind is exhibiting the characteristic transitional-season volatility. The anticyclonic suppression of early September (Sep 3 series low 443 MW) is gone, but weak mesoscale pressure gradients between summer anticyclones and approaching fall fronts create multiple "near-zero" episodes per week. This pattern will persist until the first strong cold front passage (historically late September to mid-October).

### 4-B. STEP Permian Sep 25 CCN Hearing — MAJOR STATUS CHANGE ★★★

**[출처: Texas Scorecard Sep 9, 2026; Texas Scorecard "Top Regulator Recommends Remanding" headline]**

This is the highest-urgency W38 structural development.

**Chairman Gleeson's September 9, 2026 memo** — issued just before W38 began — stated that the administrative law judges' Proposal for Decision (PFD) did not address all issues identified in PUCT's March 30, 2026 preliminary order. **Key issues Gleeson named**:
1. Whether the proposed transmission facilities are necessary to meet federal and state reliability standards.
2. Estimated congestion cost savings for customers that "may result" from the project.

**Consequence**: The scheduled September 25 CCN hearing for STEP Permian segments no longer appears on the PUCT schedule. The project may be remanded to Administrative Law Judges for supplemental proceedings before coming back to full Commission.

**Political context**: Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick previously expressed being "very disappointed" that regulators approved the first two 765-kV segments (Aug 28) despite legislative calls for a pause. Gleeson's memo effectively creates the delay that the legislature demanded — through process rather than outright denial.

**BESS/transmission impact**: STEP Permian's 765-kV infrastructure is critical for enabling full exploitation of West Texas wind capacity. Delays push back the timeline for reducing GR_WEST-to-load transmission constraints, extending the period where congestion suppresses West Hub prices relative to North/Houston nodes. This maintains the BESS locational advantage for projects sited at load centers.

### 5-B. Texas Load Growth — Extended Heat Season Reveals Structural Demand Floor ★★

**[출처: W38 daily briefings Sep 14-20; EIA September 2026 weekly update; Texas Tribune July 2026; Yes Energy BidClose data]**

W38 confirmed that Texas demand remains structurally elevated well beyond what historical seasonal patterns would predict for late September:

| Date | Peak Load (HE17) | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 14 (Mon) | 86,615 MW | Monday summer-like conditions |
| Sep 16 (Tue, est.) | ~85,935 MW (Enverus) | Tuesday |
| Sep 20 (Sun) | 84,286 MW | Sunday — historically low demand day |

The Sunday Sep 20 peak of 84.3 GW is particularly notable. Historical September Sunday peaks average approximately 72-75 GW. The +9-12 GW excess reflects:

1. **Elevated ambient temperatures**: Dallas remained at 99°F through Sep 17. Texas "fall break" (first strong cold front) typically arrives late September to mid-October.
2. **Data center baseload**: ~7.6 GW of AI/hyperscaler data center load that entered ERCOT service in 2025-2026 is essentially flat 24/7, creating an elevated overnight and weekend floor that didn't exist pre-2025.
3. **ERCOT long-term forecast**: ERCOT has forecast peak demand could exceed **111 GW by 2032**, roughly doubling from 2026 levels, driven primarily by data centers.

**BESS strategy implication**: The structural demand floor (~58-65 GW overnight minimum) and late-season heat extension together mean the "shoulder season" compression of arbitrage spreads that historically reduced BESS revenue in September is less pronounced in 2026 than in prior years.
