- **August 2026** — ERCOT to notify applicants of project classification (consistent with W25 report).

### PGRR144 — Dynamic Model Requirements for Large Loads Moving to July ROS Vote

**Status: Progressing.** PGRR144 proposes clarified dynamic model data requirements, model quality testing expectations, submission milestones, and procedures for large electronic loads (LELs, i.e., data centers). Motivated by observed system events where load tripping behavior created potential reliability concerns. [Live fetch — ZeroEmissionGrid ERCOT LLWG 06-19-26 summary; EPE Consulting PGRR144 article] ERCOT indicated a July ROS vote is the target. The ROS next meets July 9, 2026.

Power variation limits for Large Computational Loads: ERCOT proposed retaining the 10 MW peak-to-peak variation limit measured over a rolling five-second interval for LCL active power variation. [Live fetch — LLWG 06-19-26 summary]

**Reliability significance**: PGRR144, if approved, will require existing and new data centers to provide validated dynamic load models. This improves ERCOT's SCED dispatch accuracy for large electronic loads — which reduces unplanned RT price distortions caused by sudden large-load trips. For BESS operators, fewer surprise RT crashes from load-trip events is a mild positive for energy dispatch predictability.

This is the defining operational event of W26. An extreme heat wave drove ERCOT demand to new June records on multiple consecutive days.

- **Grid operated without emergency conditions.** No ERCOT Conservation Appeal or grid emergency notice was confirmed for the W26 window. ### OBBBA / IRA Construction-Start Deadline — July 4, 2026 (Passed or Imminent)

**Status: Deadline NOW PASSED (July 4, 2026 is 5 days from today's authoring date of June 29).**

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1, signed July 4, 2025) created a hard construction-start deadline of **July 4, 2026** for solar and wind projects seeking IRA technology-neutral tax credits (Sections 45Y and 48E) under the "Fast Track" pathway. Projects beginning construction before this date retain the traditional four-year safe harbor (through 2030). Projects starting after July 4 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 — a much more compressed timeline that many larger projects cannot meet. [Live fetch — Build With Basis OBBBA Final Analysis; Arnold & Porter advisory; Sidley Austin advisory; Hodgson Russ analysis]

**Current status at W26 authoring**: Developers who needed the July 4 deadline to preserve ITC/PTC eligibility have had until this week to begin construction. The W25 report noted 12 projects (>2,600 MW) had already cancelled in June 2026 due to the OBBBA regime, and 16 projects (>3,700 MW) pulled interconnection requests in May 2026.

**What to watch in W27 and beyond**: Interconnection withdrawal data in July and August 2026 will quantify how many projects failed to meet the July 4 deadline. This is the primary indicator of OBBBA damage to the ERCOT forward supply pipeline. Texas-specific context: Texas is one of the few markets where projects starting construction in mid-2026 might still achieve placement-in-service by end of 2027, per expert commentary — so the Texas pipeline damage may be modestly less severe than national estimates. [Live fetch — Native Solar "The July 4, 2026 ITC Deadline: What Texas Commercial Property Owners Need to Know Now"]

**Long-run BESS implication**: Slower renewable additions post-July 4 deadline means slower net load peak growth moderation — marginally bullish for energy arbitrage spreads if BESS capacity growth also slows (which the OBBBA also pressures, since standalone BESS ITC follows the same deadline structure).

## 5. State / Federal Policy

### Governor Abbott Directive — Dual Deadlines Now Active (July 17 and July 31)

**Status: Active; both deadlines within next 4 weeks.** [Live fetch — Governor Abbott directive letter dated June 10, 2026; HillCo Partners summary; Community Impact Austin; KXAN; WFAA]

Abbott's June 10 directive to PUC and ERCOT contains two sequential deadlines:

1. **July 17, 2026** — PUCT and ERCOT must submit a joint memorandum to the Governor's office summarizing: (a) what can be done under current authority to require data centers to fully fund their electric infrastructure, (b) statutory limitations requiring legislative fixes, and (c) recommended actions. [Live fetch — HillCo Partners; Texas Policy Research]

2. **July 31, 2026** — PUCT must initiate formal action to reduce residential ratepayer transmission costs, shifting a larger share to industrial users (data centers) who necessitate the upgrades. [Live fetch — HillCo Partners; KXAN]

The policy direction is clear: transmission and interconnection cost allocation is shifting away from socialized (ratepayer-funded) to beneficiary-pays (developer-funded). This aligns with the Batch Zero framework (developers pay own interconnection costs, no residential ratepayer subsidy). The July 17 memo will be the first public signal of whether PUCT/ERCOT identify additional authority or need the 90th Legislature (January 2027) to act.

**Trading implication**: If PUCT has sufficient authority to implement cost-allocation changes administratively, a July 31 order could alter the project economics for large-load interconnections already in the Batch Zero queue — potentially increasing project costs and causing queue withdrawals. If legislative action is required, the impact is deferred to 2027+.

### Summer 2026 — Heat Wave Confirmed; July Risk Now Primary

The W25 forecast for July as the higher-risk month (grid emergency probability 0.21% vs. 0.09% in June) is being validated: the late June heat wave established demand records in the 80–81 GW range, and the subsequent July record of 82,579 MWh (July 18) represents the new all-time ERCOT demand peak. [Live fetch — EIA heat wave article search results]

Key supply-side observations from W26:
- Non-fossil fuel resources (wind + solar + nuclear) delivered 55% of generation on the hottest days (June 28–29), demonstrating the structural importance of renewable + BESS to summer reliability. This was the first summer with ~16 GW of BESS providing meaningful reserve response.
- No conservation appeal or emergency operations declared during the W26 heat wave — the reserve margin held.
- Thermal maintenance outages were sufficiently cleared ahead of the heat wave for the grid to absorb ~81 GW demand without emergency conditions. The 11 GW of recent capacity additions (primarily BESS + solar) materially supported this outcome.

### Forward Summer Outlook (July 2026)

- Base case peak demand: 92,211 MW (ERCOT Summer 2026 Outlook, May 24). This remains ~11 GW above the June 26–27 record — indicating the highest-risk heat scenario remains ahead.
- The July 18 record of 82,579 MW confirms demand trajectory is tracking between the base case and the current record pace; a sustained triple-digit heat dome in July could push toward 85–90 GW.
- Reserve margin: 18.3% (December 2025 CDR). The heat wave performance without emergency conditions supports the reserve margin assessment's validity.
