## 1. Market Rules / Regulatory

### PUCT + ERCOT July 24 Response to Abbott Directive — Concrete Actions Announced

**Status: CONFIRMED — July 24.** [WebSearch — Governor Abbott press release; KVIA; Texas Scorecard; WBAP; Capture Cascade]

On July 24, 2026, Governor Abbott publicly released the PUCT and ERCOT's response to his June 10 directive, confirming the agencies are taking concrete steps to protect residential ratepayers from data center cost-shifting. Key actions announced:

- **Transmission cost responsibility**: Data centers (large loads exceeding 75 MW) will be required to pay for all transmission infrastructure constructed to serve them, and to contribute toward reducing residential electric bills.
- **Financial security posting**: Large customers must post financial security deposits upon interconnection. Forfeited security from project withdrawals or defaults will be used to directly offset residential transmission rates.
- **Earlier transmission charge commencement**: Large loads must begin paying transmission charges as soon as the capacity to serve them is available — even before the facility has energized. This eliminates the prior "free rider" period during construction.
- **Immediate market response**: A developer of a planned East Texas data center withdrew plans on July 24, citing the new directives as the proximate cause. This represents the first publicly confirmed project cancellation attributable to the W30 policy action. [WebSearch — Tyler Morning Telegraph, Jul 24, 2026]

These actions were released on the same day as the Batch Zero TSP/DSP deadline (July 24), concentrating a significant volume of policy change in a single day. The July 31 formal PUCT order is expected to codify these actions in tariff language. Until that order is issued, the July 24 announcements represent policy commitments rather than binding tariff changes.

**BESS implication**: The financial security posting + early transmission charge requirement significantly increases the effective interconnection cost for new large loads. This is structurally bullish for BESS evening scarcity premiums over the medium term: each large-load project withdrawal reduces the forward load addition pipeline, keeping reserve margins tighter for longer. The East Texas cancellation on July 24 is the leading indicator of a potential acceleration in Batch Zero withdrawals ahead of the August 7 ERCOT classification notifications.
