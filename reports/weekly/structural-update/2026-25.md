## 1. Market Rules / Regulatory

### PUCT June 18, 2026 Open Meeting — Key Outcomes Confirmed

The week's headline regulatory event was the June 18 PUCT Open Meeting, which was the pending watch item from W24. Two confirmed outcomes:

**Batch Zero / PGRR145 — PUCT Conforming Rule Adopted.** The PUCT formally adopted the conforming rulemaking implementing ERCOT's Batch Zero large load interconnection process. [Live fetch — ERCOT news release "PUCT Approves ERCOT's Batch Zero Process for Connecting Large Electricity Users While Protecting System Reliability for Texans" dated 06-18-2026; confirmed across Texas Tribune 06-17-2026, KVUE, Odessa American, RTO Insider, Crypto Briefing] This closes the regulatory loop on the ERCOT Board's June 2 endorsement of PGRR145. Key confirmed provisions:

- Batch grouping threshold: 75 MW and greater.
- Batch Zero applicants grouped into a coordinated study covering total system-wide load picture, transmission upgrade identification, and grid capacity allocation.
- Cost allocation: Developers pay own interconnection and infrastructure costs. Residential ratepayers are explicitly protected from subsidizing large-load transmission buildout per Governor Abbott's June 10 directive.
- Curtailment/dispatch response requirement: Data centers in the Batch Zero pool must accept ERCOT curtailment during local transmission constraint events.
- August 2026: ERCOT to notify applicants of project classification. Approximately 438,000 MW of large-load requests are in the queue; ~89% from data centers.
- Fall 2027: Transmission plan publication for the full batch.

Governor Abbott issued a directive on approximately June 10 requiring PUCT/ERCOT to ensure data centers fully fund their own electric infrastructure needs and reduce residential ratepayer transmission costs by July 31, 2026. The June 18 Batch Zero rule is the primary policy response. [Live fetch — Houston Public Media June 10; KTSM News]

**DRRS / NPRR1309 — Status: UNRESOLVED FOR W25.** No indexed source confirmed a DRRS/NPRR1309-specific PUCT order or direction at or after the June 18 meeting. The W24 hold remains: TAC endorsed NPRR1309 in April 2026; the June 1–2 Board target for Board Priority designation was not independently confirmed in prior searches. ### NPRR1322 — 60-Day DAM AS Offer Disclosure Now Operative

**Status: Implemented.** NPRR1322, which required ERCOT to publish 60-day delayed disclosures of Day-Ahead Market ancillary service only offer awards, had its Board Report dated June 3, 2026, and the data product is confirmed live. [Live fetch — ERCOT data product page "60-Day DAM Disclosure Reports" NP3-966-ER; ERCOT NPRR1322 issue page indexed] The 2-day disclosure product (NP3-911-ER) pre-existed; the new 60-day product adds a look-back window enabling historical AS strategy analysis.

**Trading implication**: For GKS market-analyst and bess-optimizer agents: 60-day delayed AS offer data is now publicly visible. Competitors' offer strategies will become legible with a 60-day lag. This is a structural transparency improvement that will affect AS offer calibration over time; recommend periodic review of 60-day disclosure reports once the dataset accumulates 60 days of history (approximately mid-August 2026 for data from late June).

### RTC+B — Six Months Operational; No Modifications in W25

The Real-Time Co-Optimization Plus Batteries (RTC+B) program launched December 5, 2025, reached its six-month operational mark in W25. [Live fetch — ERCOT news release "ERCOT Goes Live with Real-Time Co-optimization Plus Batteries (RTC+B)" December 5, 2025; ainvest.com June 2026 analysis] No modifications, market notices, or stakeholder-driven NPRR filings targeting RTC+B were identified in W25 searches.

Key operational structure as of June 2026:
- ORDC is replaced by Ancillary Service Demand Curves (ASDCs) in real-time co-optimization.
- BESS modeled as unified assets with state-of-charge (SoC) tracking in 5-minute SCED.
- Generators paid only for energy or AS delivery; standby compensation removed (eliminates ORDC-era "paid to stand by" dynamic).
- Projected wholesale market savings: $2.5–$6.4 billion annually per ERCOT/consultant analysis.
- AS cleared simultaneously with energy in real-time, enabling BESS AS re-optimization at dispatch timescale.

### One Big Beautiful Bill (OBBBA) — IRA Phase-Out Impact on Texas Renewables

President Trump signed H.R. 1 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act) July 4, 2025. Impact on ERCOT generation pipeline:

- Solar and wind projects that began construction within one year of enactment (by approximately July 4, 2026) retain full ITC/PTC eligibility. Projects starting after that cutoff must be in service by end of 2027 to qualify.
- The hard deadline is approaching: developers with projects intending to use the IRA credit structure must break ground by approximately July 4, 2026.
- Cancellation impact already visible: approximately 12 solar and battery projects (>2,600 MW total) cancelled in June 2026; 16 renewable projects (>3,700 MW) pulled interconnection requests in May 2026. [Live fetch — Inside Climate News July 2025 / broader search corpus]
- ERCOT reliability risk: The OBBBA is projected to increase ERCOT peak demand emergency risk by constraining future clean energy supply against continued large load growth. The grid would need approximately 145,000 MW in 2031 vs. ~86,000 MW in 2025. [Live fetch — Inside Climate News analysis]
- Texas household electricity cost impact: +$90/year in 2030, +$370/year in 2035 per economist estimates if full IRA repeal scenario materializes. [Live fetch — Houston Public Media July 2025]

The July 4, 2026 construction-start deadline is the next major inflection point for the ERCOT generation pipeline. Market participants should watch ERCOT interconnection withdrawal data in July and August 2026 for the first quantitative signal of how many projects failed to meet the cutoff.

## 5. Key Risks for Next Week (Week of June 22–28, 2026)

**1. DRRS / NPRR1309 Resolution.** If PUCT issued direction at the June 18 meeting, the week of June 22 may see public docket filings in Project 55750. This is the highest-priority binary watch item. **2. Batch Zero July 10/15 Deadline Window Opening.** The developer submission window under PGRR145 runs from the June 18 rule adoption through July 10 (dynamic stability studies by TSPs) and July 15 (developer attestations, models, and fees). Weeks 25–27 are the critical tracking window. Watch ERCOT market notices for Batch Zero developer response volume — a high participation rate (>50 GW) is bullish for long-run scarcity pricing and AS spread; low participation (<20 GW) suggests speculative queue. [Live fetch — EPE Consulting; JDSupra]

OBBBA July 4 Construction-Start Deadline for IRA Credits.** With approximately 12 days remaining to the deadline, developers attempting to qualify projects for the ITC/PTC will be finalizing construction starts. This creates a short-term surge in activity followed by a potential cliff in new-project filings after July 4. The number of ERCOT interconnection withdrawals in July and August 2026 will quantify the damage to the forward supply pipeline.

**4. Summer Heat Onset.** July 2026 carries higher grid emergency probability (0.21% vs 0.09% in June). With the June record still potentially intact (the W24 heat wave may have fallen short), the first July heat wave is the highest-demand risk window. Any day with load approaching 80–85 GW with materially elevated thermal outages (above 14 GW forced outages) warrants RT spike vigilance in HE17–21.

**5. AS Price Compression from New BESS Supply.** Each GW of new BESS competing for Non-Spin, RRS, and ECRS depresses average hourly AS prices. With approximately 15.5–16 GW of BESS operating and June commissioning continuing, the competitive structure for AS is more crowded than at start of 2026 (14 GW). GKS revenue strategy should weight energy arbitrage more heavily than AS as summer progresses. Monitor midday RT prices for solar-crash events (HE11–14 negative RT) as the primary risk to charge-cycle economics.

**6. West Texas Transmission Constraint — No Relief.** The 765-kV STEP project legal friction (landowner opposition, ~400 contested route miles) means no structural transmission relief for GR_WEST wind congestion before 2028. The West-to-Hub CRR opportunity in GR_WEST remains a persistent structural feature of the congestion landscape. [Carry-forward W24 context]

**7. Governor Abbott July 31 Deadline — Ratepayer Cost Order.** Governor Abbott directed PUCT to initiate action reducing residential ratepayer transmission costs by July 31, 2026. A PUCT order in late July could impose additional cost-allocation or interconnection fee changes that affect large-load project economics and indirectly affect the Batch Zero queue composition.
