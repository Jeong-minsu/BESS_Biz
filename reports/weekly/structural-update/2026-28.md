### Governor Abbott — Data Center Policy U-Turn: Rural Ban and Stronger Restrictions (July 1, 2026)

**Status: NEW DEVELOPMENT — major policy shift.** [WebSearch — US News; Texas Tribune; Real Deal Texas; KXAN; Houston Public Media; Daily Signal; The Texan — multiple sources, July 1–8 coverage]

Governor Greg Abbott executed a significant policy reversal on data centers during a July 1, 2026 campaign stop in Bullard, East Texas. This goes materially further than his June 10 directive and represents a structural shift in the Texas political environment around large load interconnection.

Key elements of Abbott's July 1 position:
- **Outright ban on data center development in rural Texas neighborhoods** — direct prohibition, not just cost-allocation policy. "We must prohibit them from building AI data centers in rural Texas neighborhoods" (Abbott, July 1, 2026).

Political context: The 90th Texas Legislature does not convene until January 2027. Whether the July 17 PUCT/ERCOT joint memo to the Governor identifies sufficient existing administrative authority will determine how quickly any formal action proceeds.

**BESS implication**: Abbott's rural ban call is structurally significant for the BESS development pipeline. However, the ban requires legislative action to implement formally. The net effect through 2026 is increased uncertainty in the forward demand growth trajectory — bearish for the most aggressive 438,000 MW queue scenarios. **Status: NEW DEVELOPMENT — W28.** [WebSearch — Utility Dive; DOE press release; Latitude Media; AEP announcement; RTO Insider; Power Magazine — multiple sources, July 8–9 coverage]

This is distinct from ERCOT's STEP $33 billion plan (which is ERCOT-planned, TDU-executed). The AEP Texas DOE loan finances AEP's portion of the STEP build-out and its own reliability upgrades within its service territory.

**BESS implication**: The AEP Texas loan accelerates the rate of transmission infrastructure deployment in the parts of ERCOT that AEP serves. In the medium term (2028–2030), this reduces congestion risk in AEP territory and improves import capability into load centers — moderately bearish for nodal congestion premiums in AEP zones over the 3–5 year horizon.
