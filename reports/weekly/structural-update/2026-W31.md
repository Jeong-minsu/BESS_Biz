**Status: CONFIRMED — Testimony July 29, 2026.** [WebSearch — KERA News Jul 30; Texas Tribune Jul 29; GilmerMirror Jul 30; KWBU Jul 30; KTSM]

ERCOT CEO Pablo Vegas testified before the Texas Senate Business and Commerce Committee on July 29, 2026. Key points confirmed from multiple indexed sources:

- **Revised demand forecast**: ERCOT now projects statewide electric demand will reach approximately **175,000 MW by 2032**, driven by data centers, population growth, and industrial expansion. This is a significantly revised figure — the April 2026 preliminary forecast of 367,790 MW was deemed likely inflated and was withheld while ERCOT reworked its forecasting methodology for large loads.
- **"It will be harder in 2–3 years"**: Vegas stated that the ease with which ERCOT navigated the July 22 91,308 MW record "would not be the way I would characterize the future." He explicitly warned the grid faces more difficult conditions within two to three years as demand grows faster than dispatchable supply.
- **Gas generation needed**: Vegas and regulators expressed confidence that solar + battery storage will cover daytime demand, but flagged that **additional natural gas generation is required** to serve evening/overnight demand when solar is absent. This is a structural acknowledgment that BESS alone cannot bridge the post-solar ramp without firm backup capacity.
- **Curtailment notification legislation requested**: Vegas proposed legislation authorizing ERCOT to notify large loads (data centers, industrial users) before emergency events to begin voluntary curtailment — essentially a formal demand-response trigger mechanism that does not currently exist under ERCOT's design.
- **Grid performed well last week**: The 91,308 MW record (July 22) was met with 20,000 MW of buffer capacity between demand and committed generation — no conservation appeals issued.

**BESS implication**: The 175,000 MW forecast — even if revised downward from April — represents a roughly 90% load increase from current 91 GW peak within 6 years. The structural need for firm evening capacity favors BESS (4-hour products) and fast-response gas peakers. The curtailment notification legislation, if enacted in the January 2027 session, would also create a new large-load flexibility signal relevant to real-time price behavior during tight conditions.

## 5. Key Macro Trends

### 1. Grid Successfully Navigated Summer 2026 Peak; Structural Risk Ahead

The W30 91,308 MW demand record was met without conservation appeals, with 20,000 MW of operating reserve buffer. ERCOT navigated the summer's hardest week cleanly. However, CEO Vegas's July 29 warning — that the grid will face significantly more difficult conditions in 2–3 years as the 175,000 MW demand trajectory builds — is the macro structural signal for the balance of the planning horizon.

The two vectors of structural concern: (a) daytime demand growth outpacing solar supply headroom, requiring additional gas peaking capacity for evening/overnight reliability; (b) Batch Zero large-load pipeline integrity — if 438 GW in queue materializes even at a 20–30% execution rate, reserve margins tighten dramatically without parallel supply additions.

### 2. BESS Fleet = Primary Scarcity-Event Market Clearing Mechanism

The 11,980 MW BESS fleet discharge record during W30 (approximately 13% of peak demand) establishes battery storage as a structurally indispensable market participant — not a marginal resource. As the fleet grows toward 18–20 GW (end-2026), the marginal clearing price during HE20–22 will increasingly reflect BESS dispatch economics rather than gas peaker marginal cost. This is structurally downward pressure on DA peak prices over the 12–18 month horizon, partially offset by the load growth trajectory.

### 3. Solar Exceeding Coal — Structural Portfolio Shift in Progress

EIA's confirmed 2026 forecast (solar exceeding coal in ERCOT for the first time) is not a headline anomaly — it reflects the cumulative effect of 39,794 MW of installed solar capacity (vs. ~14 GW coal) and the pace of additions (solar capacity doubled 2023–2025). The structural implication is a deepening midday net-load trough (duck curve) and steeper evening ramp requiring BESS and gas peakers to bridge. The WEST_TO_NORTH congestion constraint during overnight wind buildup (GR_WEST 14–22 GW) is the complementary structural constraint as solar/storage/wind portfolios balance.

### 4. Data Center Policy Crystallizing; December 2026 Tariff Change Is the Hard Deadline

The policy trajectory from W29 (Governor directive) → W30 (July 24 commitments, East Texas withdrawal) → W31 (July 31 initiation, Senate testimony) is a coherent sequence. The December 2026 Project 58481 final rule is when financial security posting, early transmission charge commencement, and cost-shift mechanisms become binding tariff language. Batch Zero August 7 classification notifications are the immediate next data point on whether pipeline withdrawals accelerate.

### 5. Forward Load Growth Forecast Revised Downward (367 GW → 175 GW) — Still Significant

ERCOT's April 2026 preliminary forecast of 367,790 MW by 2032 was publicly disavowed as likely inflated. The revised 175,000 MW figure presented by Vegas on July 29 is approximately 90% above the current 91,308 MW record peak. Even the conservative estimate implies a doubling of the grid in six years. The accuracy of this forecast determines whether new dispatchable supply investments (thermal, BESS, demand response) are built in time. The forecast rework is ongoing — investors and developers should treat the 175 GW figure as a planning range midpoint, not a committed outlook.
