## 1. Market Rules / Regulatory

### PGRR145 + NPRR1325 — Effective July 11, 2026; Batch Zero Process Formally Operative

**Status: Critical milestone this week.** [Live fetch — ERCOT.com market notice M-B062326-01; EPE Consulting; Willkie; Foley & Lardner articles indexed]

The Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) approved the conforming rule implementing the Batch Zero interconnection process (PGRR145) with an effective date of **July 11, 2026**. The companion market rule revision NPRR1325 carries the same effective date. The legacy Large Load Interconnection Study process under Planning Guide Section 9 remained operative **through end of day July 10, 2026**; the transition to the new batch study framework activates this coming week.

Key milestones now active or imminent:
- **July 10 (4 days from authoring)**: Hard deadline — ILLEs must submit all materials (Load Information Form, attestations, dynamic models, commissioning plan, fees). Projects missing this deadline are dropped from Batch Zero. July 10 is also the last day ERCOT can approve studies under the legacy PGRR115 individual study process.
- **July 11**: Batch Zero process formally operative; no new individual study submissions accepted under legacy rules.
- **July 24**: TSP/DSP deadline to finalize and submit the complete Batch Zero eligibility package to ERCOT. The July 10–24 window is strictly for administrative corrections only.
- **August 7**: ERCOT notifies each DSP/TSP of project classifications — first tangible output of Batch Zero.
- **January 29, 2027**: ERCOT delivers Batch Zero Interconnection Study results: capacity allocations per year 2028–2032, proposed transmission improvements and costs.

**The July 10 submission volume is the single most consequential near-term data point for the long-run ERCOT load trajectory.** The ~438,000 MW candidate queue will not convert 1:1; ERCOT's observed 49.8% utilization rate of requested MW at energization (from its 2025 study) implies net realistic forward additions are dramatically below the headline queue figure. The actual submission tally (expected in ERCOT market notices following August 7) will set a new credible baseline for 2028–2032 load growth modeling and scarcity pricing outlook. High participation (>50 GW submitted) is bullish for long-run AS values and energy arbitrage spreads; low participation (<20 GW) signals a speculative queue.

## 5. State / Federal Policy

### OBBBA July 4 IRA Construction-Start Deadline — NOW PASSED

**Status: Milestone passed as of W27.** [Live fetch — Arnold & Porter; IRS Notice N-25-42; SolarEdge; The Tax Adviser; SolarStock USA Q2 2026 market brief indexed]

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, signed July 4, 2025) created a hard construction-start deadline for clean energy projects seeking ITC/PTC eligibility under the "fast-track" pathway. That deadline — July 4, 2026 — has now passed as of W27.

Key confirmed details now that the deadline is in the rear-view mirror:

- **Exclusive method**: The Physical Work Test was the exclusive method for establishing that construction had begun on a wind or solar facility before July 5, 2026. The Five-Percent Safe Harbor was explicitly not available for these purposes (per IRS Notice N-25-42). This materially raised the bar for construction start qualification vs. prior IRA safe harbor rules.
- **Post-deadline pathway**: Projects that missed the deadline but are placed in service by December 31, 2027 may still qualify. However, this compressed timeline is generally unworkable for large utility-scale projects (typically 2–4 year development cycles).
- **Texas-specific context**: Texas is among the few markets where "fast-to-build" solar and BESS infrastructure could potentially allow placement in service by end of 2027 even with a mid-2026 construction start, per expert commentary. Texas supply pipeline damage may be modestly less severe than national estimates.
- **Estimated capacity impact**: SolarStock USA's Q2 2026 market brief estimated 6–8 GW of commercial mid-market capacity was attempting to safe-harbor before the deadline, with a projected 50% completion rate given labor and EPC bottlenecks — implying **3–4 GW of planned commercial mid-market capacity likely failed to qualify**. Utility-scale estimates vary more widely.
- **Confirming data still pending**: ERCOT interconnection queue withdrawal data in July–August 2026 will quantify how many GW of utility-scale projects failed the deadline. This is the primary quantitative signal to monitor going forward.

**BESS ITC impact**: Standalone BESS projects face the same OBBBA construction-start deadline for Section 48E ITC eligibility. BESS projects that did not begin physical work before July 4, 2026 lose access to the 30% ITC and face the compressed 2027 placed-in-service pathway. This materially affects the forward BESS development pipeline and could slow ERCOT BESS additions beyond 2027.

**Long-run trading implication**: Slower renewable and BESS additions post-deadline means the structural duck curve and evening ramp arbitrage opportunities persist longer than under the pre-OBBBA IRA continuation scenario. Marginally bullish for energy arbitrage spreads on a 3–5 year horizon, assuming BESS supply growth also slows as the OBBBA pressures the standalone storage development pipeline.

1. Interconnection standards
2. Net-metering arrangements for co-location
3. Large load forecasting
4. Demand reduction / emergency curtailment
